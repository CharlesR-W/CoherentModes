I gave a small language model, {{fact:model}}, the same {{fact:n_items}} text snippets ({{fact:n_per_source}} each of maths, code, Wikipedia, forum posts and poetry), recorded its residual stream at six layers, after six numbers of tokens read, at {{fact:n_steps}} checkpoints of its training, and asked which snippets it treats alike, with the label-free construction I used on a ResNet-20. This is a toy (one model, one run, one probe) meant to show how far that construction works on a language model without changes.

The idea in one example. Fix layer 8 and the final checkpoint. After 16 tokens, centre the residual vectors over the snippets and compare every pair by cosine, link each snippet to its 15 nearest, and turn the links into a random walk between snippets. Do the same after 4, 8, 32, 64 and 128 tokens: six walks over the same snippets, in six different geometries. Their average is the walk of a reading block (a run of cells along the reading axis; not a transformer block). Links that persist from one position to the next dominate it; links that come and go are diluted. The averaged walk's slowest-changing functions, made sparse, give a few coherent sets. What stays fixed: the snippets and the layer. What changes: the position, and with it the geometry. Sources are never used, only checked afterwards.

The answer, in short

Out of the box, it partly works. It runs on this model unchanged, and every control passes (position $t$ means $t$ tokens read; the bag reference depends only on token counts; the zoom recovers a planted hierarchy). Averaging along reading reproduces the sources; single cells only lean towards them. At the final step, a reading block's four sets match maths, code, Wikipedia and poetry (forum posts are split between two sets and the unassigned): at L4 to L12 the overlap between each set and its best-matching source (matched Jaccard {{fact:rd.sj.L4_12}}; chance gives {{fact:rd.null}}) is {{fact:rd.ratio.L4_12}} of the best their sizes allow. Single cells keep an above-chance association with source, but reach only {{fact:cell.ratio.point.range}} of theirs. The reading blocks' advantage is already present at training step 512; the ranges here are for the final step.

The zoom finds no clearly separated split in the four trees I ran: every cut follows sources, but its clustering ratio, {{fact:ups.real.range}}, is near what structureless synthetic noise gives ({{fact:ups.structureless.range}}) and far below planted groups (at least {{fact:ups.planted.min}}). That is a comparison with synthetic reference points, not a proof that the model has no clusters. The sources are an easy target: the mean of the token embeddings keeps more of its walk within sources than any layer, and the L8 reading block's sets match its sets at {{fact:t1_vs_bag.t128}}, so finding sources shows nothing beyond token statistics. One coordinate carries {{fact:rogue1.final.L4_10.t4_32}} of the centred variance at L4 to L10 for the first 32 tokens; how much that bends the cosine geometry was not measured (finding 9).

Five of the six result targets are met; H1 (source separation grows by 0.2 or more from $t = 4$ to 128) is not. Findings separates what is observed from how I read it.

The question

On the ResNet, the construction found groups of images that the network keeps together across a run of layers or of training. A language model adds a third axis: position, the number of tokens it has read. A snippet's residual stream at position $t$ depends on tokens 1 to $t$ only, so reading is a time axis inside one forward pass, next to depth and training. I wanted to know four things.

  1. Where does the walk separate kinds of text? Along reading, depth and training: does maths part from poetry, and from how many tokens on?
  2. Token or source? How strongly is the walk tied to the token just read, and how strongly to where the snippet came from?
  3. Is position $t$ enough? Do the sets from the residual at $t$ agree with those from a view of the layer's states at all positions up to $t$ (the slab)? The raw states at positions 1 to $t$ determine every later layer there, including its KV-cache entries; the slab view is a normalised geometry of them.
  4. What does zooming show? Cut a block's items in two with the block's own walk, redo the analysis on each half, and repeat.

What is measured

For each cell $c$ (a checkpoint, a layer and a position) the construction builds a random walk $P_c$ between the snippets, from their cosine similarities (the procedure is below). A block $B$ is a run of cells along one axis, and everything on the page comes from the block's average walk.

$$ f^\top (I - \bar P_B)\, f \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} f^\top (I - P_c)\, f \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} \tfrac12 \sum_{i,j} (P_c)_{ij}\,(f_i - f_j)^2 $$
$$ S_c(\mathcal G) = \frac1N \sum_{g \in \mathcal G}\ \sum_{i \ne j \in g} (P_c)_{ij}, \qquad \tilde S = \frac{S - S^0}{1 - S^0} $$

where $f$ is any function on the $N$ snippets and $\bar P_B$ the mean of the cells' walks; a function that changes little across every walk step in every cell of the block has a small penalty (1), and the block's eigenfunctions $\psi_k$ are the functions with the smallest. $S_c(\mathcal G)$ is the share of the walk that stays inside the groups of a grouping $\mathcal G$, $S^0$ its chance level, and $\tilde S$ the excess over chance (0 for a walk that ignores the grouping, 1 for one that never leaves it). $\boldsymbol{T}$ groups snippets by the token at position $t$ (label-free: tokens are inputs); $\boldsymbol{W}$ groups them by source (validation).

$T$ and $W$ are associations between the walk and a grouping. They do not split the walk into a "token part" and a "context part", and nothing on this page reads them that way.

Labels

The method uses no labels. A snippet's source is used only to assemble a balanced probe, to colour plots, for the validation statistics ($W$, the pair mixing ratio $X$, matched Jaccard against sources), and to choose which snippets the "follow" figure shows. Source never enters a kernel, an operator, a basis, a cut or the choice of a block.

Cells and blocks

There are {{fact:n_cells}} cells: {{fact:n_steps}} checkpoints (steps {{fact:steps}}) by six layers ({{fact:layers}}) by six positions (t = {{fact:positions}}). A block runs along exactly one axis: a reading block is one layer at positions 4 to 128, a depth block is one position at layers L2 to L12, and a training block is one layer at t = 128 across checkpoints. A single cell is also a block. Positions and checkpoints are roughly log-spaced, so with equal weights per cell, early reading and early training count for more per token or step than late ones.

The procedure in five steps

Five fixed computations, from activations to sets, unchanged from the ResNet study. The pictures use the cell (L8, t = 128) and the reading block of L8 at step {{fact:final_step}}; in every matrix the snippets are in the order of that block's first zoom cut. Exact statements are in Method.

1. Compare snippets within one cell

Take every snippet's residual stream at one layer and position, centre it over the {{fact:n_items}} snippets, scale each vector to unit length, and take the cosine between every pair. That cosine is the only geometry the construction assumes.

2. Turn similarity into a walk

Link two snippets if either is among the other's {{fact:k}} nearest neighbours (weight 1, a floor of $10^{-6}$ elsewhere). Symmetric Sinkhorn scaling turns these links into a walk $P_c$ whose rows and columns sum to 1, so every snippet carries the same weight. The lazy walk $M_c = \tfrac12(I + P_c)$ stays put half the time.

3. Average over a block

A block's operator $\bar M$ is the plain average of its cells' lazy walks. No map between cells is fitted: each cell keeps its own geometry, and the cells meet only through the snippets they share.

4. Take its eigenfunctions, with their retention in every cell

The leading eigenvectors $\psi_1, \psi_2, \ldots$ of $\bar M$ are the functions on the snippets with the smallest average penalty (1). An average can hide a cell where the function varies a lot, so next to each eigenfunction I show its retention $\rho_c(\psi_k) = \psi_k^\top M_c \psi_k$ in each cell: 1 means the function is constant on every walk neighbourhood of that cell, and a random function with mean zero gets about 0.5.

5. Sparse vectors, coherent sets

SEBA rotates the first $r$ eigenvectors (with the constant $\psi_0$) into $r$ sparse, non-negative vectors. Each snippet goes to the vector on which it is largest, or is unassigned if all are zero. The page shows $r = 2$ and $r = 4$. These coherent sets are the groups on which the averaged walk's slowest functions concentrate.
