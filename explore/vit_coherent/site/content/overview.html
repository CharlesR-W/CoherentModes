The ResNet-20 study's label-free procedure for finding which images a network treats alike, run unchanged on a second, quite different network: DINOv2 ViT-S/14 (self-supervised, released weights), read at the CLS token after each of its 12 transformer blocks, on {{fact:n_images}} ImageNet-100 images.

The procedure, as in the ResNet study: at each site, compare the probe images by cosine similarity of their activations and turn that into a symmetric random walk ($P_c$, Sinkhorn-scaled, made lazy: $M_c = \tfrac12(I + P_c)$). For a block of consecutive sites, average the walks ($\bar M$), take the slowest eigenfunctions of the average, and turn their span into $r$ sparse, near-disjoint sets with SEBA. The sets are candidates for groups of images the network treats alike across the block. Nothing is trained, and labels enter only as colours and as validation.

There are no training checkpoints, so where the ResNet explorer has a grid of checkpoints by sites, this one has a single row of twelve sites in reading order, wrapping to fit the screen (two lines of six on a desktop). Everything else (the side panel, the colourings, the kernel rules, $r = 2..8$) follows the same conventions, so the two pages can be read side by side.

On this page

  • Explorer. Pick a block of sites; its coherent sets (or one eigenfunction) colour the same 2,000 images at every site. The side panel shows the block's spectrum, representative images of each set, retention at every site, and head loadings.
  • Measurements. Charts of the same statistics as in the ResNet study, where they apply with one checkpoint: agreement of the sets between sites, their class make-up, the $r = 2$ split against an animal / non-animal partition, image statistics, and decodability by layer.
  • Method. Definitions, the five kernel rules with their values at every site, and how this page relates to the earlier ViT run on this model.
