I gave a small selective state-space model, {{fact:model}}, the same {{fact:n_items}} text snippets as the Pythia study ({{fact:n_per_source}} each of maths, code, Wikipedia, forum posts and poetry), recorded its residual stream and its recurrent state at six layers, after six numbers of tokens read, at two checkpoints (the HF initialisation and the trained model), and asked which snippets it treats alike, with the label-free construction used on the ResNet and on Pythia. The reason to look at Mamba: after reading $t$ tokens it holds a fixed-size state, and that state is everything it carries forward, so the whole of it can be measured directly, one vector per snippet per position.

The idea in one example. Fix layer 16 of the trained model. After 16 tokens, centre the residual vectors over the snippets and compare every pair by cosine, link each snippet to its {{fact:k}} nearest, and turn the links into a random walk between snippets. Do the same after 4, 8, 32, 64 and 128 tokens: six walks over the same snippets, in six different geometries. Their average is the walk of a reading block (a run of cells along the reading axis; not a Mamba block). Its slowest-changing functions, made sparse, give a few coherent sets. Then ask the same of layer 16's own recurrent state, and of the states of all layers up to 16; at layer 24 the latter is the model's whole state. Sources are never used, only checked afterwards.

The question

In a causal transformer, what is carried forward from a prefix is the KV cache, which grows with $t$; the Pythia study could reach it only through a proxy, the prefix slab. A selective state-space model carries a fixed-size recurrent state instead. After reading token $t$, layer $l$ holds a convolution buffer and an SSM state, and the collection of these over all {{fact:n_layers}} layers is everything the model keeps about the prefix. I asked five things.

  1. Where does the walk separate kinds of text? Along reading and depth, at initialisation and trained: does maths part from poetry, and from how many tokens on?
  2. Token or source? How strongly is the walk tied to the token just read, and how strongly to the snippet's source?
  3. Residual, layer state or whole state? Do the sets from the residual at position $t$ agree with those from layer $l$'s own recurrent state, and with those from the states of all layers up to $l$ (at $l$ = 24, the whole state)? Where along reading and depth do they part?
  4. What does zooming show? Cut a block's snippets in two with the block's own walk, redo the analysis on each half, and repeat; once on the whole state.
  5. Mamba against Pythia. On the same {{fact:n_items}} snippets (both models read the Pile with the same tokenizer), how far do the two models' coherent sets agree at matched relative depth?

What is measured

For each cell $c$ (a checkpoint, a layer, a position and a view) the construction builds a random walk $P_c$ between the snippets from their cosine similarities (the procedure is below). A block $B$ is a run of cells along one axis, and everything on the page comes from the block's average walk.

$$ f^\top (I - \bar P_B)\, f \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} f^\top (I - P_c)\, f \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} \tfrac12 \sum_{i,j} (P_c)_{ij}\,(f_i - f_j)^2 $$
$$ S_c(\mathcal G) = \frac1N \sum_{g \in \mathcal G}\ \sum_{i \ne j \in g} (P_c)_{ij}, \qquad \tilde S = \frac{S - S^0}{1 - S^0} $$

where $f$ is any function on the $N$ snippets and $\bar P_B$ the mean of the cells' walks; a function that changes little across every walk step in every cell of the block has a small penalty (1), and the block's eigenfunctions $\psi_k$ are the functions with the smallest. $S_c(\mathcal G)$ is the share of the walk that stays inside the groups of a grouping $\mathcal G$, $S^0$ its chance level, and $\tilde S$ the excess over chance (0 for a walk that ignores the grouping, 1 for one that never leaves it). $\boldsymbol{T}$ groups snippets by the token at position $t$ (label-free: tokens are inputs); $\boldsymbol{W}$ groups them by source (validation).

$T$ and $W$ are associations between the walk and a grouping. They do not split the walk into a "token part" and a "context part", and nothing on this page reads them that way.

The recurrent state, and the three views

After the step that reads token $t$, layer $m$ holds a conv buffer $c^m_t$ (the last three inputs to its depthwise convolution, {{fact:state.conv_numbers}} numbers) and an SSM state $z^m_t$ ({{fact:state.ssm_numbers}} numbers). The tuple $\Sigma_t = (c^m_t, z^m_t)_{m=1}^{ {{fact:n_layers}} }$ ({{fact:state.whole_numbers}} numbers), with the tokens after $t$, determines every later residual, state and next-token distribution [thm]: that is the recurrence itself, and control C1b checks it on the measured state. The residual $h^l_t$ is not a state: what layer $l+1$ does next depends on its own state, which is not a function of $h^l_t$ alone.

Labels

The method uses no labels. A snippet's source is used only to assemble a balanced probe, to colour plots, for the validation statistics ($W$, the pair mixing ratio $X$, matched Jaccard against sources), and to choose which snippets the "follow" figure shows. Source never enters a kernel, an operator, a basis, a cut or the choice of a block.

Cells and blocks

There are {{fact:n_cells_view}} cells per view: two checkpoints (the HF initialisation and the trained model) by six layers ({{fact:layers}}) by six positions (t = {{fact:positions}}); with three views and the bag, {{fact:n_cells_all}} in all. A block runs along exactly one axis: a reading block is one layer at positions 4 to 128 (trained, in any view), and a depth block is one position at layers L4 to L24 (trained, point view). A single cell is also a block. No intermediate training checkpoints of mamba-130m are published, so there is no training axis; the initialisation is a comparison, like Pythia's step 0. Positions are roughly log-spaced, so with equal weights per cell, early reading counts for more per token than late reading.

The procedure in five steps

Five fixed computations, from activations to sets, unchanged from the ResNet and Pythia studies. The pictures use the cell (L16, t = 128) and the point reading block of L16 at the trained checkpoint; in every matrix the snippets are in the order of that block's first zoom cut. Exact statements are in Method.

1. Compare snippets within one cell

Take every snippet's vector in one cell, centre it over the {{fact:n_items}} snippets, scale each vector to unit length, and take the cosine between every pair. That cosine is the only geometry the construction assumes. For the state and stack views the same is done for each constituent (each conv buffer and each SSM state) separately, and the cosine matrices are averaged, so that no constituent's scale decides the weighting.

2. Turn similarity into a walk

Link two snippets if either is among the other's {{fact:k}} nearest neighbours (weight 1, a floor of $10^{-6}$ elsewhere). Symmetric Sinkhorn scaling turns these links into a walk $P_c$ whose rows and columns sum to 1, so every snippet carries the same weight. The lazy walk $M_c = \tfrac12(I + P_c)$ stays put half the time.

3. Average over a block

A block's operator $\bar M$ is the plain average of its cells' lazy walks. No map between cells is fitted: each cell keeps its own geometry, and the cells meet only through the snippets they share.

4. Take its eigenfunctions, with their retention in every cell

The leading eigenvectors $\psi_1, \psi_2, \ldots$ of $\bar M$ are the functions on the snippets with the smallest average penalty (1). An average can hide a cell where the function varies a lot, so next to each eigenfunction I show its retention $\rho_c(\psi_k) = \psi_k^\top M_c \psi_k$ in each cell: 1 means the function is constant on every walk neighbourhood of that cell, and a random function with mean zero gets about 0.5.

5. Sparse vectors, coherent sets

SEBA rotates the first $r$ eigenvectors (with the constant $\psi_0$) into $r$ sparse, non-negative vectors. Each snippet goes to the vector on which it is largest, or is unassigned if all are zero. The page shows $r = 2$ and $r = 4$. These coherent sets are the groups on which the averaged walk's slowest functions concentrate.
