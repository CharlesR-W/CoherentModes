Q0. Do the controls hold?

Three identities and three planted checks come first. C1a checks that recurrent decoding, which is how the state is read, gives the same residuals as the model's parallel forward pass. C1b checks that the measured state is the state: a fresh decode started at $t = 64$ from nothing but the stored conv buffers and SSM states continues exactly as the original. C2 checks that the bag view depends only on token counts. P1 runs the zoom on a synthetic hierarchy with a known answer and fixes the reading threshold for $\Upsilon$ before any real tree is looked at.

Q1. Is the walk tied to the token just read, or to the source?

In plain words: $\tilde T$ is how much more often than chance a walk step joins two snippets that read the same token at position $t$; $\tilde W$ is the same for two snippets from the same source. Both are 0 for a walk that ignores the grouping and 1 for one that never leaves it. The two checkpoints sit side by side on one colour scale.

Q2. When does the walk stop linking maths and poetry?

The pair mixing ratio $X(A, B)$ is the walk mass between sources $A$ and $B$, scaled so that a walk ignoring sources gives 1. Below 1, the walk avoids crossing between them. Wikipedia against forum posts is the prose-to-prose contrast.

To see what this means for single snippets, follow two of them. The ten follow items are the first two of each source in probe order, which is random, so the choice does not depend on any result.

Q3. Residual, layer state or whole state?

Three views of the same (layer, position) are compared. The point view is the residual after block $l$ at $t$. The state view is layer $l$'s own recurrent state after $t$ tokens. The stack view is the states of layers 1 to $l$; at L24 it is the whole state, everything the model carries forward. Agreement between the views' eigenfunctions asks whether they organise the snippets the same way; it is not a measure of information. The state and stack views weight each constituent equally after its own normalisation (decision D-M1).

Q5. Does the order of the earlier tokens matter?

For each snippet, the reordered probe S shuffles tokens 1 to 127 and keeps token 128, and is read at $t = 128$ only. The two probes then have the same snippets, the same token counts and the same last token, so comparing them changes only the order of the earlier tokens. The bag view cannot tell them apart (C2). This is an association, not a causal claim about what the model uses.
