When in training does each layer's grouping take its final form?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0 (seed 1 only for the operator floor), the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, {{fact:x.when.n_steps}} checkpoints (steps {{fact:x.when.steps}}), kernel rule {{fact:x.when.rule}}, the single-snapshot operator of each of the {{fact:x.when.n_sites}} sites.

Why it is interesting

Each site of the trained network sorts the probe images into groups it treats alike. An untrained network does this too: its random filters and shortcut connections already respond to differences in colour and brightness between images, so its sites have groupings before any learning. Training has to turn the one into the other, and the order in which that happens says how the network is built. If a site reaches its final grouping before the network is accurate, the grouping is plausibly an early scaffold that later training works within (an interpretation, not something this measurement shows); if it changes until the end, the grouping is still being tuned when accuracy is nearly saturated. If deep sites settle first, the network organises its output end before its input end; if shallow sites settle first, the reverse.

"Final form" can mean three different things here, and they need not agree: the same groups of images, groups that line up with a reference partition of the classes, or the same operator. I measure each separately and give three answers.

How I measure it

(a) Agreement with the final sets (label-free; the headline). For each site and checkpoint I take the $r = 4$ SEBA sets of that snapshot's operator and score them against the same site's sets at the final checkpoint (step {{fact:final_step}}) with the matched Jaccard: pair each set with one final set so that the mean Jaccard overlap is largest, and report that mean (Appendix C). It is $1$ when the two groupings are the same and near the permutation null when they share nothing beyond set sizes. The milestone is the first checkpoint at which the agreement exceeds $0.5$ and the null's $97.5$th percentile, and "settled" is the first checkpoint from which this holds at every later one. The question is answered by the grouping alone: no label enters. $r = 4$ is the site's default resolution, fixed before these results; other $r$ are under Caveats. The final checkpoint is compared with itself and scores $1$ by construction.

(b) Alignment with a class reference (validation). The $r = 2$ sets of each snapshot against the bipartition {airplane, automobile, ship, truck} / the other six classes. This reference was chosen after seeing the final two-set split of the late sites, so it is post hoc: it can show when the sets come to line up with it, not that this is the split the network "should" make. The score is the matched Jaccard; the permutation null keeps the set sizes; the size ceiling is the largest score any two sets of these sizes could reach. Because the permutation test is passed already at step $0$ at every site, it cannot date anything, so the milestone is an effect size: the first checkpoint from which the normalised excess (score − null mean) / (ceiling − null mean) stays at or above $0.5$. That is half of the excess over chance that the set sizes allow, not half of the site's final alignment.

(c) Distance to the final operator (label-free). For the site's operator at a checkpoint and at the end, the projector distance $d_k = 1 - \lVert U_k(A)^\top U_k(B) \rVert_F^2 / k$ between the spans of their leading $k$ non-constant eigenvectors ($0$ = same span; chance {{fact:x.when.c.chance}} at $k = 4$). It is read against the seed floor: $d_k$ between seed 0 and seed 1 at the same site at the final checkpoint: the observed distance between two training runs that differ only in their random seed. It is one pair of seeds, a reference for scale, not a measured spread of run-to-run variation. A site has settled from the first checkpoint after which its distance to its own final operator stays at or below its floor. A leading-$k$ span is only well defined when the eigenvalue cut $k \mid k+1$ is reproducible on resampled probes; I use $k = 4$, whose cut is resolved in {{fact:x.when.c.resolved_share_k4}} of the seed $0$ snapshots (against {{fact:x.when.c.resolved_share_k9}} at $k = 9$), and drop distances at unresolved cuts.

Controls and nulls

  • Permutation nulls for (a) and (b): the earlier grouping's labels shuffled over the images, sizes and the number of unassigned images kept. They say what agreement set sizes alone produce. In (a) the null's $97.5$th percentile is at most {{fact:x.when.a.null_hi_max}} before the final checkpoint, so the $0.5$ threshold is the binding condition.
  • Step $0$, the untrained network, is a control in all three: any agreement there comes from the architecture, the random initialization and the images, not from learning.
  • Pixel statistics for (b): the strongest alternative to "the sets line up with the classes" is that they line up with colour or brightness, which differ between these classes in the probe. For every snapshot and each of twelve pixel statistics, in both orientations, I put the images at the top of the ranking into one set and those at the bottom into the other, with the same set sizes as the SEBA sets, and leave the middle unassigned (the same unassigned count). Each split is scored against the reference and the best is kept. This is the best of the tested two-tail splits, not of all splits by one statistic, and it is chosen with the labels, so it is a reference rather than a null.
  • Seed floor for (c), defined above.

Answer

(a) Each site's $r = 4$ sets first agree with its final sets at {{fact:x.when.a.first}}, and stay above the threshold from then on except {{fact:x.when.a.settled_diff}}; there is no depth order. (b) The $r = 2$ sets reach half of the possible excess over chance against the class reference from {{fact:x.when.b.half}}: deep sites first. (c) The leading four-mode spans come within the observed between-seed distance (and stay there) from {{fact:x.when.c.settled_before}}; at {{fact:x.when.c.only_final}} the distance falls below it only at the final checkpoint, and the floor cut is unresolved at {{fact:x.when.c.unresolved_floor}}.

The first milestone falls at the checkpoints where validation accuracy is {{fact:x.when.acc.step1024}} and {{fact:x.when.acc.step2560}}, well before it saturates. At step $25{,}600$, the last measured checkpoint before the end, agreement with the final sets was {{fact:x.when.a.step25600_min}} to {{fact:x.when.a.step25600_max}}. Some sites dipped along the way, and sites that fell back below the threshold after first passing it are: {{fact:x.when.a.dips}}. The final value of $1$ is a self-comparison, and nothing was measured between steps $25{,}600$ and {{fact:final_step}} (the interval that contains the learning-rate drops at steps {{fact:x.when.c.lr_drops}}), so I did not measure when the remaining differences disappeared within it.

Further evidence

(b) Alignment with the class reference. At step $0$ every site already beats the maximum of its permutation null ({{fact:x.when.b.step0_beat_max}} of {{fact:x.when.n_sites}} sites), but with a normalised excess of only {{fact:x.when.b.step0_nexc_min}} to {{fact:x.when.b.step0_nexc_max}}. At the final checkpoint it ranges from {{fact:x.when.b.never_final_nexc}} at {{fact:x.when.b.never}}, which never reach the milestone, up to {{fact:x.when.b.final_nexc_max}}.

Is it a simple pixel statistic? This panel tests the strongest single-statistic alternative: the best of the tested two-tail splits by one pixel statistic, with the same set sizes and unassigned count as the sets (other pixel information is not tested). At step $0$ that split matches the reference at least as well as the sets do at {{fact:x.when.b.step0_pixel_wins}} of {{fact:x.when.n_sites}} sites (at b9, {{fact:x.when.b.b9.step0.pixel}} against {{fact:x.when.b.b9.step0.score}}). The sets score above it at every later checkpoint from {{fact:x.when.b.above_pixel}}; at b9 they end at {{fact:x.when.b.b9.final.score}} against {{fact:x.when.b.b9.final.pixel}}.

Where the reference ranks: among all $511$ ways of splitting the ten classes in two, ordered by their match with the $r = 2$ sets, it is first at {{fact:x.when.b.rank_final_n1}} of {{fact:x.when.n_sites}} sites at the final checkpoint and between {{fact:x.when.b.rank_step0_min}}th and {{fact:x.when.b.rank_step0_max}}th at step $0$. This describes the post-hoc reference; it is not a test that it was the split to look for, and it is not corrected for having been picked after seeing the sets (chart in Appendix D).

(c) Distance to the final operator. At step $0$ every operator is far from its final form ({{fact:x.when.c.step0_min}} to {{fact:x.when.c.step0_max}}). At step $25{,}600$ the sites that settle only at the end are at {{fact:x.when.c.late_step25600_min}} to {{fact:x.when.c.late_step25600_max}}, against seed floors of {{fact:x.when.c.late_floor_min}} to {{fact:x.when.c.late_floor_max}}: each is still farther from its final operator than the two seeds are from each other.

Caveats

  • Only {{fact:x.when.n_steps}} checkpoints were measured, a factor of two and a half to four apart, so every milestone is "between the previous checkpoint and this one". The learning-rate drops (steps {{fact:x.when.c.lr_drops}}) both fall between the last two checkpoints.
  • The thresholds ($0.5$ in (a) and (b), the seed floor in (c)) are conventions; the charts show the whole trajectories.
  • Milestone (a) depends on the resolution: the first-milestone step ranges over sites from {{fact:x.when.a.r2.first_range}} at $r = 2$, {{fact:x.when.a.r3.first_range}} at $r = 3$, {{fact:x.when.a.r6.first_range}} at $r = 6$ and {{fact:x.when.a.r8.first_range}} at $r = 8$.
  • (c) at $k = 3$, the span the $r = 4$ sets are cut from (cut resolved in {{fact:x.when.c.resolved_share_k3}} of the snapshots): {{fact:x.when.c.k3.settled}}.
  • The seed floor uses one other seed at one checkpoint; a site whose distance stays just above it may be within the spread of more seeds.
  • The class reference in (b) is post hoc and is used only to validate; the two-tail pixel-statistic split is chosen with the labels and is a reference, not a null; splits other than the tested two-tail ones were not searched.
