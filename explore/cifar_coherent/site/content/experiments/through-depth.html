What does one operator over images and sites find when its functions may change from site to site?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, all {{fact:n_sites}} sites from stem to logits as one chain, all {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints (figures: the final checkpoint, step {{fact:final_step}}). Kernel rule {{fact:x.through-depth.rule}} ("the kNN rule") throughout; kernel rule {{fact:x.through-depth.rule_alt}} ("the perplexity rule") as a robustness check. Six couplings per checkpoint, two unit conventions, and the matrix of the source paper.

Why it is interesting

The question. Can one spectral construction describe all twelve sites at once, with a grouping of the probe images that is allowed to change from site to site but is not redrawn from scratch at every site? The other experiments read the network one block of sites at a time. A block average gives one function on the probe images for a whole run of sites: it is rigid, the same grouping at every site. Single snapshots give twelve unrelated bases, one per site, which have to be matched to each other after the fact. This experiment tests a construction in between: one function per site, all chosen jointly, with neighbouring sites' functions tied together by a price on change rather than forced to be equal.

The idea comes from Trower, Djurdjevac Conrad and Klus, who cluster graphs that change over time. Here "time" is depth: the vertices are the probe images, and the graph at step $l$ is site $l$'s walk. If the construction's leading functions stay spread over all sites, depth can be read as one continuing description. If they pile onto a few sites, those sites dominate whatever is read from them. If image groupings read off them change little between neighbouring sites while keeping most of what each site's own grouping keeps, the construction is a better compromise than block averages.

The short answer (final checkpoint, the kNN rule; details and scope below). Group the images into $k$ sets (here $k$ counts sets, not the kNN graph's neighbours) with a fitted readout of the construction, at the default coupling. At $k = 5$ the sets change between neighbouring sites {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k5.churn_ratio}} times less often than sets from four blocks of three sites, and keep almost as much of each site's walk: {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k5.info_cost}} less, on a scale where one is a spectral upper bound on what any grouping into $k$ sets can keep about the walk's next step (shape-aligned readout, relative units, default coupling). At $k = 10$ they keep {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k10.info_cost}} less than the three-site blocks, and only {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k10.info_over_avg}} more than the rigid twelve-site average. Underneath the sets: with each site's roughness put on a common scale, the construction's first $K = 16$ functions spread over all twelve sites ({{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_min}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_max}} of their weight per site). On the walks' own scale they pile onto b9, pool and logits as the coupling weakens. The depth profile of the leading function is approximately reproduced, in most settings, by the twelve sites' spectral gaps alone, so on its own it is weak evidence about sets forming or dissolving.

How I measure it

What the operator charges for. A function $f = (f_0, \dots, f_{11})$ has one slice $f_l$ per site: a value for each probe image at site $l$. Its energy is

$$E_t(f) = \sum_{l} f_l^{\top} H_l\, f_l \;+\; t \sum_{l} \lVert f_{l+1} - f_l \rVert^2, \qquad H_l = (I - M_l)/c_l ,$$

where $M_l$ is site $l$'s lazy walk, the same walk the explorer uses, and $c_l$ is a per-site scale (below). The first term is the roughness of each slice on its own site's walk: it is small when images the walk connects get similar values. The second is the change of the slice from one site to the next, comparing image $i$ at site $l$ with the same image $i$ at site $l+1$. The modes are the eigenvectors of $L_t = \mathrm{blockdiag}(H_l) + t\,(D_{\text{path}} \otimes I)$, where $D_{\text{path}}$ is the Laplacian of the chain of sites with free ends. Low modes are functions that are smooth on each site's walk and, depending on $t$, change slowly along depth; I read their joint span, the low space (first $K = 16$ modes), rather than single modes. Everything is solved on functions whose every slice has mean zero. That excludes the slice-constant ("temporal") modes, which say nothing about the images; the paper illustrates filtering them by inspection.

All {{fact:x.through-depth.n_units}} stored units pass the stage's numerical gates (largest residual {{fact:x.through-depth.max_resid}}). The specification is SPEC_SPACETIME.md; the code is spacetime.py and spacetime_stage.py.

How it differs from a block average. As $t \to \infty$ every slice is forced to be the same function, and the low modes tend to those of the average $\tfrac{1}{12}\sum_l H_l$. In native units ($c_l = 1$) that is exactly the twelve-site block average of the other experiments; in the default relative units it is the average of the rescaled site operators, a different operator. As $t \to 0$ the sites decouple and the modes can be chosen site by site, each site's own eigenvectors. In between, a slice may differ from its neighbours' at a price.

How it differs from the paper's matrix. In ordinary words: the paper's construction links image $i$ at site $l$ to the images that site $l$'s walk steps to from $i$, placed at site $l+1$, which is a random neighbour (or, with the lazy walk's self-step, $i$ itself). Across neighbouring sites, this page's operator links image $i$ only to itself (within a site, the walk's edges remain). For these symmetric walks the paper's matrix has an exact energy (SPEC_SPACETIME.md, statement S2): for an eigenvector $f$ of its matrix with eigenvalue $\lambda$, normalised so that $\sum_l b_l \lVert f_l \rVert^2 = 1$ with $b = (1, 2, \dots, 2, 1)$, $1 - \lambda = \sum_{l=0}^{10} \sum_{i,j} (M_l)_{ij}\, \big(f_l(i) - f_{l+1}(j)\big)^2$. Roughness and change are both charged through that one walk step, with no separate coefficient between them. In building the paper's matrix, site $l$'s walk is used for both slices $l$ and $l+1$, and the last site's walk does not enter (it is still used to score the fitted sets below). The two constructions are different graphs on the same vertices, not one energy at two exchange rates. The paper's matrix is shown beside the results with the same readings (its slices weighted by $\sqrt{b_l}$ so that they are orthonormal in the same sense) and the same fitted set readouts; the paper's own clustering step, $k$-means on the stacked rows, is not reproduced.

Why there is a coupling slider. The paper calls its construction parameter-free: its graph has no coupling coefficient (the number of clusters still has to be chosen). With the identity link nothing fixes the rate. Roughness is measured in units of each walk and change in squared norm, so $t$ is a free choice, and I show six values instead of picking one. They are fixed multiples of a balance coupling $t^*$ (Algorithm $1$ of Froyland, Kalia and Koltai). The excluded slice-constant modes serve only as a reference here: the first nonzero one has eigenvalue $t \cdot 4\sin^2(\pi/24)$, which grows with $t$, and $t^*$ is the coupling at which it equals the first eigenvalue on mean-zero slices. It balances two eigenvalues, not two energy terms, and it is a ruler, not an optimum. The six couplings are $t = t^* \times \tfrac{1}{64}, \tfrac{1}{16}, \tfrac14, 1, 4, 16$; at the final checkpoint that is {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.t_list}} in relative units ($t^* =$ {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.tstar}}) and {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.t_list}} in native units ($t^* =$ {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.tstar}}).

Two unit conventions. In native walk units $c_l = 1$. In relative site units (the default), $c_l$ is the mean of site $l$'s first eight nontrivial values of $1 - \lambda$, which is the least mean roughness any eight orthonormal mean-zero functions can have at that site; dividing by it sets that quantity to one at every site. It does not equalise cuts or gaps. The choice matters here because the late sites' walks are much smoother: $c_l$ is {{fact:x.through-depth.scale_mid_lo}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.scale_mid_hi}} at b1 to b8 but {{fact:x.through-depth.scale_pool}} at pool and {{fact:x.through-depth.scale_logits}} at logits, so smooth functions are about {{fact:x.through-depth.scale_ratio}} times cheaper at logits in native units.

Readings of the low space, all closed form and unchanged by any rotation of its basis. $G_l$ is the $N \times K$ matrix of the first $K$ modes' slices at site $l$.

  • Occupancy $\operatorname{tr}(G_l^{\top} G_l)/K$: the share of the low space at site $l$ (it sums to one over the sites).
  • Image directions: the eigenvalues of $R = \sum_l G_l G_l^{\top}$ (trace $K$), and how many of them are needed for $90\%$ of the trace. $K$ depth envelopes of one image function give one direction, not $K$.
  • Local strengths at site $l$: the eigenvalues of $G_l^{\top} G_l$, one per distinct image direction present there. Neighbour frames: the mean $\cos^2$ of the principal angles between the leading four local directions (left singular vectors of $G_l$) at neighbouring sites.
  • Trade-off: $A = \sum_l \operatorname{tr}(G_l^{\top} H_l G_l)$, the total roughness, and $B = \sum_l \lVert G_{l+1} - G_l \rVert_F^2$, the total change. The first $K$ modes minimise $A + tB$ over orthonormal $K$-frames of mean-zero functions, so as $t$ grows, $B$ cannot rise and $A$ cannot fall.

Controls and nulls

  • Gap-only control. Let $g_l$ be the smallest eigenvalue of $H_l$ on mean-zero functions. The $12 \times 12$ matrix $\mathrm{diag}(g_l) + t\,D_{\text{path}}$ keeps the twelve per-site gaps and the chain, and removes which image function is cheap at which site. Its smallest eigenvalue is a lower bound on the operator's first eigenvalue, and its squared ground vector is the depth profile mode $1$ would have if every site's cheapest direction were the same function. Where mode $1$'s profile is close to it, the gaps alone reproduce that profile; it does not recover the image function or the eigenvalue.
  • Independent relabelling at every site (one random draw; final checkpoint, relative units, the real chain's $t^*$). Every site's spectrum and the gap-only bound are unchanged; the correspondence "image $i$ here is image $i$ there" is destroyed.
  • Scale rank $4$ and $16$ instead of $8$ in $c_l$, each at its own $t^*$: the normalised overlap of the two settings' sixteen-mode spaces.
  • Changing the end of the chain: drop pool, drop logits, or count logits twice, each at its own recalibrated $t^*$. Measured on stem to b9: the overlap of the spaces there, and the occupancy there.
  • For the sets: the exact mean information of a random relabelling with the same set sizes, and four reference partitions (described in the sets subsection below).

Answer

At the final checkpoint (the kNN rule), the low space occupies every site in relative units: from {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_min}} ({{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_argmin}}) to {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_max}} ({{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_argmax}}) per site at $t^*$, against an even share of {{fact:x.through-depth.even_share}}. In native units, b9, pool and logits hold {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.f0.occ_tail}} of it at $t^*/64$ and {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.fstar.occ_tail}} at $t^*$. Mode $1$'s depth profile is approximately the gap-only control's (median total-variation distance {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.median}} over {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.n}} settings, maximum {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.max}}). Coupling buys an exact trade: in relative units, from $t^*/64$ to $16t^*$, the total change of the sixteen-mode space falls {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.b_ratio}}-fold while its roughness rises {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.a_ratio}}-fold. The fitted sets (shape-aligned readout, relative units, $t^*$) churn less than three-site blocks at $k = 5$ on every checkpoint under both kernel rules, while the blocks keep at most {{fact:x.through-depth.train.r1.k5.cost_hi}} of the ceiling more; at $k = 10$ the sets keep less information than the blocks on every checkpoint, by {{fact:x.through-depth.train.r0.k10.cost_lo}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.train.r0.k10.cost_hi}} under the kNN rule.

Further evidence

The subsections follow the argument: where the space lives and what shapes it, how many image directions it holds, what coupling trades, the controls, then the fitted sets and the other checkpoints.

Where the low space lives, and what shapes its depth profile

In relative units the space spreads further as the coupling grows: at $16t^*$ every site holds between {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.f5.occ_min}} and {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.f5.occ_max}}. At $t^*$, stem to b3 hold {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_head}} of it and b9 to logits {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.occ_tail}}. In native units at $t^*/64$, {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.f0.n_empty}} of the twelve sites hold under a thousandth of it each. Much of the native-units picture is the late walks being smoother; relative units remove that one scale per site and keep everything else.

Mode $1$ against the gap-only control. Mode $1$'s depth weight (the share of its squared norm at each site) is close to the gap-only profile in most settings: their total-variation distance is below $0.05$ in {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.n_below_05}} of {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.n}} settings (eight checkpoints, two kernel rules, two unit conventions, six couplings). The largest, {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.max}}, is at {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.all.max_where}}. At the default setting (final checkpoint, relative units, $t^*$) it is {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.rel.fstar}}, against {{fact:x.through-depth.tvu.rel.fstar}} between mode $1$ and an even profile; there mode $1$ leans further toward the late sites than the gap-only profile. In native units at $t^*/64$ the distance is {{fact:x.through-depth.tv.nat.f0}}, for a profile {{fact:x.through-depth.tvu.nat.f0}} away from even: the gaps alone reproduce mode $1$'s concentration on the late sites. The first eigenvalue at the default setting is {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.lam1}}, against the gap-only lower bound {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.scalar_energy}}. A single mode's depth profile is therefore weak evidence of a set forming or dissolving.

How many image directions the space holds

Sixteen modes need not be sixteen image functions: different modes, or one mode at several sites, can reuse the same image function. Pooled over the sites, $90\%$ of the space's weight lies in {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.n90.f0}} image directions at $t^*/64$, {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.n90.f3}} at $t^*$ and {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.n90.f5}} at $16t^*$ in relative units ({{fact:x.through-depth.nat.n90.f0}}, {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.n90.f3}} and {{fact:x.through-depth.nat.n90.f5}} in native units; the paper's matrix {{fact:x.through-depth.paper.n90}}). So at weak coupling the sixteen modes are not sixteen separate findings about the images. The count does not say at which sites the shared directions recur.

Neighbouring sites' leading four local directions largely agree: the mean $\cos^2$ averaged over the links is {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.frame.mean_lo}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.frame.mean_hi}} across the six couplings in relative units ({{fact:x.through-depth.paper.frame.mean}} for the paper's matrix). The low values are sensitive to small gaps between local strengths. All {{fact:x.through-depth.frame.n_weak}} links under $0.5$ (of {{fact:x.through-depth.frame.n_links_all}} across both unit conventions and six couplings) have, at one of their two sites, a gap between the fourth and fifth local strengths of about {{fact:x.through-depth.frame.weak_tie_max}} or less of the fourth, so which four directions lead is decided by small differences. Across the {{fact:x.through-depth.frame.n_untied}} links where both sites have a gap above {{fact:x.through-depth.frame.tie_cut}}, the lowest value is {{fact:x.through-depth.frame.untied_min}}.

The exact trade-off

For the first $K$ modes, $A + tB$ equals the sum of the first $K$ eigenvalues, and the modes minimise it, so the curve below can only move up and to the left as $t$ grows. That is a property of the construction, not a finding; the data add the rates. In relative units, from $t^*/64$ to $16t^*$, the sixteen-mode space's total change $B$ falls from {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.f0.b16}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.f5.b16}} while its roughness $A$ rises from {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.f0.a16}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.f5.a16}}. At $t^*$ the two are {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.a16}} and {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.b16}}: compared with $t^*/64$, the change has dropped by a share of {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.b_drop}} for {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.a_rise}} times the roughness. Change then makes up {{fact:x.through-depth.rel.fstar.share16}} of the energy $A + t^*B$ ({{fact:x.through-depth.nat.fstar.share16}} in native units).

Sets through depth: a fitted readout

This is a post-hoc fit, not part of the construction. Sets are read off the first eight modes with $k$-means, one fit for all sites together. Only sites holding more than a thousandth of an even share of the rank-$8$ space take part. Each such site's $N \times 8$ block $G_l$ is scaled by one number so that its rows have mean squared norm one. Then one $k$-means with centroids shared by all participating sites labels every (image, site) pair there. Two readouts answer different questions. Shared coordinates fits the scaled rows as they are: did this image change its joint spectral coordinates? Shape-aligned first maps each site onto the already aligned previous site, by the orthogonal transformation (reflections allowed) that best superimposes the same images (Procrustes, an SVD), so the transformations compose along the chain and restart after a site without labels; it then asks about changes that such a transformation cannot absorb. The transformation is poorly determined where the cross matrix of two sites is nearly singular: at the default setting, {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.link_n_below_01}} of the {{fact:x.through-depth.n_links}} links have a smallest-to-largest singular value ratio under $0.01$ (smallest {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.link_min}}, at {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.link_min_at}}). The readout's rank, $8$, is part of the readout and was fixed in advance. On the specification's toy (one group planted to merge at the late sites), at rank $12$ to $16$ the late sites' noise directions are rescaled to full size and the merged group is cut at random.

Scores, the same for every partition. Information kept at site $l$: the $\chi^2$ information between an image's set at site $l$ and the image that site $l$'s non-lazy walk $2M_l - I$ steps to next, as a share of its ceiling, a spectral upper bound for partitions into up to $k$ sets that a partition need not reach (the $k-1$ largest squared nontrivial eigenvalues of that walk). A fit may occupy fewer than $k$ sets at some sites (as few as {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k10.a.fstar.groups_min}} at the default with $k = 10$). The null is the exact mean information of a random relabelling with the realised set sizes; averaged over sites it is {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k5.null_lo}} to {{fact:x.through-depth.sets.k5.null_hi}} of the ceiling across the partitions plotted at $k = 5$. Churn is the share of images whose set changes between neighbouring sites, after the best relabelling of each link. Information is averaged over the labelled sites, churn over the links with both ends labelled ({{fact:x.through-depth.n_links}} links when all sites are labelled). References, each $k$-means on eight unweighted eigenvectors of its own operator and scored the same way: each site independently (labels matched along the chain), four blocks of three sites (each block's average operator), the twelve-site block average, and its relative-units version. The paper's matrix gets the same two readouts.

Caveats

  • One network, one seed.
  • The probe is class-stratified ({{fact:n_images}} test images, the same number from each class), so the labels chose the vertices, although no label enters any operator or basis. Classes appear here only as validation (the flow's class split and the mutual information).
  • One recorded site is one unit of depth and one unit of mass, a convention. Sites are not evenly spaced in the network (pool and logits are single operations, b1 to b9 are residual blocks), and every number here depends on that spacing.
  • The default coupling $t^*$ balances two eigenvalues. It is a ruler for placing the six couplings, not an optimum of anything measured here; the set comparison changes along the couplings, and the page shows all six.
  • The set readout is a fit: $k$-means on a rank of $8$ fixed in advance, and the rank is part of the readout (on the specification's toy it degrades at rank $12$ to $16$). The shape-aligned readout's rotations are poorly determined at several links (see the sets subsection).
  • Mode numbers are local to one setting. They are not matched across couplings, unit conventions, checkpoints or kernel rules, so "mode $1$" in two settings need not be the same function. Mode $1$ is read separately in each setting; the comparisons of the whole space use the rotation-invariant readings.
  • In native units at the three weakest couplings, some sites get no set labels, and set scores averaged over sites are not comparable with the references (see the sets subsection).
  • Neighbour-frame similarity is sensitive where a site's fourth and fifth local strengths are close; every value under $0.5$ on this page sits at such a near-tie.

Open checks

None of these has been run.

  • A second seed, and a probe drawn without class stratification.
  • A coupling that differs between links (for example scaled by each pair of sites' gaps), instead of one $t$ for the whole chain.
  • The set readout at ranks other than $8$ on the real network, and a readout that does not force every image into a set.
  • Matching modes across couplings and checkpoints by subspace overlap, so that a single mode could be followed.
