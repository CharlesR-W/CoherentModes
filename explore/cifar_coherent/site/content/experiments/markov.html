Is the constructed walk through the layers approximately Markov under the tested partitions?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, final checkpoint (step {{fact:final_step}}), kernel rule {{fact:x.markov.rule}}, $k = 10$ blocks per site; other rules and $k = 4$ under Caveats.

Why it is interesting

The method turns each site of the network into a random walk on the probe images: from an image, step to an image the site treats as similar, or stay put. Chaining the sites gives one walk through the whole network, and that walk is Markov by construction: where it goes at the next site depends only on the image it is at now.

Now record only which of $k$ groups the walk is in at each site. If the current group were enough to predict the next group, the network's depth would have a small, readable description: a $k \times k$ table of transition probabilities between groups for each pair of neighbouring sites, instead of one operator on all {{fact:n_images}} images per site. If the exact image still matters once its group is known, the groups leave out something the walk uses to move images on. This experiment measures how far the tested groupings are from such a description, and checks how much of that distance a walk with no geometry at all would also produce.

How I measure it

The process. $Z_s$ is the image the walk is at after site $s$; it starts uniform over the probe and moves by the site's lazy walk $M_s = (I + P_s)/2$, so the pair $(Z_s, Z_{s+1})$ has coupling $M_{s+1}/N$. Each site carries a partition $c_s$ of the images into $k$ blocks, and the coarse process is $C_s = c_s(Z_s)$. Every quantity below is exact, computed from the operators, in nats (specification: SPEC_COARSE.md §6; tests in test_markov.py).

  • One-step defect fraction $\delta_s / I(Z_s; C_{s+1})$ with $\delta_s = I(Z_s; C_{s+1} \mid C_s)$: of the information the exact image carries about the next block, the share that the current block does not carry. It is zero exactly when every image in a block of $c_s$ has the same distribution over next blocks (strong lumpability of the step $s \to s+1$), which is the condition for that step to be a closed $k$-state transition. A positive defect rules out closure of the step; on its own it does not show that earlier blocks would improve the prediction. Dividing by $I(Z_s; C_{s+1})$ makes pairs of sites with different amounts of next-block information comparable; because the denominator differs between walks too, the unnormalised defect (in nats) is reported beside the fraction. Where the denominator is zero the fraction is undefined and is drawn as a gap.
  • Chapman–Kolmogorov (CK) error, the standard check for Markov state models: for sites $a < b$ the true coarse transition $P(C_b \mid C_a)$ against the product of the one-step coarse transitions from $a$ to $b$, as a mass-weighted total-variation distance. It is shown beside the distance of the true transition from independence ($P(C_b)$ for every starting block), which is how much there is to get wrong. Values are averaged over the start sites $a$ with the same horizon $h = b - a$. A nonzero CK error rules out exact first-order Markov behaviour of the coarse process.
  • Coarse memory $I(C_{s+2}; C_s \mid C_{s+1}) / H(C_{s+2} \mid C_{s+1})$: the share of the uncertainty about the next block, given the current block, that the previous block removes. A positive value shows that the coarse process is not exactly first-order Markov: the past block improves the prediction.

The partitions. (i) Each site's own partition: spectral $k$-means of that site's single-snapshot operator, the method's own coarse description of each site. (ii) One shared partition: b9's spectral $k$-means used at every site, fixed in the specification before any result. It separates "the partition changes from site to site" from "the walk moves images between blocks". (iii) The CIFAR classes used as the partition at every site, for validation only. PCA $k$-means and random partitions are in Appendix D.

Why $k = 10$. At the final checkpoint the chains {{fact:x.markov.gap_chains}} have a clear spectral gap after $k = 10$ modes (the gap criterion in Appendix C), so $k = 10$ is the number of blocks the late operators' spectra single out. It is not chosen from the labels; that the classes also number ten is what lets them serve as a validation partition at the same $k$.

Controls and nulls

The lazy uniform walk $U = \tfrac12\left(I + \tfrac{1}{N-1}(J - I)\right)$ at every site, with exactly the same partitions. It keeps one mechanism of the constructed walk: an image stays put with probability $\tfrac12$ at each site. It removes the geometry of the moves that leave an image: on $U$ every other image is equally likely.

  • With partitions that differ from site to site, the stay-put mechanism alone produces a defect: images of one current block that stay put land in whichever next blocks the next site's partition assigns them. The comparison is of fractions with different denominators: the image carries a median {{fact:x.markov.own.real.info_nats}} nats about the next block on the constructed walk and {{fact:x.markov.own.uniform.info_nats}} on the control, and the median defect is {{fact:x.markov.own.real.defect_nats}} against {{fact:x.markov.own.uniform.defect_nats}} nats. A similar defect fraction on the control therefore shows that this mechanism alone loses a similar fraction of the next-block information, not a similar amount. It does not split the real defect into a stay-put part and a geometry part; the two walks are different processes and the quantities do not add across them.
  • With one partition shared by every site the control is exactly zero, by construction: on $U$ the distribution of the next block depends only on the current block. A nonzero defect with a shared partition therefore comes from the constructed walk sending images of one block to the other blocks in different proportions.

The three measures answer two questions. The one-step defect asks whether each step is closed on the blocks (lumpability). Coarse memory and the CK error ask whether the coarse process, as it is, behaves as a first-order Markov chain; positive values rule that out. Only adjacent triples and pairwise transitions are tested, so values near zero would not certify either property.

Answer

Closure. No tested step is closed on the blocks. With each site's own partition the median defect fraction over the {{fact:x.markov.n_pairs}} adjacent pairs is {{fact:x.markov.own.real.defect}} ({{fact:x.markov.own.real.defect_nats}} nats) on the constructed walk and {{fact:x.markov.own.uniform.defect}} ({{fact:x.markov.own.uniform.defect_nats}} nats) on the control; with b9's partition at every site it is {{fact:x.markov.shared.real.defect}} ({{fact:x.markov.shared.real.defect_nats}} nats; range {{fact:x.markov.shared.real.defect_min}} to {{fact:x.markov.shared.real.defect_max}}), where the control is exactly zero.
Markov behaviour. Neither tested coarse process is exactly first-order Markov: the previous block removes a median {{fact:x.markov.own.real.mem}} of the remaining uncertainty with the own partitions (control {{fact:x.markov.own.uniform.mem}}) and {{fact:x.markov.shared.real.mem}} with b9's partition (control exactly zero), and the CK error two sites ahead is {{fact:x.markov.h2.own.real.ck}} and {{fact:x.markov.h2.shared.real.ck}}. The shared partition reduces every measured error but removes none.

Further evidence

Do small one-step defects add up over many sites? A one-step defect says nothing directly about how wrong the chained coarse model is between distant sites, and a large chained error could simply reflect a transition that is far from independence. The CK error answers the first, and the distance from independence gives its scale. Chaining each site's own one-step coarse transitions misses the true transition two sites ahead by {{fact:x.markov.h2.own.real.ck}} in total variation (distance from independence {{fact:x.markov.h2.own.real.ckind}}; control {{fact:x.markov.h2.own.uniform.ck}}), and from stem to logits, {{fact:x.markov.h_max}} sites apart, by {{fact:x.markov.h11.own.real.ck}} against a distance from independence of {{fact:x.markov.h11.own.real.ckind}} (control {{fact:x.markov.h11.own.uniform.ck}}). With b9's partition at every site the errors are {{fact:x.markov.h2.shared.real.ck}} and {{fact:x.markov.h11.shared.real.ck}} (distances from independence {{fact:x.markov.h2.shared.real.ckind}} and {{fact:x.markov.h11.shared.real.ckind}}; control exactly zero).

Caveats

  • The stay-put mechanism is part of the constructed walk ($M_s = (I + P_s)/2$), so every absolute value includes it. The control shows what fraction of the next-block information it alone loses with the own partitions; it is not subtracted.
  • Neighbouring own partitions agree only partly (median normalised mutual information {{fact:x.markov.own.nmi}}), which is what the stay-put mechanism turns into a defect.
  • Figures use kernel rule {{fact:x.markov.rule}}. Across the {{fact:x.markov.n_rules}} kernel rules the median defect fraction ranges from {{fact:x.markov.rules.own.real.defect_lo}} to {{fact:x.markov.rules.own.real.defect_hi}} with the own partitions (control {{fact:x.markov.rules.own.uniform.defect_lo}} to {{fact:x.markov.rules.own.uniform.defect_hi}}) and from {{fact:x.markov.rules.shared.real.defect_lo}} to {{fact:x.markov.rules.shared.real.defect_hi}} with b9's partition.
  • At $k = 4$ the median defect fraction is {{fact:x.markov.k4.own.real.defect}} with the own partitions (control {{fact:x.markov.k4.own.uniform.defect}}) and {{fact:x.markov.k4.shared.real.defect}} with b9's partition.
  • Only adjacent triples and pairwise transitions are tested; a process can pass these and still have longer memory. One network, one seed, one probe, the final checkpoint.
