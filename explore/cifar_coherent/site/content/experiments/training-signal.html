Do the images grouped alike in activations also have similar loss gradients?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, all {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints (steps {{fact:x.training-signal.first_step}} to {{fact:final_step}}), kernel rule {{fact:x.training-signal.rule}}; groupings: the coherent sets ($r = 4$) of the time block at site b9 over all checkpoints; $r = 2$ and the other sites under Further evidence and Caveats.

Why it is interesting

The activations at a site say which images the network currently treats alike. The loss gradient of an image says something different: the direction of a hypothetical plain gradient step on that one probe image, with the network in evaluation mode, across every weight of the network. Two images whose gradients point the same way would push the weights in similar directions (up to the size of each push); two with opposite gradients would pull them apart.

If the images that a site groups together also had aligned gradients beyond what their labels already give, the groups found in the activations would also be groups for this gradient signal. If not, this statistic detects nothing in the groups beyond their class make-up. This experiment measures which of the two holds for the tested grouping and statistic.

How I measure it

The gradients. For each probe image $i$ and checkpoint, $g_i$ is the gradient of the image's cross-entropy loss with respect to all trainable parameters of the network (computed per image with vmap(grad(loss)) in measure_v2.py), and the stored quantity is the cosine $\cos(g_i, g_j)$ for every pair (gradgram_<step>.npy). The cosine compares directions only: the length of $g_i$ mostly tracks how large the image's loss is. These gradients belong to the whole network and do not depend on any site; a site enters only through the grouping that is scored. The gradients use the labels (through the loss), so this is a validation measurement: nothing here feeds back into the sets.

The statistic. For a grouping of some images, the mean gradient cosine over pairs of distinct images in the same group minus the mean over pairs in different groups (pair-weighted, so larger groups weigh more; images the grouping leaves unassigned are left out of both means). It is positive when images grouped together have more aligned gradients than images grouped apart. Every grouping below is scored on the same assigned images, those the sets assign ({{fact:x.training-signal.r4.n_assigned}} of {{fact:n_images}} at $r = 4$), so that differences between groupings are not differences of population.

The grouping. The coherent sets of the time block at b9 over all {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints: one labelling of the probe, fixed over training, so the same grouping is scored against the gradients of every checkpoint. b9 is the last convolutional site; groupings from the other sites' time blocks, scored against the same gradients, are in Appendix D. $r = 4$ is the site's default number of sets; $r = 2$ is reported beside it. Neither is chosen from the gradients or the labels.

Why a class-built reference is needed. In logit space the cross-entropy gradient of image $i$ is $p_i - e_{y_i}$: it always contains the image's own label. Two images of different classes therefore push their own class's logit in opposite directions however alike they are otherwise, so the label term can favour groupings that follow the classes. A grouping that merges classes is therefore compared with a label-built grouping that merges the same classes: the class-union grouping, each class given wholly to the set that holds most of its images, scored on the same assigned images. It splits no class. It is a reference, not a ceiling: nothing bounds the sets' value by it for whole-parameter gradient cosines. The parameter gradients $g_i = J_i^\top (p_i - e_{y_i})$, with $J_i$ the Jacobian of the logits, carry this label term through the network.

A separate, label-free span comparison. This is a different quantity on different objects, and it cannot settle the loss-gradient comparison above or its nulls. It uses label-free sample kernels built from the same Jacobians (SPEC_DOT.md §2): the NTK, $\operatorname{tr} J_i J_j^\top$, and the GGN kernel, which weights each image's Jacobian by the square root of the cross-entropy Hessian in the logits, $\operatorname{diag}(p_i) - p_i p_i^\top$ (it uses the predictions, not the labels). The Jacobian features are centred over the probe before cosine normalisation (the "cos" form; kernels_modal.py, double-centring of the Gram, then row normalisation), turned into an operator by the same kernel rule, and its leading modes are compared with each site's leading modes by span overlap: the mean squared cosine of the principal angles between the two spans of $r$ modes ($1$ for identical spans, $0$ for orthogonal ones). This compares the leading structure of the two geometries, not the sets.

Controls and nulls

  • Permutation among the assigned images ({{fact:x.training-signal.n_perm}} draws). It keeps the set sizes and the unassigned images and removes any relation between the sets and the images. A value above its band shows only that the sets are not arbitrary with respect to the gradients.
  • Permutation within each class ({{fact:x.training-signal.n_perm}} draws). Within each class, the set labels of its assigned images are shuffled among them. It keeps each class's count in every set, so every set's class make-up is exactly preserved, and keeps the unassigned images; it removes which images of a class the sets put together. It tests this one scalar contrast, given the assigned images and the class-by-set counts. A value above its band would show that the sets' allocation of images within classes raises the contrast; inside means no departure detected by this statistic; below means the actual allocation gives less contrast than shuffled allocations. Neither inside nor below rules out within-class gradient structure that this aggregate misses.
  • The class-union grouping's own permutation null (same construction). The ratio of the sets' value to the class-union value is quoted only at checkpoints where the class-union value is above the $97.5$th percentile of this null; it is at all {{fact:x.training-signal.n_steps}} checkpoints for both $r$.
  • For the span overlaps, $r/N$ (here {{fact:x.training-signal.rn.r4}} at $r = 4$) is the expected overlap of two random $r$-dimensional spans. It is a scale reference, not a control: the site operators and the gradient kernels come from the same network on the same images, so they share structure for reasons that have nothing to do with training dynamics (see Caveats), and no null for that was run.

Answer

At the final checkpoint, on the {{fact:x.training-signal.r4.n_assigned}} images they assign, b9's sets have a within-minus-between gradient cosine of {{fact:x.training-signal.r4.final.sets}}, above the permutation band ($97.5$th percentile {{fact:x.training-signal.r4.final.perm_hi}}) but below the class-union grouping on the same images ({{fact:x.training-signal.r4.final.union}}, ratio {{fact:x.training-signal.r4.final.ratio}}) and below the class-count-preserving permutation band ({{fact:x.training-signal.r4.final.wcls_lo}} to {{fact:x.training-signal.r4.final.wcls_hi}}). At $r = 4$ the observed contrast is below that band at every measured checkpoint after initialization, and inside it at initialization; at $r = 2$ it is above the band at initialization and at the final checkpoint (Further evidence).

Further evidence

With two sets the final checkpoint differs. At $r = 2$ ({{fact:x.training-signal.r2.n_assigned}} assigned images) the sets score {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.final.sets}} at the final checkpoint against {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.final.union}} for their class union (ratio {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.final.ratio}}) and a within-class band of {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.final.wcls_lo}} to {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.final.wcls_hi}}: above it, as at step {{fact:x.training-signal.first_step}}, and below it at the other {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.n_below_wcls}} checkpoints. So with two sets the contrast is below the class-count-preserving band at those {{fact:x.training-signal.r2.n_below_wcls}} checkpoints and above it at the final one; the chart is in Appendix D.

A separate label-free comparison. The answer is measured with gradients that contain the label, which can favour groupings built from classes. The label-free GGN and NTK span comparison asks a different question (do the leading spans of the two geometries overlap?) and cannot adjudicate the loss-gradient nulls; it is kept because it is the only check here with no label in the gradient side. At the final checkpoint b9's leading four modes overlap {{fact:x.training-signal.ggn.r4.b9}} with the GGN-cos kernel's and {{fact:x.training-signal.ntk.r4.b9}} with the NTK-cos kernel's, against {{fact:x.training-signal.rn.r4}} for random spans; at the stem they are {{fact:x.training-signal.ggn.r4.stem}} and {{fact:x.training-signal.ntk.r4.stem}}. Without the label, the late sites' leading modes still overlap the gradient-side kernels' far above the random-span scale; part of the NTK overlap is built in (see Caveats), and this compares leading spans, not the sets' gradient alignment.

Caveats

  • One grouping. The lead figure scores the time block at b9 only. Groupings from every site's time block, scored against the same whole-network gradients at the final checkpoint, range from {{fact:x.training-signal.sites.sets_min}} to {{fact:x.training-signal.sites.sets_max}}; all {{fact:x.training-signal.sites.n}} are above their permutation band, {{fact:x.training-signal.sites.n_below_wcls}} are below their within-class band and {{fact:x.training-signal.sites.n_above_wcls}} above it. That chart varies the grouping, not the gradients, and is not a layer-by-layer measurement.
  • Scale over training. All values shrink over training, and so does the alignment of per-image gradients in general: the mean cosine over all pairs falls from {{fact:x.training-signal.mean_cos.first}} to {{fact:x.training-signal.mean_cos.final}}, and the ten classes' within-minus-between value (all {{fact:n_images}} images) from {{fact:x.training-signal.classes.first}} to {{fact:x.training-signal.classes.final}}. Comparisons are made within a checkpoint, not across checkpoints.
  • The NTK contains a site's geometry. In the trace NTK, the final layer's weights contribute a term proportional to the dot product of the pool activations, so part of the overlap at pool and the sites just before it is built in. In the GGN kernel the same term is multiplied by $\operatorname{tr}(H_i^{1/2} H_j^{1/2})$, which depends on the predictions, so the link is weakened but not removed. Neither comparison has a null beyond the $r/N$ scale reference.
  • Near-ties in the spectra. The eigengap at the cutoff ($\lambda_r - \lambda_{r+1}$) is {{fact:x.training-signal.ggn.r4.gap}} for the GGN-cos operator and {{fact:x.training-signal.ntk.r4.gap}} for NTK-cos at $r = 4$, and {{fact:x.training-signal.sitegap.r4.b9}} for b9 (lazy eigenvalues). How stable the four-mode spans are under resampling of the probe was not tested.
  • Site kernel form. With the dot (Euclidean) site kernel of Appendix A instead of the cosine one, b9's overlaps are {{fact:x.training-signal.ggn_dotsite.r4.b9}} (GGN-cos) and {{fact:x.training-signal.ntk_dotsite.r4.b9}} (NTK-cos); the per-site chart is in Appendix D.
  • The loss-gradient kernel through the same span comparison (label-dependent) overlaps b9's leading four modes by {{fact:x.training-signal.lossgrad.r4.b9}}; it is kept apart from the label-free comparison and charted in Appendix D.
  • One network, one seed, one label-stratified probe, {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints, kernel rule {{fact:x.training-signal.rule}}.
