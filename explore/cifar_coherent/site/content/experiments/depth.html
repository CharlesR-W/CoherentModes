Where along depth does the network redraw its grouping?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0 (seed 1 only for the floor), the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, the final checkpoint (step {{fact:final_step}}) and step $0$, kernel rule {{fact:x.depth.rule}}, the single-snapshot operators of neighbouring sites ({{fact:x.depth.n_pairs}} adjacent pairs, stem→b1 to pool→logits).

Why it is interesting

In a residual network most layers add an update to what they receive, and the shortcut passes the rest through unchanged, so neighbouring sites could treat images alike almost everywhere. If most sites carry the grouping forward and a few redraw it, those few are where the network changes what counts as similar. That gives a map of the network by what it does rather than by how it is drawn. The architecture suggests one candidate answer: the stage boundaries, where the feature map is downsampled and the number of channels doubles. If the grouping is redrawn elsewhere, the stage boundaries alone do not explain where; the untrained network (step $0$) shows whether the pattern changes with training.

How I measure it

Two measures of change between a site and the next one, drawn on one chart so that they can be compared pair by pair.

  • Sets. The matched Jaccard between the $r = 4$ SEBA sets of the two sites' single-snapshot operators (pair each set with one set of the other site so that the mean Jaccard overlap is largest; Appendix C). It is plotted as $1 - $ matched Jaccard, so that $0$ means the same grouping and larger means more change.
  • Operators. The projector distance $d_3 = 1 - \lVert U_3(A)^\top U_3(B) \rVert_F^2 / 3$ between the spans of the two operators' three leading non-constant eigenvectors ($0$ = same span; chance {{fact:x.depth.op.chance}}). SEBA at $r = 4$ rotates the constant and $\psi_1, \psi_2, \psi_3$ into four sets, so $d_3$ compares exactly the spaces the sets are cut from, without the hard assignment of images to sets. (The generator checks that the alignment study's operators are the archive's: their leading eigenvalues agree at every site.) A span is only well defined when the cut $3 \mid 4$ is reproducible on resampled probes; where it is not, the value is drawn as a separate dot and not used.

The two measures answer the same question at different grain. The sets are what a reader sees in the explorer, but they are hard: a set can be cut differently while the span it comes from barely moves. The operator distance has no cut into sets. A pair that stands out in both is unlikely to be only an artefact of turning eigenvectors into sets (the two measures share the same spectral input, so this does not rule out every artefact).

Controls and nulls

  • Permutation null (sets): site $s$'s set labels shuffled over the images, sizes and unassigned count kept; it gives the agreement that set sizes alone produce (at most {{fact:x.depth.sets.null_hi_max}}, $97.5$th percentile).
  • Step $0$ (both): the same measures on the untrained network. It shows what the architecture and the images give without training; it does not separate what the shortcuts, the random filters or the image statistics each contribute. A pair that changes at step $0$ as much as after training is not evidence of a learned redrawing.
  • Seed floor (operators): $d_3$ between seeds $0$ and $1$ at the same site and checkpoint, how far two training runs that differ only in their seed end up ({{fact:x.depth.op.floor_min}} to {{fact:x.depth.op.floor_max}} at the final checkpoint over the sites whose floor cut is resolved; unresolved at {{fact:x.depth.op.floor_unresolved}}). It is one observed pair of seeds, a reference for scale rather than a measured range of run-to-run variation.
  • A second seed (sets): the final $r = 4$ sets of seed $1$ (kernel rule {{fact:x.depth.rule}}), compared between neighbouring sites in the same way, and with seed $0$'s sets at the same site.

Answer

At the final checkpoint, {{fact:x.depth.sets.final.min_pair}} has both the lowest set agreement and the largest resolved three-mode projector distance: the sets of neighbouring sites agree least at {{fact:x.depth.sets.final.min_pair}} ({{fact:x.depth.sets.final.min}}; range {{fact:x.depth.sets.final.min}} to {{fact:x.depth.sets.final.max}} at {{fact:x.depth.sets.final.max_pair}}, null at most {{fact:x.depth.sets.null_hi_max}}), and the operators change most at {{fact:x.depth.op.final.max_pair}} ($d_3$ = {{fact:x.depth.op.final.max}}), then at {{fact:x.depth.op.final.second_pair}} ({{fact:x.depth.op.final.second}}) and {{fact:x.depth.op.final.third_pair}} ({{fact:x.depth.op.final.third}}); in the untrained network both single out {{fact:x.depth.sets.step0.min_pair}} instead (sets {{fact:x.depth.sets.step0.min}}, $d_3$ = {{fact:x.depth.op.step0.max}}), which after training is the pair whose sets agree most ({{fact:x.depth.sets.final.at_step0_min_pair}}) and whose $d_3$ is {{fact:x.depth.op.final.at_step0_max_pair}} (cut {{fact:x.depth.op.final.at_step0_max_pair_resolved}}).

The two profiles rise and fall together along depth: the Spearman rank correlation between $1 -$ matched Jaccard and $d_3$ over the pairs with a resolved cut is {{fact:x.depth.rho.final}} at the final checkpoint ({{fact:x.depth.rho.final.n}} pairs) and {{fact:x.depth.rho.step0}} at step $0$ ({{fact:x.depth.rho.step0.n}} pairs). The largest change after training lies inside a stage ({{fact:x.depth.op.final.max_pair_stage}}), not at a stage boundary.

Against the seed floor. At every other resolved pair the neighbouring operators differ by no more than the larger of the two sites' seed floors. Caveats

  • The two measures are on different scales; compare where along depth they are high or low, not their heights.
  • Only two seeds were measured; one seed pair gives an observed reference, not a spread of run-to-run variation.
  • The operator ranking uses only the leading three-mode span and excludes pairs whose cut is unresolved (at the final checkpoint {{fact:x.depth.op.final.unresolved}}); it says nothing about the rest of the spectrum.
  • Which of the operator changes is largest depends on the cut ($k = 3$ against $k = 4$, above). The pair the sets single out is among the two largest in both.
  • The rank correlation is over {{fact:x.depth.rho.final.n}} pairs and is descriptive.
  • Only adjacent sites at two checkpoints are in the lead figure; every pair of sites, every checkpoint and blocks of two and three sites are in Appendix D.
