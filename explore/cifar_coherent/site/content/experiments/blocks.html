Which layer's structure does a whole-path block reflect?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, final checkpoint (step {{fact:final_step}}), kernel rule {{fact:x.blocks.rule}}, the {{fact:x.blocks.n_starts}} averaged blocks that start at one site and end at the logits; the other kernel rules under Further evidence.

Why it is interesting

Each site of the network gives a random walk on the probe images, and the slowest-changing functions of that walk say which images the site treats alike. A block averages the walks of a run of consecutive sites, so its slowest functions say which images are treated alike along the whole run. The block from a site to the logits asks this of everything the network does to an image after that site.

An average over many layers could come out as a compromise that no single layer draws. It could also come out looking like one particular layer, which would mean that the whole-path summary is dominated by one stretch of the network (by resemblance, not causation), and that reading that one layer would be almost as good as reading the block. If the layer it resembles is the first layer of the block, the block adds little; if it is a later layer, the block is reporting a split that the start of the path does not yet draw. This experiment measures which single layer each such block resembles, and how closely.

How I measure it

The quantity. For the averaged block $a \to$ logits (the mean of the lazy walks $M_c = (I + P_c)/2$ of the sites $c = a, \dots,$ logits) and for each single site $c$, I take the leading $k$ nontrivial eigenvectors of each and compute the span overlap $\lVert U_k(\text{block})^\top U_k(c) \rVert_F^2 / k$: the mean $\cos^2$ of the $k$ principal angles between the two $k$-dimensional spans. It is $1$ when the spans coincide and {{fact:x.blocks.chance3}} at $k = 3$ for random spans orthogonal to the constant ($k/(N-1)$). It is the complement of the projector distance of Appendix C. The eigenvectors are the stored ones (layer_64000.npz, vecs × scale); nothing is refitted.

Why spans, and why $k = 1$ and $k = 3$. A single eigenvector is only defined up to rotation when its eigenvalue is close to the next one, so a single-mode match ($k = 1$) means something only where the cut between modes $1$ and $2$ is resolved. The span of the leading three ($k = 3$) does not depend on how modes inside it are rotated, only on the cut after mode $3$. I state the span answer first and the single-mode answer where its cut is resolved.

When a cut counts as resolved. For a single site I use the reproducibility score $\mathrm{span}_k$ of the spectral-alignment measurement: rebuild the site's operator on two overlapping, label-free subsamples of the probe and take the smallest $\cos^2$ principal angle between the two rebuilds' leading-$k$ spans; the cut is resolved when $\mathrm{span}_k \geq 0.5$ (definition in Appendix C). An averaged block was never rebuilt on subsamples, so for it I use a proxy fixed before looking at the overlaps: its cut counts as unresolved when its eigenvalue gap $\lambda_k - \lambda_{k+1}$ is no larger than the largest gap at which a single site's cut already failed ({{fact:x.blocks.gap_thr}}, at {{fact:x.blocks.gap_thr_where}}, $\mathrm{span}$ {{fact:x.blocks.span_thr_value}}; cuts $k = 1$ to $4$, same checkpoint and rule).

Stated up front, by construction. The block's leading function maximises the mean retention over the sites of the block, and site $a$'s own leading function maximises retention at $a$, so the block's leading function retains no more at $a$ than $a$'s own does (it does at {{fact:x.blocks.n_below}} of {{fact:x.blocks.n_starts}} start sites). That the block differs from its start site is therefore expected; the information is in which site it resembles.

Controls and nulls

  • Chance: random spans give an overlap of {{fact:x.blocks.chance3}} at $k = 3$, far below every measured value, so the comparison between sites, not against chance, carries the answer.
  • The start site (the diagonal of the figure): how much the block keeps of the layer it starts at.
  • The medoid of the block: the site of the block whose own span overlaps most, on average, with those of all the other sites in the block (unresolved sites are excluded both as candidates and from the average). This is the strongest alternative to "one stretch dominates the resemblance": an average could simply land on whichever site is most typical of the run. Where the closest site differs from the medoid, the block is not just reporting the most typical site. Disagreement with the medoid does not establish which sites dominate the average; it only rules out this one simple description.

The overlaps carry no sampling bands: no second seed or probe of these blocks is stored. Differences of a few hundredths between neighbouring sites should not be read as a ranking.

Answer

The leading three-dimensional span of every block starting from {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_first}} to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_last}} is closest to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_site}}'s (overlap {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_best_lo}} to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_best_hi}}, against {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_own_lo}} to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_own_hi}} with the start site itself), and that of every block starting from {{fact:x.blocks.k3.late_first}} onwards is closest to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.late_sites}} among the resolved sites (overlap {{fact:x.blocks.k3.late_best_lo}} to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.late_best_hi}}); the single leading eigenvector, whose cut is resolved everywhere here, gives {{fact:x.blocks.k1.best}}.

The match is not exclusive: for the blocks starting from {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_first}} to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_last}} the sites within two hundredths of the closest are {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_within2}} (largest margin over the runner-up {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_margin_max}}). For the later starts, the raw largest overlap is with {{fact:x.blocks.k3.late_raw_site}} (up to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.late_raw_hi}}), whose $3 \,|\, 4$ cut is unresolved, so I do not report it as the match. The medoid coincides with the closest site at {{fact:x.blocks.k1.n_med_agree}} of {{fact:x.blocks.n_starts}} starts for $k = 1$ but only {{fact:x.blocks.k3.n_med_agree}} for $k = 3$: for the early starts the medoid is {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_med_sites}}, with overlap {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_med_lo}} to {{fact:x.blocks.k3.early_med_hi}}, so at $k = 3$ the block is not simply its most typical site. This does not say which sites dominate the average.

Further evidence

The kernel rule changes the answer. The rule decides how each site's activations become a walk, and so how much of each site's leading contrast its walk retains. Repeating the measurement under each of the {{fact:x.blocks.n_rules}} kernel rules moves the closest site for the block stem $\to$ logits: at $k = 3$, {{fact:x.blocks.rules.k3.stem}}; at $k = 1$, {{fact:x.blocks.rules.k1.stem}}. Under {{fact:x.blocks.rule3}} the walks at stem to b9 retain much less of their leading mean-zero contrast than pool and logits do: their leading lazy eigenvalues are {{fact:x.blocks.rule3.lam_early_lo}} to {{fact:x.blocks.rule3.lam_early_hi}}, against {{fact:x.blocks.rule3.lam_pool}} and {{fact:x.blocks.rule3.lam_logits}}. Every site stays put with the same probability ($\tfrac12$); a lazy eigenvalue near $0.5$ means the non-lazy part of the walk keeps almost none of that contrast after one step, and near $1$ that it persists. As a limiting illustration only: a site whose lazy walk retained every mean-zero function at exactly $0.5$ would add the same amount to every candidate's mean retention and could not change which function the average ranks first. The early sites here are near that limit, not at it, so this is consistent with the change of answer under this rule but does not show that the late sites determine the block's leading mode. Under rule {{fact:x.blocks.rule}} the early sites' leading eigenvalues are {{fact:x.blocks.rule0.lam_early_lo}} to {{fact:x.blocks.rule0.lam_early_hi}}. Which layer a block reflects is therefore a property of the network and the kernel rule together.

Caveats

  • The block's resolution is a proxy (an eigenvalue gap calibrated on single sites), not a subsample rebuild of the block; a gap and a reproducibility score do not track each other closely across single sites (see the cut diagnostics in Appendix D).
  • The answer depends on the kernel rule (Further evidence); the resolution flags exist for rule {{fact:x.blocks.rule}} only.
  • Only blocks ending at the logits, the averaged (not product) operator, the final checkpoint, one seed and one probe. The comparison of the averaged and product operators is in Appendix A.
  • No sampling band: the overlaps of neighbouring late sites differ by a few hundredths, below any resolution this measurement has shown.
