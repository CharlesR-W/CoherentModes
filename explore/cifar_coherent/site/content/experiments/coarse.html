How much of each layer's walk can k groups keep, and does the spectrum prefer a k?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, final checkpoint (step {{fact:final_step}}) unless stated, kernel rule {{fact:x.coarse.rule}}; single snapshots at every site and the {{fact:x.coarse.gap.chains_n}} layer chains; $k = 2, \dots, 32$ groups. Other rules and checkpoints under Caveats.

Why it is interesting

The method turns each site of the network into a random walk on the probe images: from an image, step to an image the site treats as similar, or stay put. Chaining sites gives a walk from one layer to a later one. The simplest description of such a walk is a partition: sort the images into $k$ groups and keep only which group the walk starts in and which group it ends in. A partition with few groups is easy to read; the question is what it costs.

This experiment asks two things. First, how much of the dependence between where the walk starts and where it ends survives when only $k$ groups are kept, measured against all the dependence there is, against the most that any $k$ groups could keep, and against what arbitrary groups keep. Second, whether the walk itself points to a number of groups: if its spectrum has a clear gap after $k$ modes, a $k$-group description is singled out by the operator, without anyone choosing $k$. If there is no such gap, any $k$ is a choice of resolution, and the first answer gives its cost.

How I measure it

The coupling. Every operator here is doubly stochastic, so it defines a joint distribution of a start image $X$ and an end image $Y$: for a single snapshot $M = (I + P)/2$, $X$ is an image and $Y$ where one step of the lazy walk takes it; for a layer chain $Q = M_b \cdots M_a$, $X$ is the image at site $a$ and $Y$ the image the chained walk reaches at site $b$. With uniform weight on the images, the dependence between $X$ and $Y$ is measured by the $\chi^2$ mutual information $$ I(X;Y) = \lVert Q \rVert_F^2 - 1 = \sum_{i \ge 2} \sigma_i^2 ,$$ where $\sigma_1 = 1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots$ are the singular values of $Q$ (near independence it is proportional to Shannon's mutual information: to leading order the Shannon value in nats is half of it). Everything below is exact, from the operators (SPEC_COARSE.md §1–2; tests in test_coarse.py).

  • Retained information. For a partition $c$ of the start images, the one-sided value $I(c(X); Y)$ is the dependence left when only the start group is known. For a chain with partitions $c$ at site $a$ and $c'$ at site $b$, the two-sided value $I(c(X); c'(Y))$ is the dependence between start group and end group: the information a $k$-group description of both layers keeps. Single snapshots use the one-sided value (the operator is symmetric, so both sides agree); chains use the two-sided value, because the question for a chain is whether groups at one layer map to groups at the other.
  • Ceiling. No partition into $k$ nonempty groups keeps more than $F_k = \sigma_2^2 + \dots + \sigma_k^2$ (Eckart–Young: the grouped operator has rank at most $k$); the same holds two-sided with $k$ the smaller number of groups. Every partition is compared with the ceiling at its own number of nonempty groups $k_\text{eff}$, which can be smaller than the number asked for.
  • Why spectral $k$-means is the candidate. For a partition of the start images, the one-sided information it loses is exactly the within-group variance of the full spectral embedding $\Psi(x) = \sqrt N\,(\sigma_2 v_2(x), \sigma_3 v_3(x), \dots)$ (an identity, not an approximation). $k$-means on the top $k - 1$ coordinates of $\Psi$ is the truncated version of that problem: the direct attempt to keep the most one-sided information, not guaranteed to reach the ceiling. For chains it is a heuristic: each end is clustered separately on its own truncated embedding (right singular vectors at the start, left at the end), and the two partitions are then scored as a pair; the identity does not cover that two-sided score. It is compared with SEBA (sparse bases of the leading singular vectors, at the ranks $r \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16\}$ stored in the coarse archive, each image assigned to its largest SEBA value) and with PCA $k$-means (on the top principal components of the activations; no operator involved).
  • Headline quantity. The retained information as a share of the total $I(X;Y)$, shown with the ceiling and the random null at the same $k$. The ratio $I / F_{k_\text{eff}}$ (efficiency) is shown only as a tightness check, for the reason under Controls.
  • The gap criterion (coarse_summary.block_gap, fixed before these results and never fitted to labels). A gap after $k$ modes (constant included) points to $k$ groups. For chains the size of a gap is $\sigma_k / \sigma_{k+1}$. For single snapshots it is $(1 - \sigma_{k+1}) / (1 - \sigma_k)$, because the lazy half squeezes every $\sigma$ towards one, so ratios of $\sigma$ say little; $1 - \sigma$ is the decay rate of the mode, and ratios of rates are unchanged by the laziness. Over $k = 2, \dots, 32$ the largest ratio is a clear gap if it is at least $1.15$ and more than $1.1$ times the runner-up; otherwise there is no clear gap.

$k$ is not chosen here: every chart runs over $k$. The value $k = 10$ quoted in the Answer is where the chains that pass the gap criterion below have their largest ratio, and the classes, which also number ten, are scored there only as validation.

Controls and nulls

  • Random balanced partitions (mean of four draws) give what $k$ arbitrary groups keep. In a single snapshot they keep a nonzero amount, most of it from the stay-put half of $M$ (an image that stays put is always in its own group). For chains they are independent balanced endpoint partitions, drawn separately at the two ends; they keep almost nothing two-sided, but only because their two ends share no membership, so they do not control for what two matching partitions keep through the stay-put path alone.
  • The lazy uniform chain $U^L$ (for chains; $U = \tfrac12\left(I + \tfrac{1}{N-1}(J - I)\right)$, one factor per site of the chain, as coarse.uniform_lazy), scored with the same spectral partitions at both ends. It keeps the stay-put path and the overlap between the two end partitions and removes all geometry, so what the real chain keeps above it is not explained by shared membership and laziness. At $k = 10$ it reaches a median {{fact:x.coarse.unif.share_med}} of the real chain's two-sided value over all chains, and at most {{fact:x.coarse.unif.share_max}}, for a pair of adjacent sites.
  • The total and the ceiling bound the retained information from above. Most of a single snapshot's total is out of reach of any small partition: the stay-put half alone gives every snapshot $I(X;Y) \ge N/4 - 1$ = {{fact:x.coarse.lazy_floor}}.
  • Why efficiency is only a tightness check. On the lazy uniform walk, where every other image is equally likely and there is no geometry at all, every partition attains the ceiling exactly (a test in test_coarse.py). A ratio near one therefore says that a partition is tight for this operator, not that the operator has groups; the retained amount against the total and the null is what answers the question.
  • Classes and predictions are scored as validation partitions at their $k_\text{eff}$. They never choose $k$, a mode or a rule.

Caveats

  • The single-snapshot shares are small partly by construction: the stay-put half of $M$ puts at least {{fact:x.coarse.lazy_floor}} of each total (here {{fact:x.coarse.cells.total_min}} to {{fact:x.coarse.cells.total_max}}) into which exact image the walk is at, which no partition into few groups can keep; at most {{fact:x.coarse.cells.ceil32_pct_max}} of any snapshot's total is within reach of $32$ groups. Scored on $P = 2M - I$ (no stay-put half), the same partitions of the b9 snapshot keep {{fact:x.coarse.cell.p.spec_pct}} against a ceiling of {{fact:x.coarse.cell.p.ceil_pct}} and {{fact:x.coarse.cell.p.rand_pct}} for random partitions.
  • Chain totals shrink with length, so shares are not comparable across lengths: the median adjacent chain has total {{fact:x.coarse.adj.total}} with ten groups within reach of {{fact:x.coarse.adj.ceil_pct}}; stem→logits has total {{fact:x.coarse.full.total}} with {{fact:x.coarse.full.ceil_pct}} within reach.
  • The gap verdicts depend on the kernel rule. Across the {{fact:x.coarse.gap.rules_n}} rules at the final checkpoint, {{fact:x.coarse.gap.rules.chains10_lo}} to {{fact:x.coarse.gap.rules.chains10_hi}} chains have a clear gap after ten; b9→logits has one under {{fact:x.coarse.gap.rules.chain_clear}} of them. The single snapshots' gap after the first nontrivial mode holds at {{fact:x.coarse.gap.rules.cells_k2_lo}} to {{fact:x.coarse.gap.rules.cells_k2_hi}} of the sites, depending on the rule.
  • $\chi^2$ information weights fine structure more than Shannon information; the two rank partitions alike near independence but give different values. The gap thresholds are fixed conventions. One network, one seed, one probe.
