How does the association of the leading eigenfunctions with twelve pixel statistics change over training?

Tested: ResNet-20, seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}}-image probe, the {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints from step $0$ to step {{fact:final_step}}, every site's single-snapshot block, kernel rule {{fact:x.image-stats.rule}}, the leading eigenfunctions $\psi_1..\psi_8$; set comparisons at $r = {{fact:x.image-stats.r_sets}}$.

Why it is interesting

The method describes each site by eigenfunctions of a walk on the probe images: $\psi_1$ is the image-to-image direction along which the site's walk mixes most slowly, the coarsest way the site orders the images. Nothing in that construction knows what the network was trained for, so a leading eigenfunction can just as well follow how bright an image is, how blue its border is, or how busy its texture is. An untrained network already maps images with similar pixels to similar activations, so at step $0$ one should expect exactly that.

The question is how far training moves the leading eigenfunctions away from such simple image properties, at which sites and when. If they stayed tied to brightness and colour at every site, the structure the method finds in a trained network would mostly restate the input. If the tie weakens at some sites and not others, the place and time it weakens suggests where training has replaced the image's coarse appearance with something else as the site's leading direction (a weaker fit can also come from the leading direction changing identity between nearby modes). This experiment measures that tie for one fixed, small set of image statistics; it does not say what replaces it.

How I measure it

The statistics. Twelve numbers per image computed from its pixels alone: the means of R, G and B, the mean luminance $Y$, the RMS contrast of $Y$, the mean saturation, the border-minus-centre differences of R, G, B and $Y$ and their size, and the share of $Y$'s energy at high spatial frequency (definitions in Appendix C). They were fixed before looking at any eigenfunction, cover brightness, colour, contrast, background and texture, and are standardised over the probe. Luminance is linear in the channel means and the border-minus-centre difference of $Y$ in those of R, G and B, so they span {{fact:x.image-stats.rank}} dimensions.

The quantity. For each site and checkpoint, the $R^2$ of a least-squares fit of $\psi_1$ on the twelve statistics plus an intercept, over the {{fact:n_images}} probe images. This is a closed-form fit on an already-computed eigenfunction, not part of the construction. $R^2$ is the share of $\psi_1$'s variance across images that a linear combination of the statistics reproduces: $R^2 = 1$ if $\psi_1$ is such a combination, near zero if no linear combination of them predicts it in this sample (a nonlinear relation can remain). It does not depend on $\psi_1$'s sign or scale, so it is comparable across sites and checkpoints even though the eigenfunction itself changes. $\psi_1$ is used because it is the leading eigenfunction at every snapshot; $\psi_2..\psi_8$ and their span are under Caveats.

What it assumes. A linear relation, in sample, with these twelve statistics. A low $R^2$ therefore says that $\psi_1$ is not a linear function of these statistics; it does not rule out that $\psi_1$ follows other image properties, or these ones nonlinearly. The comparison with the method run on the pixels themselves (Further evidence) covers part of that gap.

Controls and nulls

  • Image permutation ({{fact:x.image-stats.n_perm}} draws): the same fit with the images' statistics shuffled across images. It keeps the statistics' distribution and $\psi_1$, and removes any pairing between them, so it gives the $R^2$ an in-sample fit with {{fact:x.image-stats.rank}} free directions reaches by chance. Its $95$th percentile, pooled over $k = 1..8$, is at most {{fact:x.image-stats.null_max}} over all snapshots.
  • Within-class fit (labels used for validation only): $\psi_1$ and each statistic have their mean over the image's CIFAR class removed before the same fit. CIFAR classes differ in their statistics (on average, one class has bluer images than another), so an eigenfunction that only separates classes would get a positive $R^2$ without following the statistics among images of one class. The within-class $R^2$ removes that route. Its null permutes images within each class; $95$th percentile at most {{fact:x.image-stats.wc_null_max}}.
  • Step $0$ is the untrained network: every value at step $0$ is the reference for what the architecture alone, with random weights, produces.

Answer

At step $0$ the twelve statistics give $R^2$ from {{fact:x.image-stats.step0.lo}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.step0.hi}} for $\psi_1$ at every site; at step {{fact:final_step}} $R^2$ is {{fact:x.image-stats.final.stem}} at stem and decreases from site to site down to {{fact:x.image-stats.final.b8}} at b8, with {{fact:x.image-stats.final.late_lo}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.final.late_hi}} at b9, pool and logits (null at most {{fact:x.image-stats.null_max}}). By step {{fact:x.image-stats.t1k}}, b9, pool and logits were at most {{fact:x.image-stats.t1k.late_hi}} (and stayed so), while b7 and b8 were still at least {{fact:x.image-stats.t1k.b7b8_lo}}; by step {{fact:x.image-stats.t2k}}, b7 and b8 were at most {{fact:x.image-stats.t2k.b7b8_hi}}.

Further evidence

Is what remains at the late sites only class make-up? This changes the answer at the late sites. With class means removed, the fit gives $R^2$ from {{fact:x.image-stats.step0.wc_lo}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.step0.wc_hi}} at step $0$, and at step {{fact:final_step}} {{fact:x.image-stats.wc.final.stem}} at stem but at most {{fact:x.image-stats.wc.final.b7b8_hi}} at b7 and b8 and at most {{fact:x.image-stats.wc.final.late_hi}} at b9, pool and logits (null at most {{fact:x.image-stats.wc_null_max}}). At b9, pool and logits the raw fit is {{fact:x.image-stats.final.late_lo}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.final.late_hi}} and the fit after removing class means is at most {{fact:x.image-stats.wc.final.late_hi}}. The two fits use different responses, predictors and variance denominators, so the difference is a contrast, not a decomposition of the raw $R^2$.

Does the image still decide the grouping, only not through these twelve statistics? This is the strongest alternative to the answer, and the linear fit cannot exclude it. The method run unchanged on the network's input (the pixels as the view) gives its own SEBA sets; agreement of a site's sets with them measures whether the site groups images as the method groups their pixels, with no choice of statistics and no linear fit. At step $0$ the agreement (AMI on the images both assign) is {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.step0.stem}} at stem and {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.step0.b9}} at b9; at step {{fact:final_step}} it is {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.final.stem}} at stem and at most {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.final.late_hi}} at b9, pool and logits (null at most {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.null_max}}; both sets assign a share of at least {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.cov_lo}} of the images). It is lower at step {{fact:final_step}} than at step $0$ at every site, so the drop is not specific to the twelve statistics. Agreement with the same site's sets at step $0$ (the untrained network) ends at {{fact:x.image-stats.untr.final.stem}} at stem and at most {{fact:x.image-stats.untr.final.late_hi}} at b9 to logits; that chart is in Appendix D, and the full source-agreement tables in Appendix B.

Caveats

  • Linear, in-sample fits on twelve chosen statistics. A low $R^2$ does not rule out nonlinear dependence on these statistics or dependence on other image information; the pixel-source agreement tests one nonlinear version (the method's own kNN geometry on the pixels), not all of them.
  • The two measures are not interchangeable. At stem, the agreement with the pixel source falls (from {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.step0.stem}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.pix.final.stem}}) while the $R^2$ of $\psi_1$ stays near its step-$0$ value: the sets use $\psi_1..\psi_{r-1}$ and are compared with sets from a different geometry, not with the statistics.
  • $\psi_1$ alone. The largest $R^2$ over $\psi_1..\psi_8$ taken one at a time is at most {{fact:x.image-stats.maxk.final.late_hi}} at b9 to logits at step {{fact:final_step}} (at b7, {{fact:x.image-stats.maxk.final.b7}}, from $\psi_k$ with $k$ = {{fact:x.image-stats.maxk.final.b7_k}}). Allowing any combination of $\psi_1..\psi_8$, the best fit falls from {{fact:x.image-stats.span.step0.lo}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.span.step0.hi}} at step $0$ to {{fact:x.image-stats.span.final.late_lo}} to {{fact:x.image-stats.span.final.late_hi}} at b9 to logits at step {{fact:final_step}} ({{fact:x.image-stats.span.final.stem}} at stem; null at most {{fact:x.image-stats.span_null_max}}): a direction in the leading span still follows the statistics more than $\psi_1$ does. Both charts are in Appendix D.
  • The eigenfunction compared at two checkpoints is not the same function; the measure is a property of each snapshot's leading direction, not a tracked object.
  • Single-snapshot blocks only, the default kernel rule, one network, one seed, one probe. Per-statistic correlations (which statistic carries the fit) are in Appendix D as heatmaps; they are not part of the answer.

Open checks

  • A nonlinear fit (for example kernel ridge on the statistics, cross-validated) and a richer statistic set, to bound how much image information the late sites' eigenfunctions carry beyond the linear fit. Not run.
  • Held-out $R^2$ (fit on one part of the probe, score on the rest) instead of in-sample $R^2$ against its null. Not run.
  • The harmonicity test: an intervention asking whether boundaries between coherent sets mark sharp changes in the downstream computation, which would say whether the part of the late sites' structure not captured by these pixel statistics matters to the network's output. Not run.
  • The same measures on a second seed and on multi-snapshot blocks. Not run.
