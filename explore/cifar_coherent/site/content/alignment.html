This appendix compares the explorer's single-site operators with each other: different sites of one network, the same site at different checkpoints, and sites of networks of different depth (ResNet-20, ResNet-32 and ResNet-56, two training seeds each). Every network sees the same {{fact:al.n_images}} probe images, so every operator lives on the same node set and two operators can be compared directly, with no matching of images.

Each comparison is a distance between two eigendecompositions. Some distances depend on a cut $k$ (how many eigenfunctions are kept) or a diffusion time $t$, so each figure lets you choose the distance and its $k$ or $t$. The monotone alignment of two networks' sites (dynamic time warping, DTW) is a comparison tool used on these distances; it is not part of the construction.

What was found

  • Sites of networks of different depth can be aligned, and the alignment is as good as between two seeds of one depth. At the end of training the matched distance between ResNet-20 and ResNet-56 is 0.19–0.22 (projector distance, $k=9$), against 0.18 for two seeds of the same depth and 0.30–0.38 for permuted blocks. The $k=9$ cut is not reproducible in most snapshots along the path, so this is checked at a reproducible cut ($k=3$: 0.10–0.14 across depth, 0.10 across seeds, about 0.25–0.32 for permuted blocks) and with the diffusion-kernel distance, which has no cut ($t=16$: 0.10–0.12, 0.09–0.11, 0.20–0.26); the conclusion is the same. The order of sites is found by the distances themselves; DTW's monotonicity is not what produces it. The path goes through the stage ends, but inside a stage it is not proportional to depth.
  • The correspondence is learned. Until step 256 the alignment is no better than permuted blocks; it appears between steps 1,024 and 6,400.
  • Within ResNet-20, sites change gradually with depth, and neighbouring sites are no farther apart than two seeds at the same site; the one large jump is from the last block to the pooled features. Over training each site approaches its final operator monotonically and reaches the seed floor before the first learning-rate drop.
  • The number of eigenmodes changes little about which pairs are close (rank correlation at least 0.83 between any two cuts) but a lot about what is reproducible: cuts beyond $k\approx 5$ are reproducible in about half of the snapshots or fewer, and small cuts separate best from the null.
  • A standard baseline, CKA, gives nearly the same alignment (rank correlation 0.86–0.97 with the spectral distances). What the spectral comparison adds is the mode-level view: which modes two sites share one-to-one, which are only shared as a span, and which images move. The eigenvalues alone do not align the networks.

Two seeds per depth; cross-depth numbers use different-seed pairings. The perplexity-100 rule could not be built for {{fact:al.missing.rule1.snapshots}} early-training ResNet-56 snapshots, which are shown as missing.

What is compared

Networks. ResNet-$(6n+2)$ for $n = 3, 5, 9$, seeds 0 and 1: {{fact:al.n_nets}} networks trained with the same recipe, differing only in the number of blocks per stage. Final test accuracy: ResNet-20 {{fact:al.acc.r20s0.final}} and {{fact:al.acc.r20s1.final}}, ResNet-32 {{fact:al.acc.r32s0.final}} and {{fact:al.acc.r32s1.final}}, ResNet-56 {{fact:al.acc.r56s0.final}} and {{fact:al.acc.r56s1.final}} (seeds 0 and 1).

Sites. The stem, every block output, the pooled features and the logits: {{fact:al.sites.r20s0}}, {{fact:al.sites.r32s0}} and {{fact:al.sites.r56s0}} sites. The residual blocks fall into three stages (16, 32 and 64 channels); the figures mark the stage boundaries.

Checkpoints. The explorer's eight, at steps {{fact:al.steps}}, for every network. A snapshot is one network at one checkpoint and one site: {{fact:al.n_cells}} snapshots in all.

Operator per snapshot. Exactly the explorer's construction (Method): centred cosine Gram, kernel, zero diagonal, doubly stochastic $P$, lazy walk $M = (I + P)/2$, for two kernel rules (rule 0, the union 15-NN graph, which is the default; rule 1, per-image perplexity 100). $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_{64}$ are the eigenfunctions after the constant one, in order of decreasing eigenvalue, $U_k = [\psi_1 \dots \psi_k]$ and $P_k = U_k U_k^\top$.

Distances between two snapshots

For two snapshots $A$ and $B$, all on the same {{fact:al.n_images}} images ($N$):

  • Projector distance $d_k(A,B) = 1 - \|U_k(A)^\top U_k(B)\|_F^2 / k \in [0, 1]$: zero when the two leading-$k$ spans are equal, and $1 - k/(N-1)$ in expectation for two independent random spans (the chance line; {{fact:al.chance.k8}} at $k = 8$, {{fact:al.chance.k64}} at $k = 64$). $k \in \{${{fact:al.k_grid}}$\}$.
  • Diffusion-kernel distance $h_t(A,B) = 1 - \langle A_t, B_t\rangle_F / (\|A_t\|_F \|B_t\|_F)$ with $A_t = M_A^t - \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top/N$, over the whole spectrum: no cut; each mode is weighted by $\lambda^t$. $t \in \{${{fact:al.t_grid}}$\}$.
  • Eigenvalue-only distance $s_k = \|\lambda_{1..k}(A) - \lambda_{1..k}(B)\|_2/\sqrt{k}$. It ignores the eigenvectors: two operators with the same spectrum and unrelated eigenfunctions are at distance zero.
  • CKA distance (baseline) $1 - \mathrm{CKA}$ of the two centred cosine Grams. It is a standard representation-similarity measure, shown for comparison; it is not an output of this construction.

Cut diagnostics. A leading-$k$ span is only well defined when the cut between modes $k$ and $k+1$ separates eigenvalues. For every snapshot the operator was rebuilt on two overlapping two-thirds subsets of the images (no labels used), and $\mathrm{span}_k$ is the smallest $\cos^2$ principal angle between the two rebuilds' leading-$k$ spans on the shared images (as in the explorer's stability check). A cut is unresolved for a pair of snapshots when $\mathrm{span}_k < 0.5$, or is missing, in either snapshot. At an unresolved cut, $d_k$ depends on how the near-equal modes happen to be split; it says how sensitive the comparison is, not how the two sites differ. The stability horizon of a snapshot is the largest $k$ with $\mathrm{span}_k \ge 0.5$.

How unresolved cuts are drawn

In every projector-distance heat map, a cell whose cut is unresolved is hatched and faded; in line charts, an unresolved point is a hollow circle (resolved points are filled). Hover any cell or point for the eigenvalue gap $\lambda_k - \lambda_{k+1}$ and $\mathrm{span}_k$ of both snapshots. The other distances have no cut and are drawn plainly.

Missing snapshots. Under kernel rule 1, {{fact:al.missing.rule1.snapshots}} snapshots have no operator: the per-image perplexity calibration could not reach its target on these collapsed early-training snapshots ({{fact:al.missing.rule1.list}}). They are drawn as missing (white, crossed) and every comparison that needs them is left out of the rule-1 summaries: {{fact:al.missing.rule1.comparisons}} of {{fact:al.missing.rule1.comparisons_of}} cross-network comparisons. {{fact:al.subset_failures_note}}

Replicates and floors. The replicate is the training seed. The seed floor of a site is the distance between that site in the two seeds of the same depth, at the same checkpoint and kernel rule. A difference between sites or between depths is read against it. Comparisons across depth have four seed pairings, shown as individual values, not averaged.

Shared initial draws. Networks of different depth trained with the same seed draw the initial weights of the stem and the first blocks identically (same random generator, same construction order), so at early checkpoints their stems and first blocks are partly the same function. Every cross-depth summary in this appendix (the values in captions, the tables' first lines, the default selections) therefore uses the two different-seed pairings; the two same-seed pairings are still shown, labelled "same seed: shared initial draws".

One site over training

Reading

Default rule, $k=9$, ResNet-20 seed 0. Every site approaches its final operator monotonically: distance to the final checkpoint is 0.71–0.96 at initialization, 0.32–0.53 at step 1,024, 0.18–0.37 at step 2,560 and 0.13–0.21 at step 25,600, which is the level of the seed floor. Does the cut matter?

Reading

The cut matters for what can be trusted more than for what is seen. Across all six networks at the final checkpoint, the cut is reproducible in 86–100% of snapshots for $k\le 4$, 73% at $k=5$, about half for $k=6$–$9$, 23% at $k=10$ and almost never beyond $k=14$. The exception is pool and logits, where $k=8$–$10$ is reproducible (nine class contrasts) while $k=2$–$3$ is not. Distances rise with $k$ for neighbouring sites and for the seed floor alike (ResNet-20 neighbours: 0.01–0.18 at $k=1$, 0.25–0.68 at $k=64$; seed floor 0.04–0.19 and 0.40–0.69), so a larger $k$ adds mostly non-reproducible directions and does not separate sites better.

Do the distances order pairs the same way?

Reading

Within networks at the final checkpoint, the projector distances order pairs of sites almost identically for every $k$ (Spearman $\ge 0.83$ between any two cuts; 0.93 between $k=3$ and $k=32$). The diffusion-kernel distance interpolates between them: $t=256$ agrees with $k=1$ (1.00), $t=16$ with $k=9$ (0.99), $t=4$ with $k=64$ (0.99). The baseline (CKA on the centred-cosine Grams) agrees with all of them at 0.86–0.97. The eigenvalue-only distance is the outlier (0.45–0.57 with the projector distances): the spectrum alone does not carry the ordering.

Across depth

For two networks at one checkpoint and one distance, $D$ is the site × site matrix of distances. Three things are read off it.

  • DTW path. A monotone path from (stem, stem) to (logits, logits) with steps (1,0), (0,1) and (1,1), minimizing the weighted sum of $D$ along it (diagonal steps count twice, the start once). The reported cost is that minimum divided by $L_A + L_B - 1$, the total weight of every such path, so the optimized and the reported quantity agree and no path length is favoured. DTW forces the match to be monotone; on a matrix with no structure every path ties.
  • Unconstrained matches. For each site of $A$ its nearest site of $B$ (row argmin), and the reverse (column argmin). The inversion fraction is the share of site pairs whose matches come in the opposite order: it says whether monotonicity is found in $D$ or only imposed by DTW.
  • Architectural reference. Block $j$ of stage $s$ in $A$ ($n_A$ blocks per stage) is paired with block $\lceil j\,n_B/n_A \rceil$ of stage $s$ in $B$; stem, pool and logits with themselves. Reported: the mean gap in relative depth between the DTW path and this reference (stage coordinate $s - 1 + j/n$, so a match in another stage counts as a large gap), and whether the path passes through the three stage ends.

References. A DTW cost is read against: the permutation null, the optimum recomputed after randomly permuting the residual-block sites of $B$ (stem, pool and logits fixed; {{fact:al.n_perm}} permutations); the constant-matrix reference, the cost of any path on a matrix whose every entry is the mean of $D$; the seed floors, the DTW cost between the two seeds of each depth at the same checkpoint; and the untrained reference, the same comparison at checkpoint 0, so that a correspondence that the architecture produces before any training is not attributed to learning. Same-depth cross-seed pairs, where the expected match is the diagonal, are included as pairs of their own.

Summary for every distance

Reading

Final checkpoint, different-seed pairings, default rule. With the projector distance at $k=9$ the matched cost is 0.19–0.22 across depth, against 0.18 for two seeds of the same depth and 0.30–0.38 for permuted blocks: networks of different depth align about as well as two seeds of one depth. About three quarters of the matched path at $k=9$ crosses a cut that is unresolved in one of the two snapshots; at $k=3$, where cuts are mostly resolved, the costs are 0.10–0.14 across depth and 0.10 across seeds, and with the cut-free diffusion kernel ($t=16$) 0.10–0.12 and 0.09–0.11. Every eigenvector-based distance recovers the stage ends with no inversions; the gap from the architectural reference is 0.07–0.09 stage units for same-depth pairs, about 0.10 for 20 against 32 and 0.20–0.27 for 20 against 56. Separation from the permutation null is best at small cuts (cost/null 0.36–0.48 at $k=3$, 0.53–0.62 at $k=9$, 0.72–0.82 at $k=32$) and for the diffusion kernel at $t=16$ (0.42–0.57); the CKA baseline does as well (0.44–0.58). The eigenvalue-only distance shows inversions (up to 0.25) and misses stage ends; $t=1$ barely separates from the null. At checkpoint 0 no distance beats the null for different-seed pairings.

Along the matched path

Reading

Along the matched path between ResNet-20 (seed 1) and ResNet-56 (seed 0), $d_9$ is 0.19–0.30 through stage 1, 0.20–0.26 in stage 2, 0.12–0.18 in stage 3 and 0.10 at pool and logits. The seed floors at the same sites are 0.10–0.35 (ResNet-20) and 0.09–0.32 (ResNet-56), so there is no depth at which the two networks differ clearly more than two seeds do. Over training

Reading

Different-seed pairings, $k=9$. The matched cost equals the permutation null at steps 0, 64 and 256 (ratio 0.95–1.04), is just below it at step 1,024 (0.91–0.96 for 20 or 32 against 56), and then separates: 0.71–0.82 at step 2,560, 0.64–0.72 at 6,400, 0.59–0.63 at 25,600 and 0.53–0.57 at the end. The gap from the architectural reference falls in step with it (above 0.7 stage units before step 1,024, 0.26–0.32 at 2,560, 0.19–0.27 at the end). The correspondence between depths is learned during training; the architecture alone does not produce it at initialization. Same-seed pairings of different depth share their initial draws for the stem and early blocks and are not a clean reference at early steps (for example cost 0.43 against 0.72 for the two ResNet-20/ResNet-56 pairings at step 0).

Which modes, and which images

The scalar distances say how far apart two snapshots are, not which eigenfunctions are kept, mixed or reordered, or which images move. For {{fact:al.vec_cells}} snapshots (every network at step {{fact:al.final_step}} and ResNet-20 seed 0 at every checkpoint; kernel rule 0) the leading 64 eigenfunctions are shipped (16-bit; $d_k$ recomputed from them differs from the full-precision value by at most {{fact:al.int16_dk_max_error}}), and the figure below computes in your browser:

  • the mode-overlap matrix $G = U_{64}(A)^\top U_{64}(B)$, shown as $G^2$ (the sign of an eigenfunction is arbitrary);
  • the cumulative block overlap $\|G_{[:i,:j]}\|_F^2 / \min(i, j)$, the share of the smaller of the two leading spans captured by the other (on the diagonal, $1 - d_i$);
  • the principal angles between the two leading-$k$ spans, as $\cos^2$ (rotation-invariant);
  • the per-image discrepancy $\delta_i = [(P_k(A) - P_k(B))^2]_{ii}$, which sums to $2k\,d_k$, averaged by class (labels colour and group only) and the images with the largest $\delta_i$.

Entries of $G$ for single modes are meaningful only at isolated, reproducible eigenvalues. Inside a group of near-equal eigenvalues the eigenfunctions can rotate freely among themselves, so $G$ can look mixed while the spans agree. Read the cumulative overlap and the principal angles there; the marks along the axes show where the cuts are unresolved.

Reading

Final checkpoint, ResNet-20 seed 1 b6 against ResNet-56 seed 0 b18 (matched by DTW): modes 1–6 correspond nearly one-to-one (squared overlaps 0.96, 0.92, 0.84, 0.87, 0.79, 0.66), modes 7–9 are mixed among themselves, and the leading nine modes of one network lie 68–96% inside the leading nine of the other, with almost nothing (under 0.1) from modes 10–12; the principal angles give $\cos^2$ 0.97 down to 0.67. Two seeds of ResNet-20 at b6 look the same (0.96 to 0.78). At the logits the nine-dimensional span is shared ($\cos^2$ 0.96–0.79 across depth, the same as across seeds) but single modes are not: only mode 1 keeps its index, because the eigenvalues inside the span are close together. Earlier in the network less is shared (ResNet-20 b2 against ResNet-56 b8: modes 1–4 at 0.57–0.90, then little). The per-image discrepancy is spread over images and classes and not carried by a few images (the 200 most-moved images hold 23–38% of it).

Iso-accuracy (secondary)

Deeper networks reach a given test accuracy at a different step. As a secondary comparison, ResNet-20 at its final checkpoint is also compared with each deeper network at the earliest saved checkpoint whose test accuracy is at least ResNet-20's final accuracy (same seed), accepted only if it overshoots that target by at most 0.5 points. This choice is conditioned on an outcome and uses test labels: it is a validation analysis, not part of the label-free construction, and the iso-step comparisons above are primary.

Where a network's checkpoint is not accepted, its iso-accuracy comparison is not made: the tolerance was fixed before the run and is not relaxed afterwards. With a single accepted deeper network there is no seed floor at iso-accuracy for that depth (it would need the other seed at its own accepted checkpoint).

Definitions used in this appendix

Floors, summaries and pair sets
  • Seed floor of one side of a comparison: the same-depth cross-seed comparison at the same checkpoint. For a deeper network at its iso-accuracy checkpoint, the other seed at that seed's iso-accuracy checkpoint; for ResNet-20 in an iso-accuracy comparison, the other seed at the final checkpoint.
  • Settled (values under the training figure): the earliest checkpoint from which on the distance to the same site at the final checkpoint stays at or below that site's seed floor at the final checkpoint. For $d_k$ it is given only when the cut is resolved in the floor and at every checkpoint from then on; otherwise the step of the latest unresolved cut is named.
  • Floor along the path (C3): the mean of $A$'s floor at site $i$ and $B$'s floor at site $j$.
  • Below the null from (C4 values): the earliest checkpoint from which on the DTW cost is below the 5th percentile of the permutation null in every seed pairing.
  • Pair sets of the rank correlations: as in the caption of the rank-correlation figure.
  • Data shipped to this page: of the all-pairs distance tensors, only the blocks the figures draw (each network's site × site matrices at each checkpoint, each site's checkpoint × checkpoint matrix, and the site × site matrices of every pair of networks at each checkpoint and at iso-accuracy), 16-bit, fetched per distance; the eigenfunctions are fetched per snapshot. {{fact:al.site_mb}} MB in all, of which {{fact:al.site_vectors_mb}} MB are eigenfunctions. Probe images: {{fact:al.probe_match}}.
