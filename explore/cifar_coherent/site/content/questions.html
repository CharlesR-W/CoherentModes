Every plot except the explorer itself, grouped by the question it answers. Each section says how to read the figure and then what it shows on this one network. Class labels, losses and gradients appear here only as checks on the label-free sets; none of them enters the construction.

All set comparisons use one number, the matched Jaccard score. For two collections of hard sets, compute the Jaccard overlap $|A \cap B| / |A \cup B|$ of every pair, match sets one-to-one to maximise the total overlap, and average over the matched pairs. 1 means identical sets; the grey band is what random sets of the same sizes give (200 shuffles, 2.5 to 97.5 percentiles). Unless a control says otherwise: the default kernel rule (15-nearest-neighbour graph) and $r = 4$ SEBA sets.

Do through-training groups share a training signal?

For each image I have its loss gradient with respect to every parameter. If a group of images is one "thing" for the network, one might expect their gradients to point in similar directions: training on one of them moves the others. This uses labels (through the loss), so it relates the label-free sets to the training signal; it is not part of how the sets are found.
