Every number in the experiments comes from one construction with a handful of choices: which kernel turns activations into a walk, whether distances use the cosine or the full dot product, whether a block averages its snapshots' walks or composes them, how many eigenfunctions SEBA rotates ($r$) and how many an operator comparison keeps ($k$). This appendix records each choice, the label-free diagnostic it was made on, and how much the output moves when the choice changes. Labels appear only as validation columns, computed after the choice.

Cosine or dot product (Euclidean)

Every rule above is a function of the cosine distance $d_{ij} = 1 - \cos_{ij}$, which on unit-norm activations equals $\lVert \hat x_i - \hat x_j \rVert^2/2$: the construction normalises each image's activation vector and so discards its norm. The dot (Euclidean) variant keeps the norm. It replaces $d_{ij}$ by the half squared Euclidean distance induced by the same dot product on the unnormalised, flattened activations, $D_{ij} = (G_{ii} + G_{jj})/2 - G_{ij} = \lVert x_i - x_j \rVert^2/2$ with $G = XX^\top$, divided by one global scale per snapshot (the mean of $G_{ii}$ over the probe after centring). All five rules are invariant to that scale, and on unit rows $D$ is exactly $1 - \cos$, so the cosine construction is the special case. Everything else is unchanged: probe, checkpoints, sites, rules, scaling, $M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, SEBA, products (SPEC_DOT.md; data tag r20s0d).

Two identities limit what "dot product" can mean here. For the global-$\kappa$ rules (3 and 4), $\exp(\kappa G_{ij})$ and $\exp(-\kappa D_{ij})$ differ by a factor $a_i a_j$ with $a_i = \exp(\kappa G_{ii}/2)$, which the symmetric scaling removes, so an exponentiated dot product is not a separate rule. For the rank-based rules (0, 1, 2), ranking neighbours by the raw dot product $G_{ij}$ (maximum inner product) does not come from a metric; it was run once as a diagnostic, below.

How the two are compared. Agreement of two operators is the mean $\cos^2$ of the principal angles between their leading non-trivial spans ($r$ eigenvectors, constant excluded; a random span gives about $r/N$, with $N$ = {{fact:x.app-choices.n_images}}). Label-free diagnostics beside it: the largest 15-NN in-degree (how many images list one image among their 15 nearest), the 15-NN overlap between the two distances, and the participation number of the leading eigenvectors. Validation only: agreement of each span with the nine-dimensional span of the class indicators. Leading modes were recomputed in float64 from the saved operators (data/kernels/compare_single.json, compare_blocks.json, compare_ggn.json); every comparison stores the eigenvalue gap at its cutoff, since a near-tie there makes a fixed-rank span unstable.

Average versus product

A block's default operator is the average $\bar M = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} M_c$ of its snapshots' walks. It ignores the order of the snapshots. The alternative is to compose them.

The project's theory builds on a product, not an average. For a layer block $a < b$, the chronological product $Q = M_b \cdots M_a$ (functions are column vectors; layer $a$ applied first) is the nearest cross-layer object: its right singular vectors are functions at layer $a$, its left singular vectors their normalised images at layer $b$ (Froyland's coherent pairs). The title's "cross-Hankel" is an analogy to this: for a time series $y$, a Hankel matrix $H_{ij} = y_{i+j}$ has windows of the past as rows and of the future as columns, and for linear systems its leading singular vectors give a small state through which the past influences the future; $Q$ plays that role between layer $a$ and layer $b$.

To first order in the step size the two agree: if $M_c = I - \varepsilon L_c$, then $Q^\top Q = I - 2\varepsilon \sum_c L_c + O(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\bar M = I - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{|B|}\sum_c L_c$, so they share leading vectors up to $O(\varepsilon)$ when the relevant eigenvalues of $\sum_c L_c$ are simple. Beyond first order they differ: $(\tfrac12(M_a + M_b))^2$ contains $\tfrac14(M_aM_b + M_bM_a)$, while $Q^\top Q$ does not average.

avgprod.json reports, for rules 0 and 1, with the constant projected out of both sides, the mean $\cos^2$ of principal angles between $\mathrm{span}(\psi_1..\psi_r)$ of $\bar M$ and the top-$r$ non-trivial right and left singular vectors of $Q$ and of the two-end product $M_b M_a$, $r \in \{3, 6\}$. At step {{fact:final_step}}, $r = 3$, over the {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.n_pairs}} layer pairs, median (lowest): {{fact:mode_name.rule0}}, {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule0.r3.right}} for the right and {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule0.r3.left}} for the left singular vectors of $Q$, and {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule0.r3.end_right}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule0.r3.end_left}} for the two-end product; {{fact:mode_name.rule1}}, {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule1.r3.right}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule1.r3.left}}, and {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule1.r3.end_right}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.avgprod.rule1.r3.end_left}}. Product blocks in the explorer

The explorer's Operator switch replaces $\bar M$ by the ordered product, for every layer and time block and every kernel rule. With $C_c = M_c - \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/N$ (the constant projected out; each $M_c$ is symmetric and doubly stochastic, so $C_c = M_c(I - \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/N)$), the block operator is $Q = C_b \cdots C_a$, first snapshot applied first, with singular value decomposition $Q = \sum_k \sigma_k u_k v_k^\top$. Product · start shows the right singular vectors $v_k$: the functions on the probe images that keep the largest share $\sigma_k = |Q v_k|$ of their norm after being smoothed by every snapshot of the block in turn. Product · end shows $u_k = Q v_k / \sigma_k$, what they have become after the last snapshot. Both are exported in exactly the average's format ($\sigma_0 = 1$ for the constant, SEBA on $v_0..v_{r-1}$ or $u_0..u_{r-1}$), from a float64 eigensolve of $Q^\top Q$ or $QQ^\top$.

Two properties differ from the average. The product is multiplicative: a function's survival is the product of its step gains $g_c = |C_c w_{c-1}| / |w_{c-1}|$ ($w$ the function pushed through the block so far, from first to last snapshot for $v$, from last to first for $u$), so one snapshot that erases a split sinks it, where the average only dilutes it. The explorer's retention strip shows these gains (solid) next to $\rho_c$ (dashed); for $\psi_k$ they multiply to $\sigma_k$ (largest absolute difference ${{fact:x.app-choices.prod.gain_check}}$ over {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.gain_check_n}} exported block–rule pairs). The product is order-sensitive, but only up to reversal: each $M_c$ is symmetric, so reversing the snapshots transposes $Q$, which keeps the singular values and swaps start and end. The average ignores order entirely. No order-specific test (for example an ordered against a reversed chain on data that are not symmetric) has been run, so this is a comparison of constructions, not an experiment.

Measured at step {{fact:final_step}}, {{fact:mode_name.rule0}}, $r = 4$: matched Jaccard between the average's and the product's hard sets on the same block, mean over the layer blocks of each length, {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len2.sets_start}} (start) and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len2.sets_end}} (end) for two sites, {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len4.sets_start}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len4.sets_end}} for four, {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len6.sets_start}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len6.sets_end}} for six, {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len8.sets_start}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len8.sets_end}} for eight, {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len10.sets_start}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.len10.sets_end}} for ten, and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.full.sets_start}} and {{fact:x.app-choices.prod.full.sets_end}} for the full stem–logits block. The SEBA rank $r$

SEBA rotates the leading $r$ eigenvectors (constant included) into $r$ sparse vectors and thresholds them into at most $r$ hard sets. Every block is exported at $r \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}$, and the explorer and the experiments use $r = 4$ unless they say otherwise. $r$ is a resolution, not a number the spectrum supplies: a choice of $r$ would be read off the spectrum only if one gap $\lambda_{r-1} - \lambda_r$ stood out, and the chart below shows the gaps at every exported resolution. Over the {{fact:x.app-choices.gaps.n_rows}} operators in it, the largest gap falls at {{fact:x.app-choices.gaps.argmax_counts}} operators. How the sets at one $r$ relate to those at another (whether finer sets refine coarser ones) is measured by the rank-nesting entropy (Appendix C) and shown for the end-of-training sets in that experiment.

Layouts (display only)

  • Per checkpoint: umap.AlignedUMAP across the twelve sites (identity relations between consecutive sites), n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.1, alignment_regularisation=0.02, random_state=0, on the top $50$ principal components ($u_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}$ from the eigendecomposition of $C$) of each snapshot.
  • Across checkpoints, per site: centre, scale to unit RMS, orthogonal Procrustes (reflections allowed) onto the previous checkpoint, then rescale to the $0.5$ and $99.5$ percentiles for drawing. The within-checkpoint alignment is part of the UMAP objective and shapes the layouts; across checkpoints only rotation, reflection, centring and scale change.
  • No quantity is computed from the two-dimensional coordinates. Distances in a layout are not distances in the operator.
  • Other seed ("layout from"). A second ResNet-20, identical recipe, training seed 1; only its activations' top $50$ PCs and its own AlignedUMAP and Procrustes layouts are computed, for the same probe images and steps. Colours always come from seed 0's blocks. Seed 1's layouts are aligned along its own checkpoints, not onto seed 0's.
  • Spectral layout. For each snapshot, the diffusion coordinates $\lambda_k \psi_k$, $k = 1..32$, of that snapshot's own lazy walk $M_c$ under the {{fact:mode_name.rule0}} rule (diffusion map at $t = 1$; constant excluded; the same single-snapshot eigenfunctions the explorer shows for $a = b$, extended to $32$), then the same AlignedUMAP and Procrustes steps as above. It is fixed per snapshot and does not change with the selected block or kernel rule.
  • Under the dot kernel, the PCA layout uses the top $50$ PCs of the centred dot Gram and is rotated onto the cosine layout for display; the other-seed layout is not available.

Variants of the construction

The transfer-operator pilot

A pilot of one more variant, labelled as such. For two snapshots $a$ and $b$ of the same images, the transfer operator $(Tf)(y) = \mathbb E[f(X) \mid Y = y]$ maps a function of the images at $a$ to its best prediction at $b$; its singular functions are the pairs of source and destination observables with the largest correlation. Because each image is one exact pair, an unrestricted estimate reproduces the pairing itself, so every estimate here uses a declared finite function space and is judged on held-out images. Estimators: linear CCA on cosine PCA scores, a soft Ulam joint table on landmarks, and whitened cross-covariance on Nyström or diffusion-coordinate features (the ridge terms are fits; definitions in Appendix C). {{fact:bn.tr_pairs}} pairs were fixed in advance; each of {{fact:bn.tr_splits}} splits uses {{fact:bn.tr_split_sizes}} images (fit / tuning / test). The control permutes the pairing inside each split and repeats the whole procedure, tuning included ({{fact:bn.tr_perm}} permutations).

More views: synthetic check, supported functions per pair, the sample-size curve on the full test set and the composition test

The pilot also stores SEBA sets built from the transfer functions where their held-out stability passed a threshold. They are exploratory summaries computed on all images after fitting (the held-out correlations evaluate the fitted function pairs on held-out images, not the partitions), and they are not shown on this page or in the explorer.
