This appendix puts the method's numbers next to the same numbers from simpler methods run on the same blocks, and from null data that keep some properties of the activations and destroy others. Each chart shows a reference drawn from one of these, so a reader can see whether a spectrum, a set of sets or an agreement score of the kind shown in the Explorer also appears without the structure in question. The charts describe; they do not grade. In the explorer, the Sets from control paints any of these sources on every panel.

How to read a null band

A null is a way of generating data, or labels, that keeps some properties of the real thing and destroys others. Running the method on many draws of a null gives a spread of values; the band shows that spread (here usually the 5th to 95th percentile of the draws). A real value inside the band is one that this generator's recorded draws covered; a value outside it lies outside the central 90% of the recorded draws. No null here is the null: each one answers a different question, so every chart names the null it shows and what that null keeps.

Agreement scores come with a permutation value: the score when one labelling is shuffled across the images with its set sizes kept ({{fact:bn.n_perm}} shuffles for the stored comparisons, {{fact:bn.n_perm_site}} for the few computed by the site build). That 95th percentile is for one block and one $r$. If you scan many blocks and pick the largest score, compare it with the scanned-blocks reference instead: shuffle the labels in every block scanned, take the largest score over those blocks, repeat, and read the 95th percentile of that largest score. It comes from shuffled labels, not from the observed scores.

A worked example: reading one agreement score

Open the explorer on the layer block b4 to b6 at step {{fact:final_step}}, $r = 4$, and choose Sets from: cosine PCA + SEBA (this view). The side card reads: AMI on jointly assigned images {{fact:bn.ex.ami}}, coverage {{fact:bn.ex.cov}}, permutation 95th percentile {{fact:bn.ex.q95}}, scanned-blocks reference {{fact:bn.ex.max95}}. In words: both methods put {{fact:bn.ex.cov}} of the images in some set; on those images, how much the two partitions coincide is measured as {{fact:bn.ex.ami}} on a scale where 0 is what shuffled labels give and 1 is the same partition. Shuffling the labels of this one block gives values up to {{fact:bn.ex.q95}} in 95% of shuffles, and the largest over all {{fact:bn.ex.nblocks}} layer blocks at that step, under shuffling, stays below {{fact:bn.ex.max95}} in 95% of shuffles. Matched Jaccard: {{fact:bn.ex.mj}}, against a permutation mean of {{fact:bn.ex.mj_null}} (it is not near 0 under shuffling, because it depends on the set sizes). None of these numbers says which of the two partitions is preferable; they say how far they coincide.

The factors and their levels

A the graph (union kNN with $k$ = 5, 15, 50, 150; mutual 15-NN; per-image perplexity 30, 100, 300; a locally scaled kernel; an $\varepsilon$-ball at the default's mean degree; four global temperatures), each also at a matched median effective neighbour count $1/\sum_j P_{ij}^2$; B the floor added before balancing; C the normalisation (Sinkhorn against $D^{-1/2}K^{(\alpha)}D^{-1/2}$ and the raw adjacency scaled by its norm); D the diagonal (kept kernel diagonal; products of non-lazy walks with laziness $\gamma$); E how a block combines its snapshots (mean of $M$, mean of $M^2$, ordered product, two-end product, products of heat kernels, one graph on the concatenated view, union and intersection graphs); F the weights of a time block's checkpoints; G the SEBA threshold; H the view (uncentred cosine; spatially averaged features); I the number of images.

(f) Linear content and CKA

Two linear summaries sit next to the method. Variance capture asks how much of the block's cosine Gram the method's span reproduces, against the best any span of that dimension can do (the principal directions). The Dirichlet energy asks the reverse: how smooth the principal directions are under the snapshots' walks, against the method's eigenfunctions. CKA (centred kernel alignment) compares the snapshots' Gram matrices with each other; here it is shown next to the overlap of their leading eigenfunctions and the agreement of their sets.

(g) Planted benchmarks

Synthetic data with a known answer, run through the production code path (centring, row normalisation, the same graph and operators), {{fact:bn.bench_units}} runs: 10 seeds, $N$ = 500, 1,000 and 2,000, each benchmark swept along one difficulty axis. Each cell (the synthetic analogue of a snapshot) is embedded by its own random isometry, which changes no dot product. The oracle knows the generative form: it projects each preprocessed cell onto the planted subspace and classifies there with a held-out kNN vote (chance-normalised accuracy $\kappa$) or regresses ($R^2$), so it shows how much of the planted information survives the preprocessing. Truth and oracle are validation (V); they feed no kernel, basis, rank or selection.

(h) The transfer-operator pilot

A pilot of one more variant, labelled as such. For two snapshots $a$ and $b$ of the same images, the transfer operator $(Tf)(y) = \mathbb E[f(X) \mid Y = y]$ maps a function of the images at $a$ to its best prediction at $b$; its singular functions are the pairs of source and destination observables with the largest correlation. Because each image is one exact pair, an unrestricted estimate reproduces the pairing itself, so every estimate here uses a declared finite function space and is judged on held-out images. Estimators: linear CCA on cosine PCA scores, a soft Ulam joint table on landmarks, and whitened cross-covariance on Nyström or diffusion-coordinate features (the ridge terms are fits). Twelve pairs were fixed in advance; each of {{fact:bn.tr_splits}} splits uses {{fact:bn.tr_split_sizes}} images (fit / tuning / test). The control permutes the pairing inside each split and repeats the whole procedure, tuning included ({{fact:bn.tr_perm}} permutations).

More views: synthetic check, supported functions per pair, the 10,000-image sample-size curve and the composition test

The pilot also stores SEBA sets built from the transfer functions where their held-out stability passed a threshold. They are exploratory summaries computed on all images after fitting (the held-out correlations evaluate the fitted function pairs on held-out images, not the partitions), and they are not shown on this page or in the explorer.

Stored by the baselines study (explore/cifar_baselines/; schema in SCHEMA.md): the sources' labels and their agreement rows on every explorer block, the null draws, stability, linear content and ablations of the representative blocks, the random-subspace SEBA runs, the planted-benchmark summaries (NOTES_B.md) and the transfer pilot (TRANSFER.md). Computed by the site build with the study's own functions: the predicted-class agreement rows, the agreement of the stored null draws with the method, the source-by-source matrices and the snapshot-by-snapshot set agreement ({{fact:bn.n_perm_site}} permutations each).
