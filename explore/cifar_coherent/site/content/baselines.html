The experiments read numbers produced by one construction. This appendix asks whether those numbers mean what they are taken to mean: it puts the method next to simpler methods run on the same blocks and next to null data that keep some properties of the activations and destroy others, and it checks how far the method's output moves with the sample, the starting point, the rank, the machine and the choice of distance. Each section opens with the question it checks about the method. The charts describe; they do not grade. In the Explorer, the Sets from control paints any of these sources on every panel.

Everything here uses kernel rule {{fact:mode_name.rule0}} and the average operator $\bar M$ (the default construction), on the {{fact:n_images}} probe images. "The method" means SEBA sets from $\psi_0..\psi_{r-1}$ of $\bar M$, for $r \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}$ (definitions: Appendix C). The sources were computed on all {{fact:bn.n_grid_blocks}} explorer blocks; the nulls with many draws and the stability checks on {{fact:bn.n_reps}} representative blocks fixed before any class outcome was looked at.

Reading a null

What it checks. Every number below is read against a reference. Without one, a spectrum or an agreement score cannot be told apart from what the same procedure returns on data that lack the structure in question; this section says how the references are built and how to read them.

A null is a way of generating data, or labels, that keeps some properties of the real thing and destroys others. Running the method on many draws of a null gives a spread of values; the band shows that spread (here the 5th to 95th percentile of the draws unless a caption says otherwise). A real value inside the band is one that this generator's recorded draws covered; a value outside it lies outside the central 90% of the recorded draws. No null here is the null: each one answers a different question, so every chart names the null it shows and what that null keeps.

Agreement scores come with a permutation value: the score when one labelling is shuffled across the images with its set sizes kept ({{fact:bn.n_perm}} shuffles for the stored comparisons, {{fact:bn.n_perm_site}} for the few computed by the site build). That 95th percentile is for one block and one $r$. If you scan many blocks and pick the largest score, compare it with the scanned-blocks reference instead: shuffle the labels in every block scanned, take the largest score over those blocks, repeat, and read the 95th percentile of that largest score. It comes from shuffled labels, not from the observed scores.

A worked example: reading one agreement score

Open the explorer on the layer block b4 to b6 at step {{fact:final_step}}, $r = 4$, and choose Sets from: cosine PCA + SEBA (this view). The side card reads: AMI on jointly assigned images {{fact:bn.ex.ami}}, coverage {{fact:bn.ex.cov}}, permutation 95th percentile {{fact:bn.ex.q95}}, scanned-blocks reference {{fact:bn.ex.max95}}. In words: both methods put {{fact:bn.ex.cov}} of the images in some set; on those images, how much the two partitions coincide is measured as {{fact:bn.ex.ami}} on a scale where $0$ is what shuffled labels give and $1$ is the same partition. Shuffling the labels of this one block gives values up to {{fact:bn.ex.q95}} in 95% of shuffles, and the largest over all {{fact:bn.ex.nblocks}} layer blocks at that step, under shuffling, stays below {{fact:bn.ex.max95}} in 95% of shuffles. Matched Jaccard: {{fact:bn.ex.mj}}, against a permutation mean of {{fact:bn.ex.mj_null}} (it is not near $0$ under shuffling, because it depends on the set sizes). None of these numbers says which of the two partitions is preferable; they say how far they coincide.

The sources and the nulls

What it checks. Which simpler procedures and which kinds of null data the method is compared with, and what each comparison can rule out. A source that returns the method's sets without its construction would make the construction unnecessary for those sets; a null that returns them would make them a property of what that null keeps.

Status tags, as on the explorer's control: S a label-free spectral object built from dot products; P post-processing, a diagnostic or a null generator; B a fitted baseline outside the construction (k-means fits); V uses the CIFAR class labels (validation, or a null whose strata are classes). Each source gives one hard label per probe image; unless stated, it has $r$ sets.

Spectra against null bands

What it checks. Whether the block operator's leading eigenvalues differ from those of data that keep part of the activations' structure and destroy the rest. If a null gave the same leading eigenvalues, the leading modes could come from what that null keeps (for the trajectory shuffle, each snapshot's own walk; for the Gaussian, each snapshot's covariance spectrum) rather than from the images being the same images across the block.

Class agreement under the trajectory shuffle is not a zero reference: the shuffle leaves the block's first snapshot in place, so sets that follow that snapshot keep its class structure. Its band is in the table under this chart.

What forced extraction returns

What it checks. Whether SEBA returns sets even when there is nothing to find. SEBA and k-means declare $r$ set slots whatever they are given; some slots may stay empty, and SEBA can leave images unassigned. If SEBA on a random subspace gave the same unassigned share and set sizes as on the method's eigenvectors, those properties of the sets would come from the rounding step, not from the operator. The second check asks how much SEBA's answer moves with its starting rotation.

Stability

What it checks. Whether the sets would change with a different sample of images, on images the construction has not seen, with the rank $r$, or with one snapshot of the block left out. A grouping that moves with the sample cannot be read as a property of the network; one that survives on held-out images is not an artefact of fitting the probe.

More views: subsample stability of every single snapshot, and the check on the full CIFAR-10 test set

Linear content and CKA

What it checks. Whether the method is principal components, or a standard similarity index, in another form. If the method's span reproduced the block's cosine Gram as well as the principal directions do, and the principal directions were as smooth under the walks as the method's eigenfunctions, the graph construction would add nothing to PCA; if CKA between snapshots tracked the overlap of their eigenfunctions and the agreement of their sets, it would add nothing to CKA.

Variance capture asks how much of the block's cosine Gram the method's span reproduces, against the best any span of that dimension can do (the principal directions). The Dirichlet energy asks the reverse: how smooth the principal directions are under the snapshots' walks, against the method's eigenfunctions. CKA (centred kernel alignment) compares the snapshots' Gram matrices with each other; here it is shown next to the overlap of their leading eigenfunctions and the agreement of their sets.

Planted benchmarks

What it checks. Whether the method recovers structure where the answer is known, returns nothing where nothing is planted, and fails in the ways one would predict where the planted structure is not what it is built to find. On real activations there is no ground truth; synthetic data with a planted answer are the only place the method can be scored directly.

The synthetic data run through the production code path (centring, row normalisation, the same graph and operators), {{fact:bn.bench_units}} runs: $10$ seeds, $N \in \{500, 1000, 2000\}$, each benchmark swept along one difficulty axis. Each cell (the synthetic analogue of a snapshot) is embedded by its own random isometry, which changes no dot product. The oracle knows the generative form: it projects each preprocessed cell onto the planted subspace and classifies there with a held-out kNN vote (chance-normalised accuracy $\kappa$) or regresses ($R^2$), so it shows how much of the planted information survives the preprocessing. Truth and oracle are validation (V); they feed no kernel, basis, rank or selection.

What it checks. The experiments that compare operators (across depth, over training, across networks) read one distance at one cut $k$.
