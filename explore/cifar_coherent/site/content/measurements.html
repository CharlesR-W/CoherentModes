Matched Jaccard

For two hard labelings $A$ ($r_A$ sets) and $B$ ($r_B$ sets), with unassigned images in no set: $J_{ij} = |A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$; a Hungarian matching of $\min(r_A, r_B)$ pairs maximises the total $J$; the score is the mean matched $J$, in $[0, 1]$. Null: the label vector of $A$, unassigned entries included, is permuted uniformly (set sizes and unassigned count kept); 200 permutations from one seeded bank; bands are the 2.5 to 97.5 percentiles, $\mu_0$ is the null mean. Size ceiling (against a fixed reference labeling): the largest score any sets of $A$'s sizes could reach. Normalised excess $= (\text{score} - \mu_0) / (\text{ceiling} - \mu_0)$. Full definitions: Method.

Image statistics against eigenfunctions and SEBA vectors

Twelve statistics of each probe image's pixels (channel means, luminance $Y$, RMS contrast, saturation, border-minus-centre differences, high-frequency share; definitions in Method) are correlated over the {{fact:n_images}} images with $\psi_1..\psi_8$ and with the SEBA vectors for $r = 2, 3, 4$ of each block. Nulls permute the images (1,000 permutations, one bank shared by all blocks); within-class nulls permute images within each class. Thresholds: pointwise two-sided 5% level, median over single-snapshot blocks, {{fact:q.img.thr_point_median}}; familywise 5% level over a block's 17 × 12 correlations, median over single-snapshot blocks, {{fact:q.img.thr_family_median}} (within-class: {{fact:q.img.thr_family_wc_median}}); familywise over every block and entry, {{fact:q.img.thr_global}}.

Per-image loss gradients within and between sets

For each probe image and checkpoint, $g_i$ is the gradient of its loss with respect to every parameter. For a labeling, the within-set mean is the mean of $\cos(g_i, g_j)$ over ordered pairs $i \ne j$ in the same set, the between-set mean the same over pairs in different sets (unassigned images excluded); the plotted quantity is within minus between. The loss uses the labels, so this compares the sets with the training signal; it is not part of how the sets are found. Definition: Method.
