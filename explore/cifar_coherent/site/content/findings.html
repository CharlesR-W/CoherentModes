These are readings of one network (ResNet-20, CIFAR-10, one seed) made with the procedure in the Overview. They are also worked examples of how to think with the tool. Each finding opens with a claim and a tag: observed is a direct reading of a measured quantity, reading is an interpretation I would defend, and speculation is a guess worth checking. Then come the evidence, with captions that only say what is plotted; how I read it, and what else it could be; and what to change in the explorer to test it yourself. Class names appear here, in the prose, to describe sets that the method found without them.

1. Four sets at the end of training, and what is in them

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 4$) of the layer block b7 to pool (four snapshots) at the last checkpoint, step {{fact:final_step}}, kernel rule {{fact:default_mode}}.

Claim (observed). At the last checkpoint, the layer block b7 to pool splits the probe into four coherent sets of {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set1.size}}, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set2.size}}, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set3.size}} and {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set4.size}} images, with {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.n_unassigned}} unassigned, and every set's SEBA vector keeps a retention between {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.retention_min}} and {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.retention_max}} in each of the block's four snapshots. Here they are, from the top of each set.

What is in them (reading). Counting classes (200 images each), set 1 holds {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set1.top_classes}}; set 2, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set2.top_classes}}; set 3, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set3.top_classes}}; set 4, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set4.top_classes}}. So at the end of training the late layers group the ten classes into four unions: small animals, road vehicles, the vehicles of sky and water, and horses with the largest share of the deer. Every class but deer has most of its images in a single set. The matched Jaccard of the sets against the ten classes is {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_classes}}, against {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_classes_ceiling}} for the best that four sets of these sizes could do: it nearly reaches the size ceiling of the matched Jaccard. The class counts below are the stronger evidence. That is the network's own coarse ontology at this depth, found without labels, and it is a plausible one; a person sorting these pictures into four piles might well do the same.

What else it could be
  • Four is my choice. The gaps where $r = 2, 3, 4$ cut the spectrum are {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r2.gap_after_lambda1}}, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r3.gap_after_lambda2}} and {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.gap_after_lambda3}}; none dominates. At $r = 3$, horse and deer join the small animals (that set's largest classes: {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r3.set1.top_classes}}), so set 4 is the least canonical of the four.
  • The readout seen from below. The block includes pool, which is one linear map away from the logits, so the sets could be the classifier's geometry propagated back. Against this, the sets agree more with b9's own late-training sets (time block steps 2,560 to {{fact:final_step}}: matched Jaccard {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_b9_time_late}}) than with b9 alone, its single-snapshot block ({{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_b9_alone}}).
  • The kernel rule. The same block under perplexity 100, perplexity 300 and kNN scale gives sets matching these at {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_rule1}}, {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_rule2}} and {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_rule3}}; half-mass, the rule that fails in the middle layers (Method), gives {{fact:q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.vs_rule4}}.

Try it. Open this block in the explorer. Switch the colouring to class to see the make-up on the layouts; set $r$ to 3, then 6, and watch which sets merge or split; move the block's start from b7 down to b4.

2. Looking back: when the two-set split becomes vehicles against animals

What we are looking at

The two coherent sets ($r = 2$) of the single-snapshot block at site b9, recomputed at each checkpoint in turn. The screenshot shows step 64; change the checkpoint in the explorer to see the others.

Now fix one site, b9, and ask what its own two-set split (the single-snapshot block, $r = 2$) looks like at each checkpoint. I score it against the class grouping {airplane, automobile, ship, truck} versus the other six, with the matched Jaccard, its permutation null, and the ceiling that the set sizes allow (Measurements).

Claim (observed). By step {{fact:q.va.b9.half_step}}, when validation accuracy is {{fact:q.train.step64.val_acc}}, b9's two-set split is already the best match to vehicles against animals among all 511 ways of splitting the ten classes in two, and it has half of the alignment its sizes allow. By step {{fact:q.va.b9.most_step}} it has 80% of its final excess over the null and keeps it, ending at {{fact:q.va.b9.final.score}} against a ceiling of {{fact:q.va.b9.final.ceiling}}. Over the same stretch, airplanes leave the vehicle set.

Step through the slider:

  • Step 0. The split beats its null (score {{fact:q.va.b9.step0.score}} against a null maximum of {{fact:q.va.b9.step0.null_max}}), but vehicles against animals ranks only {{fact:q.va.b9.step0.va_rank}} of 511, and a split of the same sizes by a single pixel statistic does better ({{fact:q.va.b9.step0.pixel_best}}, by mean blue). In this probe, vehicles are {{fact:q.img.vehicle_contrast.mean_b}} standard deviations bluer than animals, and a linear fit on twelve pixel statistics explains $R^2$ = {{fact:q.img.step0.b9.r2_psi1}} of b9's $\psi_1$ at step 0. Beating the null happens at step {{fact:q.va.onset_step.all_sites}} at every site, so it cannot date anything; I use the effect size instead.
  • Step 64. Vehicles against animals is the best-matching class split (rank {{fact:q.va.b9.step64.va_rank}}); normalised excess {{fact:q.va.b9.step64.norm_excess}}, and it stays above one half from here on; {{fact:q.va.b9.step64.airplane_in_vehicle_set}} of the airplanes are in the vehicle set.
  • Step 1,024. Score {{fact:q.va.b9.step1024.score}}, with agreement {{fact:q.va.b9.step1024.agree}} on the assigned images. Airplanes in the vehicle set: {{fact:q.va.b9.step1024.airplane_in_vehicle_set}}.
  • Later. Airplanes in the vehicle set fall to {{fact:q.va.b9.step6400.airplane_in_vehicle_set}} at step 6,400 and {{fact:q.va.b9.step64000.airplane_in_vehicle_set}} at the end, with {{fact:q.va.b9.step64000.airplane_unassigned}} unassigned, while every other class has at least {{fact:q.va.b9.step64000.min_class_in_matched_excl_airplane}} of its images in its own side's set.

Across depth (observed). b7 to logits pass one half at step {{fact:q.va.b7.half_step}} and stay there, b6 at step {{fact:q.va.b6.half_step}}, b3 to b5 at step {{fact:q.va.b3.half_step}}, and the stem, b1 and b2 never do. Yet at the end, vehicles against animals is the best-matching class split even at the stem (rank {{fact:q.va.stem.final.va_rank}}; score {{fact:q.va.stem.final.score}}, agreement {{fact:q.va.stem.final.agree}} on the assigned images).

How I read it (reading). The deep layers draw a vehicle-like split from step {{fact:q.va.b7.half_step}}, well before the network is any good at the task, and it is mostly in place by step {{fact:q.va.b9.most_step}}. The early layers lean the same way but stay weak. The precise end-of-training split at b9 is animals against automobile, truck and ship, with airplanes left in between. My guess (speculation): airplanes start out grouped with the other vehicles by colour (sky and water are blue), and as the network learns what an airplane is, it stops treating airplanes like cars, while still pairing them with ships when given four sets (finding 1).

What else it could be
  • Colour or background carries the early split. The single-statistic baseline beats b9's split at step 0 ({{fact:q.va.b9.step0.pixel_best}} against {{fact:q.va.b9.step0.score}}) but not from step {{fact:q.va.b9.above_pixel_from_step}} on ({{fact:q.va.b9.step64.pixel_best}} against {{fact:q.va.b9.step64.score}} at step 64; it reaches {{fact:q.va.b9.final.pixel_best}} at the end, against {{fact:q.va.b9.final.score}}).
  • Matched Jaccard depends on set sizes and on the unassigned count. That is why I read the normalised excess and the agreement on assigned images alongside it.
  • Eight checkpoints are coarse. They cannot place an onset inside steps 0 to 64 or 256 to 1,024; 73 checkpoints were saved. The perplexity-100 rule gives the same milestones at b9.
  • Airplanes may be unassigned because they sit between the two sets rather than because they are pushed out of one; the SEBA values of airplane images would tell.

Try it. Open b9 alone at step 0, step 64, step 1,024 and the end, and switch to the class colouring at each. Or take b9's late-training sets and look up the column to see where they sat earlier.

3. Where depth redraws the grouping

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 4$) of the single-snapshot block at each site, compared between sites at the same checkpoint. The screenshot shows site b2 alone at step {{fact:final_step}}, its sets painted on every site.

Claim (observed). At the last checkpoint, the $r = 4$ sets of single-snapshot blocks at adjacent sites agree with matched Jaccard {{fact:q.carry.r4.final.adjacent_min}} to {{fact:q.carry.r4.final.adjacent_max}} (the permutation null for adjacent pairs at this checkpoint is at most {{fact:q.carry.r4.final.adjacent_null_hi_max}}). The grouping changes most at {{fact:q.carry.r4.final.adjacent_min_pair}} ({{fact:q.carry.r4.final.b2_b3}}), then stem to b1 ({{fact:q.carry.r4.final.stem_b1}}), b6 to b7 ({{fact:q.carry.r4.final.b6_b7}}) and b8 to b9 ({{fact:q.carry.r4.final.b8_b9}}), and least at {{fact:q.carry.r4.final.adjacent_max_pair}}. But training does not create this agreement: the mean over adjacent pairs is {{fact:q.carry.r4.step0.mean_adjacent}} at step 0 and {{fact:q.carry.r4.step64000.mean_adjacent}} at the end.

How I read it (reading). At step 0 the leading eigenfunctions share a strong image-statistics component, with luminance prominent. A linear fit on twelve pixel statistics explains $R^2$ = {{fact:q.img.step0.stem.r2_psi1}} of $\psi_1$ at the stem and {{fact:q.img.step0.b9.r2_psi1}} at b9; luminance alone gives a squared correlation of {{fact:q.img.step0.stem.corr2_psi1_luminance}} at the stem and {{fact:q.img.step0.pool.corr2_psi1_luminance}} at pool; and the stem's step-0 $\psi_1$ has |cos| {{fact:q.step0.psi1_abs_cos_with_stem.b9}} with b9's, {{fact:q.step0.psi1_abs_cos_with_stem.pool}} with pool's and {{fact:q.step0.psi1_abs_cos_with_stem.logits}} with the logits'. So at step 0 neighbouring sites agree largely because the untrained network carries the same image statistics through. Training changes what is carried and where it is redrawn. By the end the stem and the logits share little, b9 and the logits share nearly everything, and the largest breaks cut the network into five stretches: stem; b1 and b2; b3 to b6; b7 and b8; b9 to logits.

What else it could be
  • Residual shortcuts copy their input, so some adjacent agreement is architecture, not learning. The stage boundaries, where the shortcut subsamples with stride 2 and zero-pads the new channels, are sharper tests: b6 to b7 is a low point, b3 to b4 ({{fact:q.carry.r4.final.b3_b4}}) is not. The lowest point, b2 to b3, lies inside the first stage, and I have no account of it yet.
  • The stretches depend on where I cut. The five above come from the four lowest adjacent values; "where the grouping changes most" is the robust statement.

Try it. Colour every site by b2's own sets and compare the b2 and b3 columns; then select b3 alone. For contrast, look at b5 at step 0.

4. One snapshot's split against a block's

What we are looking at

The leading eigenfunction $\psi_1$ (not the sets) of the layer block b1 to logits at step {{fact:final_step}}, compared with $\psi_1$ of the single-snapshot block at b1.

Claim (observed). At the end of training, the leading eigenfunction of the layer block b1 to logits is not b1's own (|cos| {{fact:q.f5.b1_vs_b1-logits.final.abs_cos_psi1}} between the two $\psi_1$); it is closest to the single-snapshot $\psi_1$ of {{fact:q.f5.b1_vs_b1-logits.final.block_psi1_best_static_site}} (|cos| {{fact:q.f5.b1_vs_b1-logits.final.block_psi1_best_static_abs_cos}}). Averaging trades retention at b1 for retention in the later layers.

How I read it (reading). Half of this holds by construction. The block's $\psi_1$ maximises mean retention over the block and b1's own $\psi_1$ maximises retention at b1, so the block's must retain less at b1; it does at {{fact:q.f5.final.n_sites_block_ret_own_below_static}} of {{fact:q.f5.final.n_sites}} start sites. The informative half is which split the average picks: not a compromise blurred across the layers, but the split that b6 draws on its own and that b6 to pool keep. A split that only early layers draw loses. That is what a block is for: asked "what does the network treat alike from b1 to the logits?", the average answers with b6's split. The agreement with the start site's own $\psi_1$ rises as the block starts later (b5: {{fact:q.f5.b5_vs_b5-logits.final.abs_cos_psi1}}, b7: {{fact:q.f5.b7_vs_b7-logits.final.abs_cos_psi1}}; median {{fact:q.f5.final.median_abs_cos_psi1}}).

What else it could be
  • $\psi_1$ may not be canonical. If $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ are close, the single direction is arbitrary within their span. The leading three-dimensional spans also differ (mean $\cos^2$ {{fact:q.f5.b1_vs_b1-logits.final.span3_mean_cos2}}), so this is not only a rotation within a span.
  • Average against product. The theory's object is the product of the walks, which weights snapshots differently; the comparison is in Method.

Try it. Open the block's $\psi_1$, then b1's own and b6's own, and compare the retention strips.

5. The sets and the training signal

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 2$ and $r = 4$) of the time block at site b9 over all {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints, set against per-image loss gradients at each checkpoint. The screenshot shows $r = 4$.

Claim (observed). On the images the sets assign, the within-minus-between gradient cosine of b9's label-free sets at the last checkpoint is {{fact:q.grad.r2.b9.all.final.sets_over_class_union_assigned}} times that of the matching class-union grouping with two sets, and {{fact:q.grad.r4.b9.all.final.sets_over_class_union_assigned}} times with four ({{fact:q.grad.r4.b9.late.final.sets_over_class_union_assigned}} for the late-training block).

Against the ten classes the sets score low: their within-minus-between cosine goes from {{fact:q.grad.r4.b9.all.first.sets_diff}} at step 0 to {{fact:q.grad.r4.b9.all.final.sets_diff}} at the end, against {{fact:q.grad.classes.first.diff}} to {{fact:q.grad.classes.final.diff}} for the classes; at the last checkpoint the sets reach at most {{fact:q.grad.r4.all.final.ratio_to_classes_max}} of the classes' value at any site. But the classes are the wrong yardstick for a grouping that merges classes. In logit space the negative cross-entropy gradient of image $i$ is $e_{y_i} - p_i$, so two images of different classes push their own logits in opposite directions however alike they are otherwise. That is the reason to compare the sets with the class-union grouping, which gives each class wholly to the set holding most of it and so merges the same classes. The plotted gradients are with respect to all parameters, $g_i = J_\theta z_i^\top (p_i - e_{y_i})$, so the logit-space fact motivates the reference; the measurement below does the rest.

How I read it (reading). Measured on the same images against the grouping that merges the same classes, the sets are about as gradient-coherent as that grouping with two sets, and about half as coherent with four. The low ratio to the ten classes mostly reflects that the sets merge classes. What the network treats alike in its activations, it also, to a good extent, trains alike.

What else it could be
  • One site. The class-union reference is computed at b9 only so far.
  • The loss itself. Any label-based loss builds the label into the gradient. Gradients of a label-free loss on the same images would show how much of this coherence remains without the label term.

Try it. Open b9's sets over all of training and switch the colouring to the gradient cosine with the mean gradient, checkpoint by checkpoint.

Checks I'd like to run

Longer-range directions (diverging trajectories, behaviour-relevant data over training, and the route from eigenfunctions back to weights) are sketched in the Overview.

  • Split-half resampling of the probe, to see which sets survive.
  • A second seed's sets (only its layouts exist now), and other probes.
  • Denser checkpoints (73 were saved) to place the onsets in finding 2.
  • The product operator in place of the average, for findings 3 and 4.
  • The class-union gradient reference at every site, a within-class null, and a label-free loss.
  • Whether airplanes at b9 are unassigned because they sit between the two sets.
  • From the method side: a kernel on centred but unnormalised activations (which sets depend on dropping the norm), and a probe drawn without labels (removing the one label use before the sets exist).
