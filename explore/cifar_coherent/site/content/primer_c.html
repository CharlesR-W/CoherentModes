One layer is a snapshot; the groups that matter persist

I average the walks over a run of snapshots: neighbouring layers (a layer block) or neighbouring training checkpoints (a time block). Depth or training plays the role of time:

$$ \bar P = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} P_c . $$

Groups that stay together across the whole run are coherent sets, an idea from fluid dynamics (Froyland and collaborators): blobs of fluid that hold together while the flow stirs everything around them. An average can hide a weak snapshot, so each set is also checked snapshot by snapshot (retention, next slide). No map between layers or checkpoints is fitted; each snapshot keeps its own geometry.

From eigenvectors to sets

  • SEBA (Froyland, Rock and Sakellariou) turns the leading $r$ eigenvectors into $r$ sparse vectors, one per set.
  • Each image joins the set whose vector is largest for it; images with no clearly positive vector stay unassigned (grey). Nothing forces every image into a group.
  • Retention asks, snapshot by snapshot, how much of a set the walk keeps inside the set after one step: high means the set holds together there.

How I check it. Nulls that keep part of the structure and break the rest (shuffling which image is which across snapshots; a Gaussian surrogate matched to each snapshot's covariance spectrum; SEBA on random subspaces); baselines (the method on pixels, on the untrained network, and plain PCA + k-means); and a second training seed. See Baselines and nulls, the Method, or try it in the Explorer.

Coherent sets are groups of images that one snapshot's neighbourhoods keep together, and that keep holding together across a run of layers or of training; they come from activations, cosine and eigenvectors alone, with labels used only to check them afterwards.

This is exploratory, on one small network and one probe set. The average operator and its spectrum are well defined once the kernel and block are chosen, but individual eigenvectors are only meaningful when their eigenvalues are separated, the sets depend on where $r$ cuts the spectrum, and the sites, centring, cosine, kernel rule and equal weights could all have been chosen otherwise.
