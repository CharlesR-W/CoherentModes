The kernel sweep

Background to the five kernel rules in Method.

Candidates (data/r20s0b/sweep.json, produced by sweep_math.py), 19 in all: one global $\kappa \in \{2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30\}$; $\kappa$ calibrated to a median 15-NN mass share of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5; per-image perplexity 15, 30, 100, 300; kNN graphs with $k$ = 15, 50. Snapshots: b2, b5, b8, pool and logits at steps 256, 6,400 and 64,000. Each snapshot's operator is computed alone ($M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, Sinkhorn as in Method). Label-free diagnostics: the participation number $\mathrm{PN}(\psi) = 1/\sum_i \psi_i^4$ of the unit vectors $\psi_1..\psi_8$ ($N$ when all entries have equal size, 2 when two images carry everything); $1 - \lambda_1..\lambda_8$; the sizes of the SEBA sets for $r = 4, 8$; and Sinkhorn stability. Matched Jaccard of the SEBA sets with the classes was computed after the choice, as validation.

Average versus product

The project's theory builds on a product, not an average. For a layer block $a < b$, the chronological product $Q = M_b \cdots M_a$ (functions are column vectors; layer $a$ applied first) is the nearest cross-layer object: its right singular vectors are functions at layer $a$, its left singular vectors their normalised images at layer $b$ (Froyland's coherent pairs). The title's "cross-Hankel" is an analogy to this: for a time series $y$, a Hankel matrix $H_{ij} = y_{i+j}$ has windows of the past as rows and of the future as columns, and for linear systems its leading singular vectors give a small state through which the past influences the future; $Q$ plays that role between layer $a$ and layer $b$.

The explorer defaults to $\bar M$. To first order in the step size the two agree: if $M_c = I - \varepsilon L_c$, then $Q^\top Q = I - 2\varepsilon \sum_c L_c + O(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\bar M = I - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{|B|}\sum_c L_c$, so they share leading vectors up to $O(\varepsilon)$ when the relevant eigenvalues of $\sum_c L_c$ are simple. Beyond first order they differ: $(\tfrac12(M_a + M_b))^2$ contains $\tfrac14(M_aM_b + M_bM_a)$, while $Q^\top Q$ does not average.

avgprod.json reports, for the kNN-graph and perplexity-100 rules, with the constant projected out of both sides, the mean $\cos^2$ of principal angles between $\mathrm{span}(\psi_1..\psi_r)$ of $\bar M$ and the top-$r$ non-trivial right and left singular vectors of $Q$ and of the two-end product $M_b M_a$, $r \in \{3, 6\}$. At step {{fact:final_step}}, $r = 3$, over the 66 layer pairs, median (lowest): kNN graph, 0.989 (0.836) for the right and 0.991 (0.881) for the left singular vectors of $Q$, and 0.973 (0.632) and 0.975 (0.641) for the two-end product; perplexity 100, 0.993 (0.645) and 0.995 (0.649), and 0.979 (0.664) and 0.981 (0.661). With $r = 6$ the medians are 0.96 to 0.99.

Product blocks in the explorer

The explorer's Operator switch replaces $\bar M$ by the ordered product, for every layer and time block and every kernel rule. With $C_c = M_c - \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/N$ (the constant projected out; each $M_c$ is symmetric and doubly stochastic, so $C_c = M_c(I - \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/N)$), the block operator is $Q = C_b \cdots C_a$, first snapshot applied first, with singular value decomposition $Q = \sum_k \sigma_k u_k v_k^\top$. Product · start shows the right singular vectors $v_k$: the functions on the probe images that keep the largest share $\sigma_k = |Q v_k|$ of their norm after being smoothed by every snapshot of the block in turn. Product · end shows $u_k = Q v_k / \sigma_k$, what they have become after the last snapshot. Both are exported in exactly the average's format ($\sigma_0 = 1$ for the constant, SEBA on $v_0..v_{r-1}$ or $u_0..u_{r-1}$), from a float64 eigensolve of $Q^\top Q$ or $QQ^\top$.

Two properties differ from the average. The product is multiplicative: a function's survival is the product of its step gains $g_c = |C_c w_{c-1}| / |w_{c-1}|$ ($w$ the function pushed through the block so far, from first to last snapshot for $v$, from last to first for $u$), so one snapshot that erases a split sinks it, where the average only dilutes it. The retention strip shows these gains (solid) next to $\rho_c$ (dashed); for $\psi_k$ they multiply to $\sigma_k$ exactly (checked to $1.4\times10^{-7}$ on every exported block). The product is order-sensitive, but only up to reversal: each $M_c$ is symmetric, so reversing the snapshots transposes $Q$, which keeps the singular values and swaps start and end. The average ignores order entirely.

Measured, at step {{fact:final_step}}, {{fact:mode_name.rule0}}, $r = 4$, matched Jaccard between the average's and the product's hard sets on the same block, mean over layer blocks of each length: 0.99 for two sites, 0.95 for four, 0.90 for six, 0.83 for eight, 0.76 for ten, and 0.64 (start) and 0.77 (end) for the full stem–logits block. The leading spans agree more than the sets (full block, mean $\cos^2$: 0.84 start, 0.88 end). For the full block the singular values are $\sigma_{1..3}$ = 0.38, 0.20, 0.14, against $\lambda_{1..3}$ = 0.91, 0.85, 0.82: averaging and composing twelve walks give very different scales, so compare $\sigma$ only with $\sigma$. Against CIFAR classes (validation only; matched Jaccard, full block) the end sets align about as well as the average's at $r = 4$ (0.42 against 0.41) and better at $r = 8$ (0.74 against 0.61); the start sets less (0.29 and 0.38). Read naively, functions that survive the whole network in order are class-like by the time they reach the logits, but at the stem they are not yet the average's groups. One network, one seed; product_summary.json has every block, rule and $r$.

Layouts (display only)

  • Per checkpoint: umap.AlignedUMAP across the 12 sites (identity relations between consecutive sites), n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.1, alignment_regularisation=0.02, random_state=0, on the top 50 principal components ($u_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}$ from the eigendecomposition of $C$) of each snapshot.
  • Across checkpoints, per site: centre, scale to unit RMS, orthogonal Procrustes (reflections allowed) onto the previous checkpoint, then rescale to the 0.5 and 99.5 percentiles for drawing. The within-checkpoint alignment is part of the UMAP objective and shapes the layouts; across checkpoints only rotation, reflection, centring and scale change.
  • No quantity is computed from the 2-d coordinates. Distances in a layout are not distances in the operator.
  • Seed 1 ("layout from"). A second ResNet-20, identical recipe, training seed 1; only its activations' top-50 PCs and its own AlignedUMAP and Procrustes layouts are computed, for the same probe images and steps. Colours always come from seed 0's blocks. Seed 1's layouts are aligned along its own checkpoints, not onto seed 0's.
  • Spectral layout. For each snapshot, the diffusion coordinates $\lambda_k \psi_k$, $k = 1..32$, of that snapshot's own lazy walk $M_c$ under the {{fact:mode_name.rule0}} rule (diffusion map at $t = 1$; constant excluded; the same single-snapshot eigenfunctions the explorer shows for $a = b$, extended to 32), then the same AlignedUMAP and Procrustes steps as above. Where the activation layout shows the cosine geometry, this one shows the geometry the construction actually uses: two images are close when the walk's slow modes cannot tell them apart. It is fixed per snapshot and does not change with the selected block or kernel rule. Display only, like the others.

Per-image diagnostics

At each checkpoint, eval mode: cross-entropy loss, prediction, correctness; $\lVert \partial z / \partial x \rVert_F$, the Frobenius norm of the $10 \times 3072$ Jacobian of the logits $z$ with respect to the normalised input pixels; per-sample loss gradients $g_i$ with respect to all trainable parameters, $\bar g = \frac1N \sum_i g_i$ over the probe, and $g_i \cdot \bar g$, $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$, $\lVert g_i \rVert$. These use labels through the loss and never enter an operator.

Statistics behind the Measurements appendix

Defaults unless a chart says otherwise: the {{fact:default_mode}} and $r = 4$. Class labels and probe pixels only describe and validate here; none of these statistics enters an operator. Exact definitions and the JSON layout are in the docstrings of site/analysis.py and site/analysis_findings.py.

Matched Jaccard, null and size ceiling

Hard labelings $A$ ($r_A$ sets) and $B$ ($r_B$ sets; 10 for the classes), unassigned images in no set: $J_{ij} = |A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$ (0 if the union is empty); Hungarian matching maximising total $J$ over $\min(r_A, r_B)$ pairs; score = mean matched $J$. Null: permute the label vector of $A$, unassigned entries included (keeps $A$'s set sizes and unassigned count), 200 permutations from one shared seeded bank; stored as the mean $\mu_0$, the 2.5 / 97.5 percentiles and the maximum. Size ceiling (against a fixed reference labeling): the same Hungarian mean over $\min(|A_i|, |B_j|) / \max(|A_i|, |B_j|)$, the largest score any sets of these sizes could reach. The explorer's legend uses the same score with a 30-draw null.

  • When (q_when): for each site and checkpoint, matched Jaccard of that single-snapshot block's sets with the same site's sets at the final checkpoint, and with the classes.
  • Carry-over (q_carry): at each checkpoint, matched Jaccard between the single-snapshot sets of every pair of sites (no null), of adjacent sites (with null), and of adjacent layer blocks of width 2 and 3.

Class make-up

For the default rule, every layer block and every time block, and $r \in \{2, 3, 4\}$: the $(r + 1) \times 10$ table of counts $n_{jc} = \#\{\text{images in SEBA set } j \text{ with class } c\}$, rows in reliability order, with a last row for the unassigned images. The charts show it for single-snapshot blocks as a heatmap with a checkpoint slider (colour range 0 to 200, the images per class). Per-class placement (at $r = 2$): for each class $c$ and snapshot, the fraction of $c$'s 200 images in the set matched to $c$'s super-class (below), and the fraction unassigned.

Vehicles against animals at $r = 2$

$V$ = {airplane, automobile, ship, truck} (800 images), $A$ = the other six classes (1,200). For every kernel rule and single-snapshot block (and, for the default rule, every time block), the two $r = 2$ SEBA sets are compared with the 2-set labeling $(V, A)$:

  • score: their matched Jaccard (for $2 \times 2$, the better of the two pairings), with null mean $\mu_0$, null band and null maximum over the 200 permutations, and size ceiling as above;
  • norm_excess $= (\text{score} - \mu_0) / (\text{ceiling} - \mu_0)$: 0 at the null mean, 1 when the sets are as aligned with $(V, A)$ as their sizes allow;
  • agree: under the matching, the fraction of assigned images whose set is matched to their own super-class; vehicle fraction of set $j$: the share of $V$ among its members.

Milestones per site, over the {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints $t$, with $T$ the last:

  • half_step: the first $t$ such that $\text{norm\_excess}(t') \ge 0.5$ at every $t' \ge t$;
  • most_step: the first $t$ such that $\text{score}(t') - \mu_0(t') \ge 0.8\,(\text{score}(T) - \mu_0(T))$ at every $t' \ge t$ (80% of the final excess over the null mean, kept from then on); undefined if the final score does not exceed its null maximum.
  • The permutation onset (the first $t$ whose score exceeds its null maximum at every later checkpoint, a one-sided Monte Carlo permutation $p$ of $1/201$, the smallest 200 draws can give) is step {{fact:q.va.onset_step.all_sites}} at every site here, so the charts report half_step and most_step.

Pixel baseline (a closed-form comparison, not part of the method): for each snapshot, each of the 12 image statistics below and each sign, assign the top $n_1$ images by the signed statistic to set 1 and the bottom $n_2$ to set 2 ($n_j$ = that snapshot's SEBA set sizes; the rest unassigned), and score the split against $(V, A)$ the same way. pixel_best is the largest of these 24 scores. It is chosen using the labels, so it is an upper reference for single-statistic splits of these sizes. Best class bipartition (default rule): over all 511 splits of the 10 classes into two groups, the one whose labeling has the largest matched Jaccard with the $r = 2$ sets, and the rank of $(V, A)$ among them (1 = best).

Image statistics

From the probe pixels ($32 \times 32 \times 3$, scaled to $[0, 1]$, no normalisation), twelve statistics per image, with $Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B$ (Rec. 601 luma of the gamma-encoded values):

  • mean_r, mean_g, mean_b: channel means over the 1,024 pixels; luminance: mean of $Y$; contrast: standard deviation of $Y$ (RMS contrast); saturation: mean over pixels of $(\max - \min) / \max$ of $(R, G, B)$ (0 where $\max = 0$).
  • bc_r, bc_g, bc_b, bc_lum: mean over the border ring (the 448 pixels within 4 px of the edge) minus mean over the central $16 \times 16$ pixels, for $R$, $G$, $B$ and $Y$ (a background proxy); bc_dist: the Euclidean norm of (bc_r, bc_g, bc_b).
  • hf_frac: with $F$ the 2-D DFT of $Y - \bar Y$, the share of $\sum |F|^2$ at frequency radius above 8 cycles per image (half the Nyquist radius) among all non-zero frequencies.

For each block (every single-snapshot block; layer blocks stem..logits, b1..logits, b5..pool and b7..pool at every checkpoint; time blocks over all checkpoints and over the later half at every site), 17 functions: $\psi_1..\psi_8$ and the SEBA vectors for $r = 2, 3, 4$.

  • Correlation: Pearson correlation over the $N$ probe images between a function and a statistic. The sign for $\psi_k$ follows the stored sign convention; only $|\mathrm{corr}|$ is sign-free.
  • Set contrast: for hard set $j$ ($r = 2, 3, 4$), the mean of the standardised statistic over set $j$ minus its mean over all other images (unassigned included), in units of the statistic's SD over the $N$ images.
  • Within-class correlation: the Pearson correlation after subtracting, from both the function and the statistic, their mean over the image's class. Class make-up alone cannot produce it: it measures whether $f$ tracks the statistic among images of the same class.
  • $R^2$ of the fit (a closed-form least-squares fit): $R^2$ of $\psi_k$ regressed on the 12 standardised statistics plus an intercept. Its null permutes the images (same bank as below); stored per block as the 95th percentile pooled over $k = 1..8$ (expected null mean about $\text{rank}/N$, the 12 statistics having rank {{fact:q.img.rank}}; largest over blocks {{fact:q.img.r2_null_hi_max}}).
  • Class contrasts (labels describe the statistics only): mean standardised statistic over class $c$ minus over the other classes, and over $V$ minus over $A$.

Thresholds. One bank of 1,000 image permutations is applied to the statistics, the same draws for every block. Per block: pointwise = 95th percentile of $|\text{null corr}|$ pooled over the $17 \times 12$ entries (a two-sided 5% level for one entry); familywise = 95th percentile over draws of the largest $|\text{null corr}|$ among the block's $17 \times 12$ entries; familywise for $\psi$, per statistic = the same over $\psi_1..\psi_8$ only; the same pointwise and familywise pair for the set contrasts. Global = 95th percentile over draws of the largest $|\text{null corr}|$ over every block and entry ({{fact:q.img.thr_global}} here). The within-class correlations have their own null, permuting images within each class (a second bank of 1,000), with familywise thresholds per block and per statistic over $\psi_1..\psi_8$.

Gradient coherence

$G_t$ is the cosine Gram of per-sample loss gradients at checkpoint $t$. For the sets of a time block at one site (all checkpoints, or the later half), at each $t$: mean of $G_t$ over ordered pairs $i \ne j$ in the same set, minus the mean over assigned pairs in different sets (pair-weighted, unassigned excluded). The same for the 10 classes. Null: permute set labels among the assigned images, 100 permutations; the band is the measured 2.5 / 97.5 percentile range (pairs sharing an image are not independent, so it is not a standard error). Set alignment: mean $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$ over each set's members.

Class-union reference (b9; $r = 2$ and 4; both time blocks). Give each class wholly to the set that holds most of its assigned images. This labels images by unions of classes, merging classes as the sets do but cutting none. The primary reference computes the same within-minus-between statistic for these union labels on the images the sets assign, so its population is the same as the sets' own statistic (unassigned images excluded). The reported quantity is the ratio of the sets' statistic to this reference at the final checkpoint. Comparing the two separates "the sets merge classes" from "the sets cut across classes"; the reference is needed because the gradients carry the one-hot label. A secondary version applies the union labels to all $N$ images, a different population from the sets'.

Decodability

A validation fit, not part of the construction: 10,000 CIFAR training images (1,000 per class) fit, all 10,000 test images evaluated (they include the {{fact:n_images}} probe images); features centred by the fit mean and L2-normalised; flattened maps and spatial channel means ("GAP") for convolutional sites. Closed-form ridge on one-hot targets, no intercept, $\lambda$ by generalised cross-validation on the fit set; weighted cosine $k$-NN, $k = 20$, votes $\exp(\cos/0.07)$.

Baselines, nulls and comparison statistics

Definitions behind the Baselines and nulls appendix (B) and the explorer's Sets from control (from the study's plan and schema, explore/cifar_baselines/PLAN.md and SCHEMA.md). All of it uses kernel rule {{fact:mode_name.rule0}}. Notation: $Z_l$ the centred, row-normalised activations of snapshot $l$, $C_l = Z_l Z_l^\top$ its cosine Gram, $H = I - \tfrac1N \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ the centring matrix, $C_B = \frac1L \sum_{l \in B} C_l$ the block Gram (the Gram of the concatenated per-snapshot features, up to the factor $1/L$), $q = r - 1$ non-constant directions, and $k_c = r$ sets for every source.

Sources

  • Cosine PCA + SEBA (S+P): the top $q$ eigenvectors of $H C_B H$, with the constant prepended, rounded by the unchanged SEBA. Classical MDS is the same computation here. For unit vectors the squared chordal distance is $d_{ij}^2 = 2 - 2C_{ij}$, and classical MDS diagonalises $-\tfrac12 H D^{(2)} H = H C H$, the doubly centred Gram, so its coordinates are the principal directions of $C$ (the "kernel reconstruction" reading of MDS).
  • PCA + k-means (B): k-means with $k = r$ and 20 starts on the scores $U_p \Lambda_p^{1/2}$, $p = \max(q, 20)$.
  • Spherical k-means (B): maximise $\sum_c \lVert \sum_{i \in c} z_i \rVert$ over partitions into $r$ sets (each image assigned to the centroid of largest cosine), evaluated through $C_B$; 20 k-means++ starts.
  • Method eigenvectors + k-means (S+B): k-means on $\psi_1..\psi_q$; row-normalised: on the rows of $[\psi_0, .., \psi_q]$ scaled to unit length (Ng, Jordan and Weiss 2001).
  • Textbook spectral clustering (S+B): $\frac1L \sum_l D_l^{-1/2} A_l D_l^{-1/2}$ with $A_l$ the snapshot's 0/1 union 15-NN graph (no floor, no Sinkhorn, no lazy step); its leading eigenvectors, rows normalised, then k-means.
  • First / middle / last snapshot (S+P): the method on the single-snapshot block $(a, a)$, $(m, m)$ with $m = \lfloor (a+b)/2 \rfloor$, or $(b, b)$.
  • Co-assignment consensus (P): $A_B(i, j) = \frac1L \sum_l \mathbb 1[\ell_l(i) = \ell_l(j) \ne \text{unassigned}]$ with $\ell_l$ the single-snapshot sets at the same $r$; the top $q$ eigenvectors of $H A_B H$, then SEBA.
  • Mean-projector consensus (S+P): the top $q$ eigenvectors of $\frac1L \sum_l U_l U_l^\top$, $U_l$ the snapshot's own $\psi_1..\psi_q$, then SEBA.
  • Pixels, untrained network, other training seed (S+P): the method unchanged on the network's input, on the same block at step 0, and on the same layer block of a second network (seed 1) at the final checkpoint.
  • Classes, predicted class (V): 10 sets each; the prediction is taken at the block's checkpoint (layer block) or its last checkpoint (time block).

Nulls

  • Trajectory shuffle: $P_l \to \Pi_l P_l \Pi_l^\top$ with independent random permutations $\Pi_l$, $\Pi_l = I$ for the block's first snapshot. Draws are coherent: draw $t$ uses the same permutation of a snapshot in every block and rank. Within-class: each $\Pi_l$ permutes images only within their class.
  • Spectrum-matched Gaussian: per snapshot, Gaussian activations whose covariance has the nonzero eigenvalues of $H R_l H / N$ ($R_l = X_l X_l^\top$ the raw Gram), independent across snapshots, then the full pipeline. It keeps the spectrum in expectation and before the pipeline's centring and row normalisation, not after them.
  • Joint second-order surrogate: one Haar orthogonal $O$ ($N \times N$, $O\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1$) shared by the block's snapshots, $C^*_l = D_l O C_l O^\top D_l$ with $D_l$ diagonal restoring the unit diagonal. Before $D_l$ this keeps the mean and every within- and cross-snapshot second moment exactly; after $D_l$ it does not, and the stored diagnostic (relative change of $\operatorname{tr}(C_l C_m)$) changes by up to {{fact:bn.n2b_mm_max}} over the twelve representative blocks ({{fact:bn.n2b_mm_b4b6}} for b4 to b6).
  • Degree-preserving rewiring: double-edge swaps on each snapshot's 15-NN graph before the floor, keeping every degree; then floor, Sinkhorn and the lazy step.
  • Random subspaces: SEBA on $[\mathbf 1/\sqrt N, V]$ with $V$ a Haar-random $N \times q$ orthonormal basis orthogonal to $\mathbf 1$; and SEBA started from random rotations of the real span.
  • Label permutation (for agreement scores): one labelling is permuted across the images, which keeps every set size and the unassigned count; for the joint AMI the permutation runs within the jointly assigned images. Any selection (a largest value over blocks) is repeated inside each permutation.

Comparison statistics

  • AMI (adjusted mutual information, arithmetic normalisation): $\mathrm{AMI}(a, b) = \dfrac{I(a, b) - \mathbb E\,I}{\tfrac12 (H(a) + H(b)) - \mathbb E\,I}$, where $\mathbb E\,I$ is the expected mutual information over permutations with the same marginals. 0 is the chance level and 1 the same partition. Joint AMI: on the images both labellings assign, always with its coverage, the share of such images. The variant with "unassigned" as one more category also rewards agreeing on which images are unassigned.
  • Matched Jaccard: $\frac{1}{\max(k_a, k_b)} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal M} J_{ij}$ over a Hungarian matching $\mathcal M$ of sets maximising total Jaccard $J_{ij} = |A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$; empty declared sets count. (The explorer's Legend card and the Measurements appendix report the mean over matched sets instead.)
  • Largest-over-blocks band: the mean and 95th percentile, over permutations, of the largest score over all blocks of one checkpoint (layer blocks) or one site (time blocks) at a fixed source and $r$.
  • Subspace overlap: $\frac1q \lVert U^\top V \rVert_F^2$ (mean squared cosine of the principal angles) of two $q$-dimensional spans with the constant removed; chance $q/(N-1)$.
  • Retention excess: $R_P(A) = \mathbf 1_A^\top P \mathbf 1_A / |A|$ minus its size null $t + (1 - t)(|A| - 1)/(N - 1)$, $t = \operatorname{tr}(P)/N$ (0 for the default zero-diagonal walk): the retention of a random set of the same size.
  • Variance capture ("kernel reconstruction"): $\lVert \Pi_U G \Pi_U \rVert_F^2 / \lVert G \rVert_F^2$ against the rank-$q$ optimum $\sum_{j \le q} \lambda_j^2 / \sum_j \lambda_j^2$ of $G$; Dirichlet energy of a unit $f$ under snapshot $l$, as plotted: $f^\top (I - M_l) f = \tfrac12 f^\top (I - P_l) f$ with $M_l$ the lazy walk.
  • CKA: $\langle \tilde C_1, \tilde C_2 \rangle_F / (\lVert \tilde C_1 \rVert_F \lVert \tilde C_2 \rVert_F)$ with $\tilde C = HCH$; debiased: the same with U-centred Grams (zero diagonal, unbiased centring), whose value under independence is near 0 at finite $N$.
  • Rank nesting: $H(\text{sets at } r \mid \text{sets at } r')$ in nats for $r < r'$; 0 when the finer sets refine the coarser ones.

Transfer-operator pilot

For snapshots $a$, $b$ of the same images, $(Tf)(y) = \mathbb E[f(X) \mid Y = y]$ on the empirical marginals. A fitted estimate is $T = (C_{YY} + \rho_Y I)^{-1/2} C_{YX} (C_{XX} + \rho_X I)^{-1/2}$ on centred finite features (linear PCA scores, Nyström features of $e^{\kappa(x \cdot x' - 1)}$, or diffusion coordinates; $\rho = \eta\, \operatorname{tr}(C)/m$ is a ridge fit), or $T = D_q^{-1/2} B D_p^{-1/2}$ for the soft Ulam joint table $B$ of landmark memberships. Its singular vectors give source and destination functions; held-out correlation is their Pearson correlation on test images; VAMP-2 is the squared Frobenius norm of the test-split cross-covariance of the six fitted source and destination functions after whitening each side by its regularised test covariance (the tuned ridge; for soft Ulam none): $\lVert S_f^{-1/2} S_{fg} S_g^{-1/2} \rVert_F^2$, as transfer.py computes it. Composition discrepancy on $a \to b \to c$: $T_{bc}T_{ab} - T_{ac} = A_c^{-1/2} S_{cb} (A_b^{-1} - G_b^{+}) S_{ba} A_a^{-1/2} + A_c^{-1/2} (S_{cb} G_b^{+} S_{ba} - S_{ca}) A_a^{-1/2}$, the regulariser term plus the partial term, with $S$ the fit cross-moments, $A$ the regularised and $G$ the unregularised marginal moments (TRANSFER.md, section 5).
