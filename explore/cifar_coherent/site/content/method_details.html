Exact definitions behind the experiments and the other appendices: the defaults, every comparison statistic with its null and ceiling, the class references and per-image quantities, the baseline sources and nulls, the distances between operators, and the coarse-graining and Markov quantities. Class labels, losses, gradients and probe pixels appear here only as comparisons; none of them enters a walk, a block operator or a SEBA rotation, and none chooses a block, $r$, $k$ or a kernel rule.

Defaults and conventions

Unless a caption or an experiment's scope line says otherwise:

  • Network and data. ResNet-20, training seed 0, the {{fact:n_images}} label-stratified probe images ($N$), {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints (steps {{fact:steps}}; "final" is step {{fact:final_step}}) and {{fact:n_sites}} sites. The construction is the one in Method: centred cosine Gram per snapshot, kernel, zero diagonal, doubly stochastic $P$, lazy walk $M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, block operator $\bar M$ (average over the block's snapshots).
  • Kernel rule {{fact:default_mode}} ({{fact:mode_name.rule0}}, rule 0). The other four rules and the dot kernel are in Appendix A.
  • Blocks. Single-snapshot blocks (one site at one checkpoint) unless a layer block $a..b$ or a time block is named.
  • SEBA rank $r$ counts the constant: $r = 4$ means SEBA applied to $\psi_0, \dots, \psi_3$ of the block operator, giving four SEBA vectors. Hard sets: each image goes to the SEBA vector with the largest value; unassigned images (every SEBA value zero) are in no set. Stored labels (seba_label) are used, never membership reconstructed from the stored eigenvectors.
  • Nontrivial rank $k$ (spans, distances, coarse-graining) does not count the constant: the leading-$k$ span is $\mathrm{span}(\psi_1, \dots, \psi_k)$ with $\psi_0 = \mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ excluded.
  • Eigenvalue scales. Stored spectra are eigenvalues $\lambda_M$ of the lazy walk $M$. Charts that show the walk without its lazy half use $\lambda_P = 2\lambda_M - 1$; every chart that uses $\lambda_P$ says so. $1 - \lambda_M = \tfrac12(1 - \lambda_P)$, so ratios of $1 - \lambda$ are the same on both scales.
  • Retention of a function in a snapshot is defined in the glossary.

Comparison statistics, nulls and ceilings

Exact definitions and the JSON layout are also in the docstrings of site/analysis.py and site/analysis_findings.py.

Matched Jaccard, its null and the size ceiling

Hard labelings $A$ ($r_A$ sets) and $B$ ($r_B$ sets; $10$ for the classes), unassigned images in no set: $J_{ij} = |A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$ ($0$ if the union is empty); a Hungarian matching maximises the total $J$ over $\min(r_A, r_B)$ pairs; the score is the mean matched $J$, in $[0, 1]$.

  • Permutation null. Permute the label vector of $A$, unassigned entries included, which keeps $A$'s set sizes and unassigned count and $B$ fixed. One bank of $200$ permutations is drawn once from a seeded generator and reused for every comparison (each null is exact on its own; nulls of different comparisons share draws). Stored: the null mean $\mu_0$, the $2.5$ and $97.5$ percentiles (the null band) and the maximum over draws.
  • Size ceiling (against a fixed reference labeling $B$): the same Hungarian mean over $\min(|A_i|, |B_j|) / \max(|A_i|, |B_j|)$, the largest score any sets with $A$'s sizes could reach against $B$. With fewer sets than classes it is well below $1$, so a score against the classes is read against its ceiling.
  • The explorer's Legend card reports the same score with a $30$-draw null. The baselines appendix uses a variant normalised by $\max(k_a, k_b)$ instead of the number of matched pairs (below).

Normalised excess

For a score with null mean $\mu_0$ and size ceiling $c$: $\text{norm\_excess} = (\text{score} - \mu_0) / (c - \mu_0)$. It is $0$ at the null mean and $1$ when the sets are as aligned with the reference as their sizes allow. The excess is $\text{score} - \mu_0$, without the division.

Agreement with the final sets, and between sites

  • Against the final sets (q_when): for each site and checkpoint, the matched Jaccard of that single-snapshot block's sets with the same site's sets at the final checkpoint (null: permute the earlier checkpoint's sets), and with the classes (null: permute the SEBA sets).
  • Between sites (q_carry): at each checkpoint, the matched Jaccard between the single-snapshot sets of every pair of sites (no null); of adjacent sites $a$, $a + 1$ (null: permute site $a$'s sets); and of adjacent layer blocks of width $2$ and $3$ (null: permute the first block's sets).

Milestones over training

Per site, over the {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints $t$, with $T$ the last. "Stays" means "at every later checkpoint as well".

  • first_step (agreement with the final sets): the first $t$ whose score against the final sets is above $0.5$ and above its null's $97.5$th percentile. The final checkpoint qualifies trivially (it is compared with itself). settled_step: the first $t$ from which this holds and stays.
  • half_step (class bipartition, below): the first $t$ from which $\text{norm\_excess} \ge 0.5$ and stays: at least half of the excess over the null mean that the set sizes allow. It is not half of the final alignment.
  • most_step: the first $t$ from which $\text{score} - \mu_0 \ge 0.8\,(\text{score}(T) - \mu_0(T))$ and stays; undefined if the final score does not exceed its null maximum.
  • onset_step: the first $t$ whose score exceeds its null maximum and stays (a one-sided Monte Carlo permutation $p$ of $1/201$ at each such checkpoint, the smallest $200$ draws can give). On this network it is step {{fact:q.va.onset_step.all_sites}} at every site, so it dates nothing, and half_step and most_step are the milestones reported.

Further comparison statistics

  • AMI (adjusted mutual information, arithmetic normalisation): $\mathrm{AMI}(a, b) = \dfrac{I(a, b) - \mathbb E\,I}{\tfrac12 (H(a) + H(b)) - \mathbb E\,I}$, where $\mathbb E\,I$ is the expected mutual information over permutations with the same marginals. $0$ is the chance level and $1$ the same partition. Joint AMI: on the images both labellings assign, always reported with its coverage, the share of such images. The variant with "unassigned" as one more category also rewards agreeing on which images are unassigned.
  • Matched Jaccard, baselines variant: $\frac{1}{\max(k_a, k_b)} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal M} J_{ij}$ over a Hungarian matching $\mathcal M$ of sets maximising total Jaccard; empty declared sets count.
  • Largest-over-blocks band: the mean and $95$th percentile, over permutations, of the largest score over all blocks of one checkpoint (layer blocks) or one site (time blocks) at a fixed source and $r$. Any selection of a largest value is repeated inside each permutation.
  • Subspace overlap (also "span agreement"): $\frac1q \lVert U^\top V \rVert_F^2$, the mean squared cosine of the principal angles between two $q$-dimensional spans with the constant removed; $1$ for equal spans; for independent random spans its expectation is $q/(N-1)$, the scale reference. A single-mode comparison ($q = 1$) is $\cos^2$ of the angle between two eigenfunctions; it is only meaningful where the cut after the first nontrivial mode is resolved in both operators (cut diagnostics).
  • Retention excess: $R_P(A) = \mathbf 1_A^\top P \mathbf 1_A / |A|$ minus its size null $\tau + (1 - \tau)(|A| - 1)/(N - 1)$, $\tau = \operatorname{tr}(P)/N$ ($0$ for the default zero-diagonal walk): the retention of a random set of the same size.
  • Variance capture ("kernel reconstruction"): $\lVert \Pi_U G \Pi_U \rVert_F^2 / \lVert G \rVert_F^2$ against the rank-$q$ optimum $\sum_{j \le q} \lambda_j^2 / \sum_j \lambda_j^2$ of $G$. Dirichlet energy of a unit $f$ under snapshot $l$, as plotted: $f^\top (I - M_l) f = \tfrac12 f^\top (I - P_l) f$.
  • CKA: $\langle \tilde C_1, \tilde C_2 \rangle_F / (\lVert \tilde C_1 \rVert_F \lVert \tilde C_2 \rVert_F)$ with $\tilde C = HCH$; debiased: the same with U-centred Grams (zero diagonal, unbiased centring), whose value under independence is near $0$ at finite $N$. CKA is a baseline representation-similarity measure, not an output of the construction.
  • Rank nesting: $H(\text{sets at } r \mid \text{sets at } r')$ in nats for $r < r'$; $0$ when the finer sets refine the coarser ones.

Class references and per-image quantities

Class make-up

For the default rule, every layer block and every time block, and $r \in \{2, 3, 4\}$: the $(r + 1) \times 10$ table of counts $n_{jc} = \#\{\text{images in SEBA set } j \text{ with class } c\}$, rows in reliability order, with a last row for the unassigned images. Heatmaps of it use the colour range $0$ to $200$, the images per class.

The class bipartition at $r = 2$

$B_1$ = {airplane, automobile, ship, truck} ($800$ images), $B_2$ = the other six classes ($1{,}200$). This reference was chosen after the final $r = 2$ sets had been seen, so it is post hoc. For every kernel rule and single-snapshot block (and, for the default rule, every time block), the two $r = 2$ SEBA sets are compared with the two-set labeling $(B_1, B_2)$:

  • score: their matched Jaccard (for $2 \times 2$, the better of the two pairings), with null mean $\mu_0$, null band and null maximum over the $200$ permutations, and the size ceiling, as above;
  • norm_excess and the milestones as above;
  • agree: under the matching, the fraction of assigned images whose set is matched to their own side of the bipartition; the $B_1$ fraction of set $j$ (veh_frac): the share of $B_1$ images among its members;
  • per-class placement: for each class $c$ and snapshot, the fraction of $c$'s images in the set matched to $c$'s side of the bipartition, and the fraction unassigned.

Rank among class bipartitions (default rule): over all $511$ splits of the ten classes into two non-empty groups, the split whose labeling has the largest matched Jaccard with the $r = 2$ sets, and the rank of $(B_1, B_2)$ among them ($1$ = best). It describes where this post-hoc reference ranks; it is neither a prespecified test nor adjusted for the selection.

Pixel baseline (a closed-form comparison, not part of the method): for each snapshot, each of the twelve image statistics and each sign, assign the top $n_1$ images by the signed statistic to set $1$ and the bottom $n_2$ to set $2$ ($n_j$ = that snapshot's SEBA set sizes; the rest unassigned), and score the split against $(B_1, B_2)$ the same way. pixel_best is the largest of these $24$ scores. It is chosen using the labels, so it is an upper reference for single-statistic splits of these sizes.

Image statistics

From the probe pixels ($32 \times 32 \times 3$, scaled to $[0, 1]$, no normalisation), twelve statistics per image, with $Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B$ (the Rec. 601 luma of the gamma-encoded values):

  • mean_r, mean_g, mean_b: channel means over the pixels; luminance: mean of $Y$; contrast: standard deviation of $Y$ (RMS contrast); saturation: mean over pixels of $(\max - \min) / \max$ of $(R, G, B)$ ($0$ where $\max = 0$).
  • bc_r, bc_g, bc_b, bc_lum: mean over the border ring (the $448$ pixels within $4$ px of the edge) minus mean over the central $16 \times 16$ pixels, for $R$, $G$, $B$ and $Y$ (a background proxy); bc_dist: the Euclidean norm of (bc_r, bc_g, bc_b).
  • hf_frac: with $F$ the two-dimensional DFT of $Y - \bar Y$, the share of $\sum |F|^2$ at frequency radius above $8$ cycles per image (half the Nyquist radius) among all non-zero frequencies.

For each block (every single-snapshot block; the layer blocks stem..logits, b1..logits, b5..pool and b7..pool at every checkpoint; time blocks over all checkpoints and over the later half at every site), $17$ functions: $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_8$ and the SEBA vectors for $r = 2, 3, 4$.

  • Correlation: Pearson correlation over the $N$ probe images between a function and a statistic. The sign for $\psi_k$ follows the stored sign convention; only $|\mathrm{corr}|$ is sign-free.
  • Set contrast: for hard set $j$ ($r = 2, 3, 4$), the mean of the standardised statistic over set $j$ minus its mean over all other images (unassigned included), in units of the statistic's standard deviation over the $N$ images.
  • Within-class correlation: the Pearson correlation after subtracting, from both the function and the statistic, their mean over the image's class. Class make-up alone cannot produce it: it measures whether $f$ tracks the statistic among images of the same class.
  • $R^2$ of the fit (a closed-form least-squares fit, in-probe, not part of the construction): $R^2$ of $\psi_k$ regressed on the twelve standardised statistics plus an intercept. Its null permutes the images (the bank below); stored per block as the $95$th percentile pooled over $k = 1, \dots, 8$. The twelve statistics have rank {{fact:q.img.rank}} on the probe, so the null mean is about rank$/N$; the largest stored null value over blocks is {{fact:q.img.r2_null_hi_max}}. A low $R^2$ does not rule out nonlinear dependence or image information outside these twelve statistics.
  • Class contrasts (labels describe the statistics only): mean standardised statistic over class $c$ minus over the other classes, and over $B_1$ minus over $B_2$.

Thresholds. One bank of $1{,}000$ image permutations is applied to the statistics, the same draws for every block. Per block: pointwise = $95$th percentile of $|\text{null corr}|$ pooled over the $17 \times 12$ entries (a two-sided $5\%$ level for one entry); familywise = $95$th percentile over draws of the largest $|\text{null corr}|$ among the block's $17 \times 12$ entries; familywise for $\psi$, per statistic = the same over $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_8$ only; the same pointwise and familywise pair for the set contrasts. Global = $95$th percentile over draws of the largest $|\text{null corr}|$ over every block and entry ({{fact:q.img.thr_global}} here). The within-class correlations have their own null, permuting images within each class (a second bank of $1{,}000$), with familywise thresholds per block and per statistic over $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_8$.

Gradient coherence

$g_i$ is the per-image cross-entropy gradient with respect to all trainable parameters of the network (not of one site), and $G_t$ the cosine Gram of the $g_i$ at checkpoint $t$. The gradients use labels through the loss. Because $G_t$ does not depend on the site, comparing sets from different sites changes only the grouping that is scored. For the hard sets of a time block at one site (all checkpoints, or the later half), at each $t$:

$$ \text{diff}_t = \underbrace{\text{mean of } G_t[i,j] \text{ over ordered pairs } i \ne j \text{ in the same set}}_{\text{within}} \;-\; \underbrace{\text{mean over assigned pairs in different sets}}_{\text{between}} , $$

pair-weighted (larger sets weigh more), unassigned images excluded. The same for the ten classes.

  • Set-permutation null: permute the set labels among the assigned images ($100$ permutations; set sizes kept, unassigned stay unassigned); the band is the measured $2.5$ to $97.5$ percentile range. Pairs sharing an image are not independent, so the band is not a standard error.
  • Within-class null: permute the set labels among the assigned images within each class. It keeps each class's count in each set and the unassigned mask, and removes any alignment between the sets and the gradients that is not carried by class make-up. The number of draws is given where it is plotted.
  • Class-union reference ($r = 2$ and $4$; both time blocks): give each class wholly to the set that holds most of its assigned images. This labels images by unions of classes, merging classes as the sets do but cutting none. The primary reference computes $\text{diff}_t$ for these union labels on the images the sets assign, so its population is the same as the sets' own statistic. A secondary version applies the union labels to all $N$ images, a different population. The reference is needed because the gradients carry the one-hot label: sets that merge whole classes inherit within-class gradient similarity.
  • Ratio to the reference: the sets' $\text{diff}_t$ divided by the class-union $\text{diff}_t$ on the same images. It is reported only where the class-union difference exceeds its own null's $97.5$th percentile; where the denominator is within its null the ratio is not interpretable and is not shown.
  • Set alignment: mean $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$ over each set's members, with $\bar g$ the mean probe gradient.

GGN and NTK sample kernels, and their overlap with a site

Label-free references on the gradient side (SPEC_DOT.md §2; data/kernels/compare_ggn.json). Per checkpoint: $J_i$ is the Jacobian of the logits with respect to all trainable parameters for image $i$ (eval-mode batch norm), $\Theta_{ij} = J_i J_j^\top$ the empirical NTK ($10 \times 10$ blocks), and $H_i = \mathrm{diag}(p_i) - p_i p_i^\top$ the softmax cross-entropy Hessian in the logits, which depends on the predicted probabilities $p_i$ and not on the label.

  • GGN kernel: $K_{ij} = \operatorname{tr}(H_i^{1/2} \Theta_{ij} H_j^{1/2}) = \langle H_i^{1/2} J_i, H_j^{1/2} J_j \rangle_F$, the dot product on the per-image GGN factor (summing the factors' outer products gives the GGN).
  • NTK kernel: $K_{ij} = \operatorname{tr}\,\Theta_{ij}$.
  • Cos form (GGN-cos, NTK-cos): the per-image features are centred, then normalised to unit length, as the site activations are; the resulting Gram goes through the same kernel rule, zero diagonal, Sinkhorn and lazy step as a site, giving an operator on the same $N$ images.
  • Overlap of a site with a reference: the subspace overlap of the leading $q$ nontrivial eigenfunctions of the site's single-snapshot operator and of the reference's operator, at the same checkpoint and rule, for $q = 4$ and $10$. The scale reference is $q/(N-1)$.
  • The loss-gradient-cos reference in the same file is built the same way from the cosine Gram of the $g_i$; it uses labels through the loss and is reported separately.

Decodability

A validation fit, not part of the construction: $10{,}000$ CIFAR training images ($1{,}000$ per class) fit, all $10{,}000$ test images evaluated (they include the {{fact:n_images}} probe images); features centred by the fit mean and L2-normalised; flattened maps and spatial channel means ("GAP") for convolutional sites. Closed-form ridge on one-hot targets, no intercept, $\lambda$ by generalised cross-validation on the fit set; weighted cosine $k$-NN with $k = 20$ and votes $\exp(\cos/0.07)$. Chance is $0.1$.

Per-image diagnostics

At each checkpoint, eval mode: cross-entropy loss, prediction, correctness; $\lVert \partial z / \partial x \rVert_F$, the Frobenius norm of the $10 \times 3072$ Jacobian of the logits $z$ with respect to the normalised input pixels; per-sample loss gradients $g_i$ with respect to all trainable parameters, $\bar g = \frac1N \sum_i g_i$ over the probe, and $g_i \cdot \bar g$, $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$, $\lVert g_i \rVert$. These use labels through the loss and never enter an operator.

Baseline sources and nulls

Definitions behind Appendix B and the explorer's Sets from control (from explore/cifar_baselines/PLAN.md and SCHEMA.md). All of it uses kernel rule {{fact:mode_name.rule0}}. Notation: $Z_l$ the centred, row-normalised activations of snapshot $l$, $C_l = Z_l Z_l^\top$ its cosine Gram, $H = I - \tfrac1N \mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$ the centring matrix, $C_B = \frac1L \sum_{l \in B} C_l$ the block Gram (the Gram of the concatenated per-snapshot features, up to the factor $1/L$), $q = r - 1$ non-constant directions, and $k_c = r$ sets for every source. Tags: S = uses the spectral construction, P = uses SEBA, B = a standard baseline, V = validation.

Sources

  • Cosine PCA + SEBA (S+P): the top $q$ eigenvectors of $H C_B H$, with the constant prepended, rounded by the unchanged SEBA. Classical MDS is the same computation here: for unit vectors the squared chordal distance is $d_{ij}^2 = 2 - 2C_{ij}$, and classical MDS diagonalises $-\tfrac12 H D^{(2)} H = H C H$, the doubly centred Gram, so its coordinates are the principal directions of $C$.
  • PCA + k-means (B): k-means with $k = r$ and $20$ starts on the scores $U_p \Lambda_p^{1/2}$, $p = \max(q, 20)$.
  • Spherical k-means (B): maximise $\sum_c \lVert \sum_{i \in c} z_i \rVert$ over partitions into $r$ sets (each image assigned to the centroid of largest cosine), evaluated through $C_B$; $20$ k-means++ starts.
  • Method eigenvectors + k-means (S+B): k-means on $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_q$; row-normalised: on the rows of $[\psi_0, \dots, \psi_q]$ scaled to unit length (Ng, Jordan and Weiss).
  • Textbook spectral clustering (S+B): $\frac1L \sum_l D_l^{-1/2} A_l D_l^{-1/2}$ with $A_l$ the snapshot's 0/1 union $15$-NN graph (no floor, no Sinkhorn, no lazy step); its leading eigenvectors, rows normalised, then k-means.
  • First / middle / last snapshot (S+P): the method on the single-snapshot block $(a, a)$, $(m, m)$ with $m = \lfloor (a+b)/2 \rfloor$, or $(b, b)$.
  • Co-assignment consensus (P): $A_B(i, j) = \frac1L \sum_l \mathbb 1[\ell_l(i) = \ell_l(j) \ne \text{unassigned}]$ with $\ell_l$ the single-snapshot sets at the same $r$; the top $q$ eigenvectors of $H A_B H$, then SEBA.
  • Mean-projector consensus (S+P): the top $q$ eigenvectors of $\frac1L \sum_l U_l U_l^\top$, $U_l$ the snapshot's own $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_q$, then SEBA.
  • Pixels, untrained network, other training seed (S+P): the method unchanged on the network's input, on the same block at step $0$, and on the same layer block of a second network (seed 1) at the final checkpoint.
  • Classes, predicted class (V): ten sets each; the prediction is taken at the block's checkpoint (layer block) or its last checkpoint (time block).

Nulls

  • Trajectory shuffle: $P_l \to \Pi_l P_l \Pi_l^\top$ with independent random permutations $\Pi_l$, $\Pi_l = I$ for the block's first snapshot. Draws are coherent: draw $t$ uses the same permutation of a snapshot in every block and rank. Within-class: each $\Pi_l$ permutes images only within their class.
  • Spectrum-matched Gaussian: per snapshot, Gaussian activations whose covariance has the nonzero eigenvalues of $H R_l H / N$ ($R_l = X_l X_l^\top$ the raw Gram), independent across snapshots, then the full pipeline. It keeps the spectrum in expectation and before the pipeline's centring and row normalisation, not after them.
  • Joint second-order surrogate: one Haar orthogonal $O$ ($N \times N$, $O\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1$) shared by the block's snapshots, $C^*_l = D_l O C_l O^\top D_l$ with $D_l$ diagonal restoring the unit diagonal. Before $D_l$ this keeps the mean and every within- and cross-snapshot second moment exactly; after $D_l$ it does not, and the stored diagnostic (relative change of $\operatorname{tr}(C_l C_m)$) changes by up to {{fact:bn.n2b_mm_max}} over the twelve representative blocks ({{fact:bn.n2b_mm_b4b6}} for b4 to b6).
  • Degree-preserving rewiring: double-edge swaps on each snapshot's $15$-NN graph before the floor, keeping every degree; then floor, Sinkhorn and the lazy step.
  • Random subspaces: SEBA on $[\mathbf 1/\sqrt N, V]$ with $V$ a Haar-random $N \times q$ orthonormal basis orthogonal to $\mathbf 1$; and SEBA started from random rotations of the real span.
  • Label permutation (for agreement scores): one labelling is permuted across the images, which keeps every set size and the unassigned count; for the joint AMI the permutation runs within the jointly assigned images. Any selection (a largest value over blocks) is repeated inside each permutation.

Transfer-operator pilot

Definitions for the pilot shown in Appendix A. For snapshots $a$, $b$ of the same images, $(Tf)(y) = \mathbb E[f(X) \mid Y = y]$ on the empirical marginals. A fitted estimate is $T = (C_{YY} + \rho_Y I)^{-1/2} C_{YX} (C_{XX} + \rho_X I)^{-1/2}$ on centred finite features (linear PCA scores, Nyström features of $e^{\kappa(x \cdot x' - 1)}$, or diffusion coordinates; $\rho = \eta\, \operatorname{tr}(C)/m$ is a ridge fit), or $T = D_q^{-1/2} B D_p^{-1/2}$ for the soft Ulam joint table $B$ of landmark memberships. Its singular vectors give source and destination functions; held-out correlation is their Pearson correlation on test images; VAMP-2 is the squared Frobenius norm of the test-split cross-covariance of the six fitted source and destination functions after whitening each side by its regularised test covariance (the tuned ridge; for soft Ulam none): $\lVert S_f^{-1/2} S_{fg} S_g^{-1/2} \rVert_F^2$, as transfer.py computes it. Composition discrepancy on $a \to b \to c$: $T_{bc}T_{ab} - T_{ac} = A_c^{-1/2} S_{cb} (A_b^{-1} - G_b^{+}) S_{ba} A_a^{-1/2} + A_c^{-1/2} (S_{cb} G_b^{+} S_{ba} - S_{ca}) A_a^{-1/2}$, the regulariser term plus the partial term, with $S$ the fit cross-moments, $A$ the regularised and $G$ the unregularised marginal moments (TRANSFER.md, section 5).

Distances between operators, across sites and networks

These compare the explorer's single-site operators with each other: different sites of one network, the same site at different checkpoints, and sites of networks of different depth (explore/spectral_align/SPEC.md). Every network sees the same {{fact:al.n_images}} probe images, so every operator lives on the same node set and two operators are compared directly, with no matching of images. Dynamic time warping is a comparison tool used on these distances; it is not part of the construction.

What is compared

  • Networks. ResNet-$(6n+2)$ for $n = 3, 5, 9$ (ResNet-20, -32, -56), training seeds 0 and 1: {{fact:al.n_nets}} networks trained with the same recipe, differing only in the number of blocks per stage. Final test accuracy: ResNet-20 {{fact:al.acc.r20s0.final}} and {{fact:al.acc.r20s1.final}}, ResNet-32 {{fact:al.acc.r32s0.final}} and {{fact:al.acc.r32s1.final}}, ResNet-56 {{fact:al.acc.r56s0.final}} and {{fact:al.acc.r56s1.final}} (seeds 0 and 1).
  • Sites. The stem, every block output, the pooled features and the logits: {{fact:al.sites.r20s0}}, {{fact:al.sites.r32s0}} and {{fact:al.sites.r56s0}} sites. The residual blocks fall into three stages.
  • Checkpoints. Steps {{fact:al.steps}} for every network; {{fact:al.n_cells}} snapshots in all.
  • Operator per snapshot. The explorer's construction for rules 0 and 1. $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_{64}$ are the eigenfunctions after the constant, in order of decreasing eigenvalue; $U_k = [\psi_1 \dots \psi_k]$.

Distances between two snapshots

For two snapshots $A$ and $B$ on the same $N$ images:

  • Projector distance $d_k(A,B) = 1 - \lVert U_k(A)^\top U_k(B)\rVert_F^2 / k \in [0, 1]$, one minus the subspace overlap: zero when the two leading-$k$ spans are equal, and $1 - k/(N-1)$ in expectation for two independent random spans (the chance line; {{fact:al.chance.k8}} at $k = 8$, {{fact:al.chance.k64}} at $k = 64$). $k \in \{${{fact:al.k_grid}}$\}$.
  • Diffusion-kernel distance $h_t(A,B) = 1 - \langle A_t, B_t\rangle_F / (\lVert A_t\rVert_F \lVert B_t\rVert_F)$ with $A_t = M_A^t - \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top/N$, over the whole spectrum: no cut; each mode is weighted by $\lambda^t$. $t \in \{${{fact:al.t_grid}}$\}$.
  • Eigenvalue-only distance $s_k = \lVert \lambda_{1..k}(A) - \lambda_{1..k}(B)\rVert_2/\sqrt{k}$. It ignores the eigenvectors: two operators with the same spectrum and unrelated eigenfunctions are at distance zero.
  • CKA distance (baseline) $1 - \mathrm{CKA}$ of the two centred cosine Grams (CKA).

Cut diagnostics

A leading-$k$ span is only well defined when the cut between modes $k$ and $k+1$ separates eigenvalues. For every snapshot the operator was rebuilt on two overlapping two-thirds subsets of the images (no labels used), and $\mathrm{span}_k$ is the smallest $\cos^2$ principal angle between the two rebuilds' leading-$k$ spans on the shared images. {{fact:al.subset_failures_note}}

  • Unresolved cut. A cut $k$ is unresolved for a pair of snapshots when $\mathrm{span}_k < 0.5$, or is missing, in either snapshot. At an unresolved cut, $d_k$ depends on how near-equal modes happen to be split; it measures the sensitivity of the comparison, not a difference between the two sites. The eigenvalue gap $\lambda_k - \lambda_{k+1}$ is reported beside it.
  • Stability horizon of a snapshot: the largest $k$ with $\mathrm{span}_k \ge 0.5$.
  • How unresolved cuts are drawn. In projector-distance heat maps a cell whose cut is unresolved is hatched and faded; in line charts an unresolved point is a hollow circle (resolved points are filled). Hovering shows $\lambda_k - \lambda_{k+1}$ and $\mathrm{span}_k$ of both snapshots. Distances without a cut are drawn plainly.
  • Unresolved share of a DTW path: the fraction of the path's cells whose cut is unresolved.

Missing snapshots. Under kernel rule 1, {{fact:al.missing.rule1.snapshots}} snapshots have no operator: the per-image perplexity calibration could not reach its target on these collapsed early-training snapshots ({{fact:al.missing.rule1.list}}). They are drawn as missing (white, crossed), and every comparison that needs them is left out of the rule-1 summaries: {{fact:al.missing.rule1.comparisons}} of {{fact:al.missing.rule1.comparisons_of}} cross-network comparisons.

Floors

  • Seed floor of a site: the distance between that site in the two seeds of the same depth, at the same checkpoint and kernel rule. The replicate is the training seed. Comparisons across depth have four seed pairings, shown as individual values, not averaged.
  • Seed floor of one side of a comparison: the same-depth cross-seed comparison at the same checkpoint. For a deeper network at its iso-accuracy checkpoint, the other seed at that seed's iso-accuracy checkpoint; for ResNet-20 in an iso-accuracy comparison, the other seed at the final checkpoint.
  • Floor along the path: at a matched pair of sites $(i, j)$, the mean of $A$'s floor at site $i$ and $B$'s floor at site $j$.

Shared initial draws. Networks of different depth trained with the same seed draw the initial weights of the stem and the first blocks identically (same random generator, same construction order), so at early checkpoints their stems and first blocks are partly the same function. Every cross-depth summary therefore uses the two different-seed pairings; the two same-seed pairings are shown only where labelled "same seed: shared initial draws".

Matching sites of two networks

For two networks at one checkpoint and one distance, $D$ is the site × site matrix of distances ($L_A \times L_B$).

  • DTW path. A monotone path from (stem, stem) to (logits, logits) with steps $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$, minimising the weighted sum of $D$ along it (diagonal steps count twice, the start once). The reported cost is that minimum divided by $L_A + L_B - 1$, the total weight of every such path, so the optimised and the reported quantity agree and no path length is favoured. DTW forces the match to be monotone; on a matrix with no structure every path ties.
  • Unconstrained matches. For each site of $A$ its nearest site of $B$ (row argmin), and the reverse (column argmin). The inversion fraction is the share of site pairs whose matches come in the opposite order: it says whether monotonicity is present in $D$ or only imposed by DTW.
  • Architectural reference. Block $j$ of stage $s$ in $A$ ($n_A$ blocks per stage) is paired with block $\lceil j\,n_B/n_A \rceil$ of stage $s$ in $B$; stem, pool and logits with themselves. Reported: the mean gap in relative depth between the DTW path and this reference (stage coordinate $s - 1 + j/n$, so a match in another stage counts as a large gap), and whether the path passes through the three stage ends.

References for a DTW cost. The permutation null: the optimum recomputed after randomly permuting the residual-block sites of $B$ (stem, pool and logits fixed; {{fact:al.n_perm}} permutations). The constant-matrix reference: the cost of any path on a matrix whose every entry is the mean of $D$. The seed floors: the DTW cost between the two seeds of each depth at the same checkpoint. The untrained reference: the same comparison at step $0$, so that a correspondence the architecture produces before any training is not attributed to learning. Same-depth cross-seed pairs, where the expected match is the diagonal, are included as pairs of their own.

Summaries over training

  • Settled (one site over training): the earliest checkpoint from which on the distance to the same site at the final checkpoint stays at or below that site's seed floor at the final checkpoint. For $d_k$ it is given only when the cut is resolved in the floor and at every checkpoint from then on; otherwise it is reported as unresolved, with the step of the latest unresolved cut.
  • Below the null from: the earliest checkpoint from which on the DTW cost is below the $5$th percentile of the permutation null in every seed pairing.
  • Pair sets of the rank correlations between distances: as in the caption of the rank-correlation figure in Appendix B.

Coarse-graining a block's coupling

Specification: SPEC_COARSE.md §1 and §2; every identity and bound below is checked numerically in test_coarse.py.

The coupling and its information

Every snapshot operator here is symmetric and doubly stochastic on the $N$ probe images, and so is an ordered product of them (doubly stochastic, generally not symmetric), so the uniform measure is stationary on both ends. A block's operator $Q$ defines a coupling $\pi(x, y) = Q[y, x]/N$:

  • single snapshot: $Q = M$ ($X$ and $Y$ are images at the same site; the reversible case);
  • layer chain $a \to b$ at one checkpoint: $Q = M_b \cdots M_{a+1} M_a$ ($X$ the image at site $a$, $Y$ the image at site $b$).

With the singular value decomposition $Q = \sum_i \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^\top$, $\sigma_1 = 1$ for the constant, the spectral embedding of the start side is $\Psi(x) = \sqrt N\,(\sigma_2 v_2(x), \sigma_3 v_3(x), \dots)$ (for a single snapshot $\sigma_i = \lambda_i(M) \ge 0$). Dependence is measured by the $\chi^2$ mutual information:

  • total: $I(X;Y) = \lVert Q \rVert_F^2 - 1 = \sum_{i \ge 2} \sigma_i^2$;
  • one-sided (start partitioned): for a partition $c$ of $X$ into blocks $A_1, \dots, A_k$ and $S_{jy} = \sum_{x \in A_j} Q[y, x]$, $I(c(X); Y) = \sum_{j,y} S_{jy}^2 / |A_j| - 1$;
  • two-sided (both ends partitioned, $c'$ of $Y$ into $B_1, \dots, B_l$): with $T_{jl} = \sum_{x \in A_j,\, y \in B_l} Q[y, x]$, $I(c(X); c'(Y)) = \sum_{j,l} T_{jl}^2 / (|A_j|\,|B_l|) - 1$.

$\chi^2$ information agrees with Shannon's to leading order near independence; it weights fine structure more heavily, and the two do not give the same values. A single snapshot has $I(X;Y) \ge N/4 - 1$ because of the lazy half of $M$.

The statements

  • Loss is within-block spread in the embedding (exact): using all $N - 1$ nontrivial modes, $$ I(X;Y) - I(c(X);Y) \;=\; \frac1N \sum_x \big\lVert \Psi(x) - \bar\Psi_{c(x)} \big\rVert^2 , $$ with $\bar\Psi_{c(x)}$ the mean of $\Psi$ over $x$'s block. Minimising within-block variance in $\Psi$ (k-means) is therefore the same problem as keeping the most one-sided information.
  • Ceiling (Eckart–Young): no $k$-block partition keeps more than the retention ceiling $F_k = \sigma_2^2 + \dots + \sigma_k^2$, since the coarse operator has rank at most $k$ including the constant. The same ceiling holds two-sided with $k' = \min(k, l)$. It is reached exactly when $v_2, \dots, v_k$ are constant on the blocks. $I(X;Y) - F_k$ is the loss floor.
  • Leakage (single snapshots, where $M$ is symmetric): the leakage of block $A$ is $\varphi(A) = 1 - \frac1{|A|} \sum_{x, y \in A} M[x, y]$, the per-step probability of leaving it, and $\mathrm{Ncut}(c) = \sum_j \varphi(A_j)$. Then $\mathrm{Ncut} \ge \sum_{i=1}^{k} (1 - \lambda_i)$ (Ky Fan) and $\max_j \varphi(A_j) \ge (1 - \lambda_k)/2$ (the lower side of the higher-order Cheeger inequality). Reported as the ratios $\mathrm{Ncut}$ / bound and $\max_j \varphi$ / bound. Because $M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, every leakage is half the leakage under $P$ and $1 - \lambda_i(M) = \tfrac12(1 - \lambda_i(P))$, so the ratios do not depend on the lazy half.

Reported quantities

  • Retained information: the absolute $I(c(X);Y)$ (single snapshots; start and end agree because $M$ is symmetric) or $I(c(X); c'(Y))$ (chains), shown with the total $I(X;Y)$ or as a fraction of it, beside the random-partition reference at the same $k$ and the ceiling.
  • $k_{\text{eff}}$: the number of non-empty groups a partition actually has (empty groups reduce $k$; for example, predictions at step $0$ occupy fewer than ten classes). Ceilings are $F_{k_{\text{eff}}}$.
  • Efficiency $\eta = I(c(X);Y) / F_{k_{\text{eff}}}$: a tightness diagnostic only. On the lazy uniform walk $\tfrac12\big(I + (J - I)/(N - 1)\big)$, which has no geometry, $\eta = 1$ for every partition, so a high $\eta$ alone says nothing about grouping.
  • Partitions scored, for every block and $k \in \{2, \dots, 16, 20, 24, 32\}$: spectral k-means (Lloyd on the top $k - 1$ coordinates of $\Psi$, uniform weights, k-means++ with $8$ restarts and a fixed seed, scored with the full-operator formulas); SEBA at $r \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16\}$ with every image assigned to its largest SEBA vector (these analysis assignments ignore the SEBA threshold and differ from the explorer's sets, which keep "unassigned"); PCA k-means on the top $50$ principal components of the activations at each end (a baseline with no operator in it); random balanced partitions ($4$ draws, independent at the two ends of a chain); and the classes and predictions as validation rows. Labels and predictions never select $k$, a mode or a rule.
  • Without the lazy half: single snapshots are also scored on $P = 2M - I$, whose singular values are $|\lambda_i(P)|$ ordered by magnitude, with the same formulas.
  • Matched agreement (chains): end blocks are matched to start blocks by maximising matched mass (Hungarian), and $\sum_j T[B_j, A_j]/N$ is reported beside its independence expectation $\sum_j \mu(A_j)\nu(B_j)$ for the same matching. It is an agreement score; the coherence quantity is the two-sided information against its ceiling.

Spectral gap criterion

A gap "after $k$" is between the $k$-th and $(k+1)$-th singular value, counting $\sigma_1 = 1$ for the constant, so it corresponds to $k$ blocks and the ceiling $F_k$; $k$ ranges over $2, \dots, 32$. Single snapshots use the Laplacian scale, the ratio $(1 - \sigma_{k+1})/(1 - \sigma_k)$, because the lazy half compresses ratios of $\sigma$; chains use $\sigma_k / \sigma_{k+1}$. Ratios with a denominator below $10^{-8}$ are left out. The gap is clear when the largest ratio is at least $1.15$ and more than $1.1$ times the second largest (coarse_summary.py, block_gap). The criterion is descriptive and is never used to choose anything downstream.

Markov quantities across layers

Specification: SPEC_COARSE.md §6; tests in test_markov.py. Every quantity is exact, computed from the operators, Shannon, in nats.

The process

At one checkpoint and kernel rule, $Z_{-1}$ is uniform on the $N$ probe images and $Z_s$ is drawn from $M_s(\cdot \mid Z_{s-1})$ for the sites $s$ = stem, b1, …, logits in order, so $(Z_s, Z_{s+1})$ has coupling $M_{s+1}/N$ and $(Z_a, Z_b)$ has coupling $M_b \cdots M_{a+1}/N$. $Z$ is Markov by construction. Each site carries its own partition $c_s$, and the coarse process is $C_s = c_s(Z_s)$. Because $M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, an image stays put with probability $\tfrac12$ at each step, so image identity carries memory across sites unless the partitions at different sites are aligned; this is part of what is measured, and it is not corrected.

Measures, as normalised fractions

  • One-step defect fraction (defect_frac): $\delta_s / I(Z_s; C_{s+1})$ with $\delta_s = I(Z_s; C_{s+1} \mid C_s) = I(Z_s; C_{s+1}) - I(C_s; C_{s+1})$. It is $0$ exactly when $P(C_{s+1} \mid Z_s = x)$ is constant on each block of $c_s$ (strong lumpability of the step), and $1$ when the current block carries none of the information the exact image carries about the next block.
  • Coarse memory fraction (mem_frac): $I(C_{s+2}; C_s \mid C_{s+1}) / H(C_{s+2} \mid C_{s+1})$, the share of the uncertainty about the next block, given the current block, that the previous block removes. A positive value shows the coarse process is not exactly Markov. Zero adjacent-triple values and zero CK errors do not certify that it is, because longer histories are not tested. The fine version $I(C_{s+2}; Z_s \mid C_{s+1})$ (memfine_frac, same denominator) is a diagnostic of information hidden from the coarse state; it can be positive when the coarse process is exactly Markov, so it never rejects closure.
  • Chapman–Kolmogorov error (ck): for sites $a < b - 1$, the true coarse transition $T_{\text{true}} = P(C_b \mid C_a)$ against the composition $T_{\text{ck}}$ of the one-step coarse transitions from $a$ to $b$; error $= \sum_i P(C_a = i)\, \mathrm{TV}(T_{\text{true}}[\cdot, i], T_{\text{ck}}[\cdot, i])$. Reported per horizon $h = b - a \ge 2$ as the mean over start sites, beside ck_ind, the same weighted TV distance of $T_{\text{true}}$ from the independent prediction $P(C_b)$: how much there is to get wrong. One-step pairs are zero by construction and are left out.
  • Alignment of the sequence: the normalised mutual information (arithmetic mean of the two entropies) of neighbouring sites' partitions on the same images.
  • Undefined values. A fraction whose denominator is below $10^{-12}$ is undefined; the export writes it as $-1$, and charts draw it as a gap, never as a value.

Partition sequences and the control

  • Each site's own partition: spectral k-means of that site's single-snapshot operator ($k = 2, \dots, 16$).
  • Shared partition: b9's spectral k-means used at every site, fixed in the specification before any result. It separates "the partition changes from site to site" from "the walk moves images between blocks".
  • PCA k-means per site (baseline); independent random balanced partitions per site ($4$ draws; a reference with no alignment across sites); the classes at every site ($k = 10$; validation only).
  • Lazy uniform walk control: every partition sequence is also run on $U = \tfrac12\big(I + (J - I)/(N - 1)\big)$ at every site. $U$ keeps the stay-put mechanism (probability $\tfrac12$) and removes the geometry of the moves that leave an image: on $U$ every other image is equally likely. With a partition shared by every site the control's defect is exactly $0$. A defect of similar size on the control shows that the stay-put mechanism alone can produce a defect of that size; it does not split the real defect into parts, since the two walks are different processes.
