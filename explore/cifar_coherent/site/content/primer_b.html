From activations to a neighbour graph

At one layer of one checkpoint, each probe image is a point: its activation vector. I link each image to the images it is most similar to, measured by cosine similarity (activations centred, then scaled to unit length). The dot product is the only geometric assumption.

What the site actually uses instead of plain "nearest neighbours"

The links are weighted by a kernel $K_{ij}$ of the cosine distance, with no self-links. The default rule gives every image a comparable neighbourhood (a 15-nearest-neighbour graph or a per-image bandwidth); a single global bandwidth fails in the middle layers. See Per-snapshot operator and Choosing the kernel.

A random walk on the graph

"The layer treats these images the same" becomes: the walk mixes quickly among them and leaks out slowly.

$$ P = \mathrm{diag}(v)\,K\,\mathrm{diag}(v), \qquad \textstyle\sum_j P_{ij} = \sum_i P_{ij} = 1 $$

The textbook walk is $D^{-1}K$ (divide each row by its total). I instead rescale rows and columns together (Sinkhorn) so every row and every column sums to 1; then every layer and checkpoint has the same uniform "resting" distribution, which is what lets them be averaged later.

Slowest modes: Laplacian eigenfunctions

An eigenfunction assigns a number to every image. The slowest ones are the smoothest assignments: linked images get similar numbers, so the value can only change where links are thin.

$$ \tfrac12 \sum_{i,j} P_{ij}\,(f_i - f_j)^2 \;\text{ small}, \qquad P\,\psi_k = \lambda_k\,\psi_k $$

Why this is principled, and how many groups

  • Nothing is trained and no labels are used. Every step is a similarity, a rescaling and an eigendecomposition; class labels are only used afterwards, to check the groups.
  • Standard mathematics. This is spectral clustering and diffusion maps (Shi and Malik; Ng, Jordan and Weiss; Coifman and Lafon), with decades of theory behind it. Once activations are collected and compared, the eigenproblem has one row per probe image, not per neuron or parameter.
  • Two main resolution controls: the neighbourhood scale (the kernel rule; it matters) and the number of groups I ask for. Several slow non-constant modes suggest candidate groups, and a gap helps choose how many; it does not dictate a unique answer.
One more detail: the lazy walk

The site uses $M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, a walk that stays put half the time. It has the same eigenvectors as $P$ and eigenvalues $\tfrac12(1 + \lambda)$ in $[0, 1]$, so a graph that is nearly two-coloured (eigenvalue near $-1$) is not mistaken for a slow mode.
