Exact definitions and settings for everything on this site. Exploratory side study: one model, one seed, not a stage of the project's experiment plan.

Model, probe, sites

  • Model. ResNet-20 (ReLU), the ModelZoo resnet20/relu-seed0 recipe retrained on Modal; 64,000 steps, validation accuracy 0.9156 at the last step. 73 checkpoints saved; the explorer uses eight: steps 0, 64, 256, 1,024, 2,560, 6,400, 25,600, 64,000. Eval mode (BatchNorm running statistics), no augmentation.
  • Probe. $N = 2{,}000$ CIFAR-10 test images, 200 per class, drawn with numpy.RandomState(0). The same images at every checkpoint and site.
  • Sites ($S = 12$): stem $=\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{BN}(\mathrm{conv}_1(x)))$; b1 to b9, the post-ReLU outputs of the nine residual blocks; pool, the global average of b9 over space (64 dims); logits (10). Convolutional sites are flattened ($C \times H \times W$: 16,384 dims for stem to b3, 8,192 for b4 to b6, 4,096 for b7 to b9).
  • A cell is a (checkpoint, site) pair.

Per-cell operator

For cell $c$ with activations $h_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$:

$$ \hat h_i = \frac{h_i - \bar h}{\lVert h_i - \bar h\rVert}, \quad \bar h = \tfrac1N \textstyle\sum_i h_i, \qquad C_{ij} = \langle \hat h_i, \hat h_j \rangle, \qquad d_{ij} = 1 - C_{ij}. $$

(A zero centred vector would stay zero, giving that image cosine 0 with every other; it does not occur here.)

$K$ is a symmetric non-negative kernel with $K_{ii} = 0$, given by one of five kernel rules, e.g. $K_{ij} = \exp(-\kappa\, d_{ij})$ for the single-temperature rules.

Sinkhorn. Find $u$ with $\sum_j \exp(\log K_{ij} + u_i + u_j) = 1$ for all $i$ (symmetric scaling, $v = e^u$), in the log domain in float64: ten fixed-point steps $u \leftarrow u - \tfrac12 \log(\text{row sums})$, then line-searched Newton steps on the convex objective $\tfrac12\sum_{ij} e^{\log K_{ij} + u_i + u_j} - \sum_i u_i$. A cell counts only if $\max_i |\text{row sum}_i - 1| < 2 \times 10^{-12}$; otherwise the measurement fails. (coherent.symmetric_balance; up to ten fixed-point warm-ups, Newton only if needed.) Then set

$$ P = \mathrm{diag}(v)\, K\, \mathrm{diag}(v), \qquad M = \tfrac12 (I + P). $$

$P$ is symmetric, non-negative, zero on the diagonal, with unit row and column sums. For any $f$, $f^\top (I - P) f = \tfrac12 \sum_{ij} P_{ij} (f_i - f_j)^2$, and $M$ has eigenvalues $\tfrac12(1 + \lambda_P) \in [0, 1]$ with the eigenvectors of $P$.

Why Sinkhorn, why a zero diagonal, why $(I + P)/2$
  • Sinkhorn instead of degree normalisation. With per-cell degree normalisation ($D^{-1/2} K D^{-1/2}$, with or without Coifman-Lafon $\alpha = 1$), each cell has its own stationary vector $\propto \sqrt{\text{degree}}$, so an average over cells has no exact trivial mode and differences in density leak into the spectrum. A doubly stochastic $P$ has the uniform measure in every cell: $\psi_0 = \mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ with eigenvalue exactly 1 for every block. (Doubly stochastic normalisation for spectral clustering: Zass and Shashua 2006; robustness to heteroskedastic noise: Landa, Coifman and Kluger 2021.)
  • Zero diagonal. This is a stated variant of the project's theory (THEORY.md, Section 3.1, keeps $K_{ii} = 1$). With the diagonal kept, the median self-weight $M_{ii}$ of the Sinkhorn operator is 1.0000 to four decimals in b1 to b8 at step 64,000 under the calibrated half-mass rule (0.0016 to 0.0017 under kNN scale; meta.json self_weight) in the trained middle layers under the half-mass rules: those operators are within about $10^{-8}$ of the identity, and a block average is dominated by its least lazy cells.
  • $(I + P)/2$. Fixes every cell's self-mass at exactly one half, and shifts the spectrum into $[0, 1]$ so that an eigenvalue of $P$ near $-1$ (alternation across a nearly bipartite graph) is not read as a slow mode. It equalises laziness only; gaps and mixing rates still differ between cells and are part of what is measured.
  • How much the change mattered (normalisation_compare.json, step 64,000, all 78 layer blocks, mean $\cos^2$ of principal angles between leading spans with the constant projected out; computed for the two single-temperature rules only). Under kNN scale the normalisation barely matters: median 1.000 against both the old $\alpha = 1$ operator and diagonal-retained Sinkhorn (lowest 0.43 and 0.997 for $r = 3$). Under calibrated half-mass the versions share almost nothing: median 0.001 ($r = 3$) and 0.007 ($r = 6$) against $\alpha = 1$, and 0.001 and 0.008 against diagonal-retained Sinkhorn, as expected when every version's leading vectors sit on (different) close pairs.

Kernel rules

$d_{ij} = 1 - C_{ij}$, $k = 15$, all kernels with $K_{ii} = 0$. $d_k$ = median over images of the distance to the $k$-th nearest other image. The sweep: why one temperature fails in the middle layers

Candidates (data/r20s0b/sweep.json, produced by sweep_math.py): one global $\kappa \in \{2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30\}$; $\kappa$ calibrated to a median 15-NN mass share of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5; per-image perplexity 15, 30, 100, 300; kNN graphs with $k$ = 15, 50. Cells: b2, b5, b8, pool, logits at steps 256, 6,400, 64,000. Each cell's operator is computed alone ($M = \tfrac12(I + P)$, Sinkhorn with Newton refinement to row residual $< 2 \times 10^{-12}$), with the participation number $\mathrm{PN}(\psi) = 1/\sum_i \psi_i^4$ of $\psi_1..\psi_8$, $1 - \lambda_1..\lambda_8$, and SEBA sets for $r = 4, 8$ (sizes, and Hungarian-matched Jaccard with the classes, computed after the fact as validation).

In the trained middle layers the images are nearly equidistant (b1 to b8 at step 64,000: median 15-NN distance 0.81 to 0.86, median pairwise distance 1.00; data/r20s0/stats_64000.json). A kernel flat enough to connect images broadly is nearly linear in $C$, and to first order in $\kappa$ its modes are principal components of $\hat h$. A kernel sharp enough to put half the mass on 15 neighbours lets a few close pairs take most of their rows after Sinkhorn scaling, and the leading eigenvectors localise on those pairs. At step 64,000, in b2, b5 and b8: global $\kappa \in \{2, 3, 5\}$ gives $1 - \lambda_1$ = 0.39 to 0.47; from $\kappa = 12$ upward, $\mathrm{PN}(\psi_1)$ is 2.0 to 2.1 in b5 and b8; half-mass calibrated has $\kappa$ = 26 to 43, median PN 2.0 to 2.1, and SEBA-8 sets of 1 or 2 images. At the tested cells and settings the per-image rules give $\mathrm{PN}(\psi_1)$ = 890 to 1,450 in all five final cells, smallest SEBA-8 set $\ge 86$, and $1 - \lambda_1$ = 0.05 to 0.07 (kNN graph), 0.10 to 0.12 (perplexity 100), 0.13 to 0.17 (perplexity 300) in the middle layers; perplexity 15 and 30 still give a 2-image SEBA set in some cell. Pool and logits give spread modes under every rule.

Blocks

  • Layer blocks: sites $a..b$ at one checkpoint, all $0 \le a \le b < 12$ (78 per checkpoint). Time blocks: checkpoints $c..d$ of the eight at one site (36 per site), equal weight per listed checkpoint (the checkpoints are roughly geometrically spaced in steps, so early training is weighted heavily per step).
  • Block operator $\bar M = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} M_c$. Eigenpairs by a dense float64 symmetric eigensolver, descending; $\lambda_0 = 1$, $\psi_0 = \mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ (not stored); $\psi_1..\psi_8$ stored with unit norm, sign fixed so the largest-magnitude entry is positive, quantised to int16 relative to $\max_i |\psi_k(i)|$.
  • Retention of a function $f$ in cell $c'$: $\rho_{c'}(f) = f^\top M_{c'} f / f^\top f$, stored for $\psi_1..\psi_8$ and every SEBA vector, for every cell of the checkpoint (layer blocks) or every checkpoint of the site (time blocks), inside and outside the block. Identity: $\lambda_k = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} \rho_c(\psi_k)$.
  • Single-cell blocks ($a = b$ or $c = d$) are ordinary spectral clustering / diffusion maps of that cell ("static"). The averaging follows Froyland's dynamic Laplacian (Froyland 2015; Froyland and Junge 2018) and Banisch and Koltai's space-time diffusion maps (2017), here with Sinkhorn-normalised kernels and uniform weight on each image's trajectory.

SEBA sets

Sparse Eigenbasis Approximation (Froyland, Rock and Sakellariou 2019, Algorithm 3.1), ported from the authors' reference code (github.com/gfroyland/SEBA) and cross-checked against it in the tests. Input $V = [\psi_0, \psi_1, \dots, \psi_{r-1}]$ (constant included), $r \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}$, $\mu = 0.99/\sqrt N$. Orthonormalise $V$ (QR); starting from $R = I$, repeat until $\lVert R_\text{new} - R \rVert \le 10^{-14}$ (at most 5,000 iterations):

$$ Z = VR, \quad S_{:j} = \frac{\operatorname{sign}(Z_{:j}) \odot (|Z_{:j}| - \mu)_+}{\lVert \operatorname{sign}(Z_{:j}) \odot (|Z_{:j}| - \mu)_+ \rVert}, \quad R_\text{new} = \operatorname{polar}(V^\top S) = U W^\top \ \text{where}\ V^\top S = U \Sigma W^\top . $$

The rotated vectors $VR$ lie in the span; the returned thresholded vectors $S$ generally do not, and are sparse approximations to it. Then flip each column of $S$ so its sum is positive, divide by its maximum, order columns by decreasing minimum entry ("reliability"), and clip negative entries to 0. Hard label of image $i$: $\arg\max_j S_{ij}$ if $\max_j S_{ij} > 0$, else unassigned. Colour: set $j$ (1-based, reliability order) takes the $j$-th set colour; unassigned is grey. The rotation and permutation are stored, so the full vectors (not just the labels) are reconstructed in the browser for loadings and retention.

What is and is not canonical

  • Given the cell choices, $\kappa$ and the normalisation, $\bar M$ and its spectrum are well defined.
  • An individual $\psi_k$ is defined only up to sign, and only when $\lambda_k$ is separated from its neighbours; inside a group of close eigenvalues only the span is meaningful. SEBA uses the span of the leading $r$, so it depends on where $r$ cuts the spectrum.
  • Sign convention, set numbering (reliability order) and colours are conventions.
  • Not canonical: the choice of sites, centring over this probe set, cosine (drops the norm), the kernel rule, and the equal weights within a block.

Average versus product

The title's "cross-Hankel" is an analogy. For a time series $y$, a Hankel matrix has entries $H_{ij} = y_{i+j}$: rows are windows of the past, columns windows of the future; for linear systems its leading singular vectors give a small state through which the past influences the future. For a layer block $a < b$, the chronological product $Q = M_b \cdots M_a$ (functions are column vectors; layer $a$ applied first) is the nearest cross-layer object and the one the project's theory builds on: its right singular vectors are functions at layer $a$, its left singular vectors their normalised images at layer $b$ (Froyland's coherent pairs). The explorer uses $\bar M$. To first order in the step size the two agree: if $M_c = I - \varepsilon L_c$, then $Q^\top Q = I - 2\varepsilon \sum_c L_c + O(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\bar M = I - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{|B|}\sum_c L_c$, so they share leading vectors up to $O(\varepsilon)$ when the relevant eigenvalues of $\sum_c L_c$ are simple. Beyond first order they differ ($(\tfrac12(M_a + M_b))^2$ contains $\tfrac14(M_aM_b + M_bM_a)$, while $Q^\top Q$ does not average). avgprod.json reports, separately for the kNN-graph and perplexity-100 rules, with the constant projected out of both sides, the mean $\cos^2$ of principal angles between $\mathrm{span}(\psi_1..\psi_r)$ of $\bar M$ and the top-$r$ non-trivial right and left singular vectors of $Q$ and of the two-end product $M_b M_a$, $r \in \{3, 6\}$. At step 64,000, $r = 3$, over the 66 layer pairs: kNN graph, median 0.989 (lowest 0.836) for the right and 0.991 (0.881) for the left singular vectors of $Q$, and 0.973 (0.632) and 0.975 (0.641) for the two-end product; perplexity 100, 0.993 (0.645) and 0.995 (0.649), and 0.979 (0.664) and 0.981 (0.661). With $r = 6$ the medians are 0.96 to 0.99.

Layouts (display only)

  • Per checkpoint: umap.AlignedUMAP across the 12 sites (identity relations between consecutive sites), n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.1, alignment_regularisation=0.02, random_state=0, on the top 50 principal components ($u_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}$ from the eigendecomposition of $C$) of each cell.
  • Across checkpoints, per site: centre, scale to unit RMS, orthogonal Procrustes (reflections allowed) onto the previous checkpoint, then rescaled to the 0.5 and 99.5 percentiles for drawing. The within-checkpoint alignment is part of the UMAP objective and shapes the layouts; across checkpoints, Procrustes removes rotation and reflection after centring and unit-RMS scaling, and percentile rescaling then sets the display coordinates.
  • No quantity is computed from the 2-d coordinates. Distances in a layout are not distances in the operator.
  • Seed 1. A second ResNet-20, identical recipe, training seed 1; only its activations' top-50 PCs and its own AlignedUMAP and Procrustes layouts are computed, for the same probe images and steps. Colours always come from seed 0's blocks. Seed 1's layouts are aligned along its own checkpoints, not onto seed 0's.

Per-image diagnostics

At each checkpoint, eval mode: cross-entropy loss, prediction, correctness; $\lVert \partial z / \partial x \rVert_F$, the Frobenius norm of the $10 \times 3072$ Jacobian of the logits $z$ with respect to the normalised input pixels; per-sample loss gradients $g_i$ with respect to all trainable parameters, $\bar g = \frac1N \sum_i g_i$ over the probe, and $g_i \cdot \bar g$, $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$, $\lVert g_i \rVert$. These use labels through the loss and never enter an operator.

Channel loadings

Target $f$: the selected $\psi_k$, or a full SEBA vector (not winner-masked). For a cell with channel matrix $X \in \mathbb R^{N \times C}$ (spatial mean of each channel, columns centred; pool = b9's channel means; logits = the 10 logits): Pearson $\mathrm{corr}(f, X_{:c})$ per channel; $R^2$ of in-sample ordinary least squares of centred $f$ on $X$ (descriptive, not held out; chance about $C/N$, at most $64/2000 = 3.2\%$). Alignment with the classifier: $\mathrm{corr}(f,\ z_c - \tfrac1{10}\sum_{c'} z_{c'})$ for each class $c$. Spatial means discard where a channel fired; the operators use the full maps.

Questions-tab statistics

  • Set overlap. Hard SEBA sets $\{A_i\}$, $\{B_j\}$ (unassigned in no set); $J_{ij} = |A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$; Hungarian matching maximising total $J$; score = mean matched $J$ over $\min(r_A, r_B)$ pairs. Null: permute the labels of $A$ (keeps set sizes and the unassigned count), 200 permutations, mean and 2.5/97.5 percentiles.
  • Gradient coherence. From the cosine Gram of per-sample gradients: mean cosine over pairs in the same set minus mean over pairs in different sets (assigned images only), for a block's sets, for the classes, and for size-matched random sets (100 permutations; pairs sharing an image are not independent, so the band is the measured spread of this null, not a standard error).
  • Decodability (validation fit, not part of the construction): 10,000 CIFAR training images (1,000 per class) fit, all 10,000 test images evaluated (they include the 2,000 probe images); features centred by the fit mean and L2-normalised; flattened maps and spatial channel means ("GAP") for convolutional sites. Closed-form ridge on one-hot targets, no intercept, $\lambda$ by generalised cross-validation on the fit set; weighted cosine $k$-NN, $k = 20$, votes $\exp(\cos/0.07)$.

Exact definitions and the JSON layout are in the docstring of site/analysis.py.

Limitations

  • One model, one seed for everything measured; one probe sample of 2,000 test images; no resampling check of the sets.
  • Cosine on centred activations; norm discarded; centring depends on the probe.
  • The kernel rule was chosen on this network (label-free diagnostics); in the middle layers no single $\kappa$ gave spread, slow modes.
  • $\bar M$ is an average over cells, not a product; high $\lambda$ does not mean present in every cell.
  • Eigenvectors inside near-degenerate groups are not individually meaningful; SEBA depends on $r$.
  • Labels are used to draw a class-balanced probe; after that, classes, losses and gradients only colour and validate, and never define a kernel or a basis.
