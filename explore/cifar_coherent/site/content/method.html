The reference tab for Neural Coherent Sets, my construction for finding the groups of inputs a neural network treats alike across layers and training: every definition and setting, in the order the pipeline runs. The Overview gives the procedure in five steps; this tab gives the exact version of each step, how I chose the kernel, and how to rebuild everything.

Glossary

  • Probe image. One of the $N = {{fact:n_images}}$ CIFAR-10 test images fed to the network; the same images, indexed $i = 1..N$, are used at every checkpoint and site.
  • Site. A place in the network where activations are recorded: stem, the outputs of residual blocks b1 to b9, pool, or logits ({{fact:n_sites}} in all).
  • Checkpoint. One of the {{fact:n_checkpoints}} saved training steps used here: {{fact:steps}}.
  • Snapshot. A (checkpoint, site) pair. Its data are the $N$ activation vectors of the probe images at that site and checkpoint. In the explorer grid each snapshot is drawn as one panel.
  • Activation cell. A region of a layer's activation space on which the rest of the network computes nearly the same thing (after an observation by Stefan Heimersheim and collaborators); on this site "cell" means only this.
  • Block. A run of consecutive snapshots whose operators are averaged. A layer block is sites $a..b$ at one checkpoint; a time block is checkpoints $c..d$ at one site.
  • Single-snapshot block. A block of one snapshot ($a = b$ or $c = d$). Its eigenfunctions are ordinary spectral clustering (diffusion maps) of that snapshot alone.
  • Kernel rule. The recipe that turns a snapshot's cosine distances into a symmetric, non-negative weight matrix $K$ with zero diagonal. There are five (table); the default is the {{fact:default_mode}}.
  • Walk $P_c$. The Sinkhorn scaling $\mathrm{diag}(v)\,K\,\mathrm{diag}(v)$ of snapshot $c$'s kernel: symmetric, non-negative, zero on the diagonal, every row and column summing to 1.
  • Lazy walk $M_c$. $M_c = \tfrac12(I + P_c)$: stay put with probability one half, otherwise take a step of $P_c$. Same eigenvectors as $P_c$, eigenvalues in $[0, 1]$.
  • Block operator $\bar M$. The equal-weight average $\bar M = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} M_c$ over the snapshots of block $B$.
  • Eigenfunction $\psi_k$. The $k$-th unit eigenvector of $\bar M$ in decreasing order of eigenvalue, read as a function on the probe images ($\psi_k(i)$ for image $i$). $\psi_0 = \mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ is the constant.
  • Spectrum. The eigenvalues $1 = \lambda_0 \ge \lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots$ of $\bar M$. For a unit eigenfunction, $1 - \lambda_k = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} \psi_k^\top (I - M_c)\, \psi_k$, the average lazy penalty over the block's snapshots, which is half the average walk penalty $\psi_k^\top (I - P_c)\, \psi_k$ of (1). For SEBA with $r$ sets the relevant gap is $\lambda_{r-1} - \lambda_r$, between the last eigenvalue used and the first one left out.
  • Retention $\rho_c$. For a function $f$ and any snapshot $c$, inside or outside the block, $\rho_c(f) = f^\top M_c f / f^\top f \in [0, 1]$. A block eigenvalue is the mean retention of its eigenfunction over the block: $\lambda_k = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} \rho_c(\psi_k)$.
  • SEBA vector. One of the $r$ sparse vectors that SEBA returns from $\mathrm{span}(\psi_0, \dots, \psi_{r-1})$: soft-thresholded, sign-fixed, scaled to a maximum of 1, with negative entries clipped to 0. It approximates the span but generally does not lie in it.
  • Coherent set. The images at which SEBA vector $j$ is the largest of the $r$ and positive (the hard assignment). These are candidates for groups the network treats alike across the block, and are numbered by reliability.
  • Unassigned. An image at which all $r$ SEBA vectors are 0 after clipping; it belongs to no set and is drawn grey.
  • Matched Jaccard. For two collections of hard sets (unassigned images in no set): the Jaccard overlap $|A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$ of every pair, a one-to-one matching that maximises the total, and the mean over matched pairs. Its null band comes from permuting one side's labels (details).

Model, probe, sites

  • Model. ResNet-20 (ReLU), the ModelZoo resnet20/relu-seed0 recipe retrained on Modal; {{fact:final_step}} steps, validation accuracy {{fact:final_val_acc}} at the last step. 73 checkpoints saved; the explorer uses {{fact:n_checkpoints}}: steps {{fact:steps}}. Eval mode (BatchNorm running statistics), no augmentation.
  • Probe. $N = {{fact:n_images}}$ CIFAR-10 test images, 200 per class, drawn with numpy.RandomState(0). Drawing a class-balanced probe is the only use of labels before the sets exist; after that, classes, losses and gradients only colour plots and validate.
  • Sites ($S = {{fact:n_sites}}$): stem $=\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{BN}(\mathrm{conv}_1(x)))$; b1 to b9, the post-ReLU outputs of the nine residual blocks; pool, the global average of b9 over space (64 dims); logits (10). Convolutional sites are flattened ($C \times H \times W$: 16,384 dims for stem to b3, 8,192 for b4 to b6, 4,096 for b7 to b9).

Per-snapshot operator

For snapshot $c$ with activations $h_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$:

$$ \hat h_i = \frac{h_i - \bar h}{\lVert h_i - \bar h\rVert}, \quad \bar h = \tfrac1N \textstyle\sum_i h_i, \qquad C_{ij} = \langle \hat h_i, \hat h_j \rangle, \qquad d_{ij} = 1 - C_{ij}. $$

(A zero centred vector would stay zero, giving that image cosine 0 with every other; it does not occur here.) $K$ is a symmetric non-negative kernel with $K_{ii} = 0$, given by one of five kernel rules, for example $K_{ij} = \exp(-\kappa\, d_{ij})$ for the single-temperature rules.

Sinkhorn. Find $u$ with $\sum_j \exp(\log K_{ij} + u_i + u_j) = 1$ for all $i$ (symmetric scaling, $v = e^u$), in the log domain in float64: up to ten fixed-point steps $u \leftarrow u - \tfrac12 \log(\text{row sums})$, then, if needed, line-searched Newton steps on the convex objective $\tfrac12\sum_{ij} e^{\log K_{ij} + u_i + u_j} - \sum_i u_i$. A snapshot counts only if $\max_i |\text{row sum}_i - 1| < 2 \times 10^{-12}$; otherwise the measurement fails (coherent.symmetric_balance). Then

$$ P = \mathrm{diag}(v)\, K\, \mathrm{diag}(v), \qquad M = \tfrac12 (I + P). $$

$P$ is symmetric, non-negative, zero on the diagonal, with unit row and column sums. For any $f$,

$$ f^\top (I - P)\, f \;=\; \tfrac12 \sum_{i,j} P_{ij}\,(f_i - f_j)^2 , $$

small when $f$ is nearly constant on images the snapshot weights as similar, and $M$ has eigenvalues $\tfrac12(1 + \lambda_P) \in [0, 1]$ with the eigenvectors of $P$.

Why Sinkhorn, why a zero diagonal, why $(I + P)/2$
  • Sinkhorn instead of degree normalisation. With per-snapshot degree normalisation ($D^{-1/2} K D^{-1/2}$, with or without Coifman-Lafon $\alpha = 1$), each snapshot has its own stationary vector $\propto \sqrt{\text{degree}}$, so an average over snapshots has no exact trivial mode and differences in density leak into the spectrum. A doubly stochastic $P$ has the uniform measure in every snapshot: $\psi_0 = \mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ with eigenvalue exactly 1 for every block. (Doubly stochastic normalisation for spectral clustering: Zass and Shashua 2006; robustness to heteroskedastic noise: Landa, Coifman and Kluger 2021.)
  • Zero diagonal. This is a stated variant of the project's theory (THEORY.md, Section 3.1, keeps $K_{ii} = 1$). With $K_{ii} = 1$ kept, the median diagonal entry of the Sinkhorn-scaled kernel over b1 to b8 at step {{fact:final_step}} is {{fact:self_weight.rule4.final.mid_median}} (to the stored precision) under the calibrated half-mass rule, against {{fact:self_weight.rule3.final.mid_median}} under kNN scale (meta.json self_weight): those half-mass operators are within about $10^{-8}$ of the identity, and a block average would be dominated by its least lazy snapshots.
  • $(I + P)/2$. Fixes every snapshot's self-mass at exactly one half, and shifts the spectrum into $[0, 1]$ so that an eigenvalue of $P$ near $-1$ (alternation across a nearly bipartite graph) is not read as a slow mode. It equalises laziness only; gaps and mixing rates still differ between snapshots and are part of what is measured.
  • How much the normalisation changes the result (normalisation_compare.json, step {{fact:final_step}}, all 78 layer blocks, mean $\cos^2$ of principal angles between leading spans with the constant projected out; computed for the two single-temperature rules only). Under kNN scale: median 1.000 against both the $\alpha = 1$ operator and diagonal-retained Sinkhorn (lowest 0.43 and 0.997 for $r = 3$). Under calibrated half-mass: median 0.001 ($r = 3$) and 0.007 ($r = 6$) against $\alpha = 1$, and 0.001 and 0.008 against diagonal-retained Sinkhorn.

Choosing the kernel

Nothing in the network fixes the kernel, and on this network it changes the answer more than any other choice. In the trained middle layers the images are nearly equidistant: in b1 to b8 at step {{fact:final_step}} the median cosine distance from an image to its 15th nearest neighbour, $d_{15}$, is 0.81 to 0.86, against a median pairwise distance of 1.00 (data/r20s0/stats_64000.json). Even near neighbours are not near.

One temperature fails in the middle layers, in two ways. A kernel flat enough to connect images broadly is nearly linear in $C$, and to first order in $\kappa$ its modes are principal components of $\hat h$: kNN scale, $\kappa = 1/d_{15}$, gives $\kappa \approx {{fact:kappa.knn_scale.final.mid_median}}$ there, spread modes, and a weak leading one ($1 - \lambda_1$ = 0.39 to 0.47 in b2, b5 and b8 for global $\kappa$ from 2 to 5). A kernel sharp enough to put half the mass on 15 neighbours lets a few unusually close pairs outweigh everything else: after Sinkhorn scaling each such image sends nearly all its probability to its partner, and $\psi_1$ sits on one pair (participation number 2.0 to 2.1 from $\kappa = 12$ upward in b5 and b8; half-mass picks $\kappa$ = 26 to 43 there). No single $\kappa$ in between gives modes that are both spread and slow.

Rules that adapt to each image work here. A 15-nearest-neighbour graph and t-SNE-style affinities with a per-image $\kappa_i$ give every image a comparable neighbourhood, so a single close pair cannot take over its row. In every final snapshot tested, $\psi_1$ is spread (participation number 890 to 1,450) and no 8-set SEBA partition has a set smaller than 86 images; in the middle layers $1 - \lambda_1$ is 0.05 to 0.17. Per-image rules are not immune: perplexity 15 and 30 still give a 2-image set in some snapshot. At pool and logits, where the images are far more clustered, every rule gives spread modes.

So the default is the {{fact:default_mode}}. Perplexity 100 and 300 are offered too, and kNN scale and half-mass stay selectable as references for the two failure modes. The choice used label-free diagnostics only. Checked afterwards, the per-image rules' sets also agree best with the classes in the middle layers (b8: matched Jaccard 0.43 to 0.44, against 0.34 to 0.35 for global $\kappa$ = 2 to 5).

Blocks and retention

  • Layer blocks: sites $a..b$ at one checkpoint, all $0 \le a \le b < 12$ (78 per checkpoint). Time blocks: checkpoints $c..d$ of the eight at one site (36 per site), equal weight per listed checkpoint (the checkpoints are roughly geometrically spaced in steps, so early training gets more weight per step).
  • Block operator $\bar M = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} M_c$, equivalently $\tfrac12(I + \bar P)$ with $\bar P$ the mean walk. The penalty is linear in the walk, so
    $$ f^\top (I - \bar P)\, f \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} f^\top (I - P_c)\, f , $$
    and the leading eigenfunctions of $\bar M$ are the unit functions orthogonal to the constant and to each other with the smallest average penalty over the block. Since $I - \bar M = \tfrac12 (I - \bar P)$, a unit eigenfunction has $1 - \lambda_k = \tfrac12\, \psi_k^\top (I - \bar P)\, \psi_k$. No map between layers or between checkpoints is used.
  • Eigenpairs by a dense float64 symmetric eigensolver, descending; $\lambda_0 = 1$, $\psi_0 = \mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ (not stored); $\psi_1..\psi_8$ stored with unit norm, sign fixed so the largest-magnitude entry is positive, quantised to int16 relative to $\max_i |\psi_k(i)|$.
  • Retention $\rho_{c'}(f) = f^\top M_{c'} f / f^\top f$ is stored for $\psi_1..\psi_8$ and every SEBA vector, for every snapshot of the checkpoint (layer blocks) or every checkpoint of the site (time blocks), inside and outside the block. Identity: $\lambda_k = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c \in B} \rho_c(\psi_k)$. A high $\lambda_k$ is a high average, not high retention in every snapshot; the retention strip shows each snapshot's value.
  • Single-snapshot blocks ($a = b$ or $c = d$) are ordinary spectral clustering / diffusion maps of that snapshot. For longer blocks, Neural Coherent Sets builds on two constructions for flows: Froyland's dynamic Laplacian (Froyland 2015; Froyland and Junge 2018) and Banisch and Koltai's space-time diffusion maps (2017), here with Sinkhorn-normalised kernels and uniform weight on each image's trajectory; in those works averaging over time finds coherent sets of a flow, and here the "time" axis is depth or training.

SEBA sets

Sparse Eigenbasis Approximation (Froyland, Rock and Sakellariou 2019, Algorithm 3.1), ported from the authors' reference code (github.com/gfroyland/SEBA) and cross-checked against it in the tests. Input $V = [\psi_0, \psi_1, \dots, \psi_{r-1}]$ (constant included), $r \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8\}$, $\mu = 0.99/\sqrt N$ (just under the entry size of an evenly spread unit vector). Orthonormalise $V$ (QR); starting from $R = I$, repeat until $\lVert R_\text{new} - R \rVert \le 10^{-14}$ (at most 5,000 iterations):

$$ Z = VR, \qquad S_{:j} = \frac{\operatorname{sign}(Z_{:j}) \odot (|Z_{:j}| - \mu)_+}{\lVert \operatorname{sign}(Z_{:j}) \odot (|Z_{:j}| - \mu)_+ \rVert}, $$ $$ R_\text{new} = \operatorname{polar}(V^\top S) = U W^\top, \qquad \text{where } V^\top S = U \Sigma W^\top . $$

The rotated vectors $VR$ lie in the span; the returned thresholded vectors $S$ generally do not, and are sparse approximations to it. Then flip each column of $S$ so its sum is positive, divide it by its maximum, order the columns by decreasing minimum entry (reliability), and clip negative entries to 0. Hard label of image $i$: $\arg\max_j S_{ij}$ if $\max_j S_{ij} > 0$, else unassigned. Set $j$ (1-based, reliability order) takes the $j$-th set colour; unassigned is grey. The rotation and permutation are stored, so the browser reconstructs the full vectors (not just the labels) for retention.

What is and is not canonical

  • Given the snapshots, the kernel rule and the normalisation, $\bar M$ and its spectrum are well defined.
  • An individual $\psi_k$ is defined only up to sign, and only when $\lambda_k$ is separated from its neighbours; inside a group of close eigenvalues only the span is meaningful. SEBA uses the span of the leading $r$, so it depends on where $r$ cuts the spectrum: the gap $\lambda_{r-1} - \lambda_r$ says how sharp that cut is.
  • Sign convention, set numbering (reliability order) and colours are conventions.
  • Choices that could be made otherwise: the sites, centring over this probe set, cosine (which drops the norm), the kernel rule, and equal weights within a block.

Average versus product

The project's theory builds on a product, not an average. For a layer block $a < b$, the chronological product $Q = M_b \cdots M_a$ (functions are column vectors; layer $a$ applied first) is the nearest cross-layer object: its right singular vectors are functions at layer $a$, its left singular vectors their normalised images at layer $b$ (Froyland's coherent pairs). The title's "cross-Hankel" is an analogy to this: for a time series $y$, a Hankel matrix $H_{ij} = y_{i+j}$ has windows of the past as rows and of the future as columns, and for linear systems its leading singular vectors give a small state through which the past influences the future; $Q$ plays that role between layer $a$ and layer $b$.

The explorer uses $\bar M$. To first order in the step size the two agree: if $M_c = I - \varepsilon L_c$, then $Q^\top Q = I - 2\varepsilon \sum_c L_c + O(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\bar M = I - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{|B|}\sum_c L_c$, so they share leading vectors up to $O(\varepsilon)$ when the relevant eigenvalues of $\sum_c L_c$ are simple. Beyond first order they differ: $(\tfrac12(M_a + M_b))^2$ contains $\tfrac14(M_aM_b + M_bM_a)$, while $Q^\top Q$ does not average.

avgprod.json reports, for the kNN-graph and perplexity-100 rules, with the constant projected out of both sides, the mean $\cos^2$ of principal angles between $\mathrm{span}(\psi_1..\psi_r)$ of $\bar M$ and the top-$r$ non-trivial right and left singular vectors of $Q$ and of the two-end product $M_b M_a$, $r \in \{3, 6\}$. At step {{fact:final_step}}, $r = 3$, over the 66 layer pairs, median (lowest): kNN graph, 0.989 (0.836) for the right and 0.991 (0.881) for the left singular vectors of $Q$, and 0.973 (0.632) and 0.975 (0.641) for the two-end product; perplexity 100, 0.993 (0.645) and 0.995 (0.649), and 0.979 (0.664) and 0.981 (0.661). With $r = 6$ the medians are 0.96 to 0.99.

Reading the layouts and colourings

The explorer grid has one row per checkpoint and one column per site; each panel is a 2-d layout of the $N$ probe images in one snapshot (UMAP), and each image is one dot in every panel. The layouts are for display only: nothing is computed from them, and a set that looks scattered in a layout can be compact in the operator, or the reverse.

A colouring is computed from the selected block only (outlined) and painted on every panel, inside and outside the block, so a block's functions and sets can be followed into other sites and checkpoints.

  • Coherent sets (SEBA, rank $r$): set $j$ in the $j$-th set colour ( set 1, set 2, ...), unassigned grey. Clicking a set in the legend dims the others.
  • Eigenfunction $\psi_k$: diverging colour of $\psi_k(i) / \max_i |\psi_k(i)|$, blue negative, grey near zero, red positive. Nodal domains: the sign of $\psi_k$ alone. Signs follow the convention above.
  • Validation colourings (labels, losses or gradients; none enters an operator): CIFAR class; correct or wrong; cross-entropy loss and input-Jacobian norm (log scale, fixed across rows from the 1st to 99th percentile over all checkpoints); gradient alignment $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$ and $g_i \cdot \bar g$ (diverging; the dot product is scaled by the 99th percentile of its magnitude). Each is taken at the row's own checkpoint, or at the final checkpoint for every row (definitions).

The side panels describe the selected function (one $\psi_k$, all sets, or one set):

  • Spectrum: $\lambda_1..\lambda_8$ of $\bar M$, with $\mathrm{PN}(\psi_k) = (\sum_i \psi_k(i)^2)^2 / \sum_i \psi_k(i)^4$ under each; a mode with $\mathrm{PN} < 0.01N$ is marked localised. With a SEBA colouring the eigenvalues of $\psi_1..\psi_{r-1}$ are drawn dark.
  • Retention strip: $\rho_c$ of the selected function in every snapshot of the block's checkpoint (layer block) or site (time block); the block's snapshots are shaded, and for $\psi_k$ a dashed line marks $\lambda_k$, their mean.
  • Legend: each set's size and its class make-up as a bar (validation). Matched Jaccard of the sets against the classes and against a second block of the same family, kernel rule and $r$, each with the mean of a 30-draw permutation null.
  • Representative images: for $\psi_k$, the 12 images with the most positive values, the 12 most negative, and the 12 nearest zero. For a set, its members by decreasing SEBA value ("highest membership"), and its members with the smallest lead of their own SEBA value over the largest of the other $r - 1$ ("boundary members").

Layouts (display only)

  • Per checkpoint: umap.AlignedUMAP across the 12 sites (identity relations between consecutive sites), n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.1, alignment_regularisation=0.02, random_state=0, on the top 50 principal components ($u_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}$ from the eigendecomposition of $C$) of each snapshot.
  • Across checkpoints, per site: centre, scale to unit RMS, orthogonal Procrustes (reflections allowed) onto the previous checkpoint, then rescale to the 0.5 and 99.5 percentiles for drawing. The within-checkpoint alignment is part of the UMAP objective and shapes the layouts; across checkpoints only rotation, reflection, centring and scale change.
  • No quantity is computed from the 2-d coordinates. Distances in a layout are not distances in the operator.
  • Seed 1 ("layout from"). A second ResNet-20, identical recipe, training seed 1; only its activations' top-50 PCs and its own AlignedUMAP and Procrustes layouts are computed, for the same probe images and steps. Colours always come from seed 0's blocks. Seed 1's layouts are aligned along its own checkpoints, not onto seed 0's.

Per-image diagnostics

At each checkpoint, eval mode: cross-entropy loss, prediction, correctness; $\lVert \partial z / \partial x \rVert_F$, the Frobenius norm of the $10 \times 3072$ Jacobian of the logits $z$ with respect to the normalised input pixels; per-sample loss gradients $g_i$ with respect to all trainable parameters, $\bar g = \frac1N \sum_i g_i$ over the probe, and $g_i \cdot \bar g$, $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$, $\lVert g_i \rVert$. These use labels through the loss and never enter an operator.

Statistics behind the Measurements tab

Defaults unless a chart says otherwise: the {{fact:default_mode}} and $r = 4$. Class labels and probe pixels only describe and validate here; none of these statistics enters an operator. Exact definitions and the JSON layout are in the docstrings of site/analysis.py and site/analysis_findings.py.

Matched Jaccard, null and size ceiling

Hard labelings $A$ ($r_A$ sets) and $B$ ($r_B$ sets; 10 for the classes), unassigned images in no set: $J_{ij} = |A_i \cap B_j| / |A_i \cup B_j|$ (0 if the union is empty); Hungarian matching maximising total $J$ over $\min(r_A, r_B)$ pairs; score = mean matched $J$. Null: permute the label vector of $A$, unassigned entries included (keeps $A$'s set sizes and unassigned count), 200 permutations from one shared seeded bank; stored as the mean $\mu_0$, the 2.5 / 97.5 percentiles and the maximum. Size ceiling (against a fixed reference labeling): the same Hungarian mean over $\min(|A_i|, |B_j|) / \max(|A_i|, |B_j|)$, the largest score any sets of these sizes could reach. The explorer's legend uses the same score with a 30-draw null.

  • When (q_when): for each site and checkpoint, matched Jaccard of that single-snapshot block's sets with the same site's sets at the final checkpoint, and with the classes.
  • Carry-over (q_carry): at each checkpoint, matched Jaccard between the single-snapshot sets of every pair of sites (no null), of adjacent sites (with null), and of adjacent layer blocks of width 2 and 3.

Class make-up

For the default rule, every layer block and every time block, and $r \in \{2, 3, 4\}$: the $(r + 1) \times 10$ table of counts $n_{jc} = \#\{\text{images in SEBA set } j \text{ with class } c\}$, rows in reliability order, with a last row for the unassigned images. The charts show it for single-snapshot blocks as a heatmap with a checkpoint slider (colour range 0 to 200, the images per class). Per-class placement (at $r = 2$): for each class $c$ and snapshot, the fraction of $c$'s 200 images in the set matched to $c$'s super-class (below), and the fraction unassigned.

Vehicles against animals at $r = 2$

$V$ = {airplane, automobile, ship, truck} (800 images), $A$ = the other six classes (1,200). For every kernel rule and single-snapshot block (and, for the default rule, every time block), the two $r = 2$ SEBA sets are compared with the 2-set labeling $(V, A)$:

  • score: their matched Jaccard (for $2 \times 2$, the better of the two pairings), with null mean $\mu_0$, null band and null maximum over the 200 permutations, and size ceiling as above;
  • norm_excess $= (\text{score} - \mu_0) / (\text{ceiling} - \mu_0)$: 0 at the null mean, 1 when the sets are as aligned with $(V, A)$ as their sizes allow;
  • agree: under the matching, the fraction of assigned images whose set is matched to their own super-class; vehicle fraction of set $j$: the share of $V$ among its members.

Milestones per site, over the {{fact:n_checkpoints}} checkpoints $t$, with $T$ the last:

  • half_step: the first $t$ such that $\text{norm\_excess}(t') \ge 0.5$ at every $t' \ge t$;
  • most_step: the first $t$ such that $\text{score}(t') - \mu_0(t') \ge 0.8\,(\text{score}(T) - \mu_0(T))$ at every $t' \ge t$ (80% of the final excess over the null mean, kept from then on); undefined if the final score does not exceed its null maximum.
  • The permutation onset (the first $t$ whose score exceeds its null maximum at every later checkpoint, a one-sided Monte Carlo permutation $p$ of $1/201$, the smallest 200 draws can give) is step {{fact:q.va.onset_step.all_sites}} at every site here, so the charts report half_step and most_step.

Pixel baseline (a closed-form comparison, not part of the method): for each snapshot, each of the 12 image statistics below and each sign, assign the top $n_1$ images by the signed statistic to set 1 and the bottom $n_2$ to set 2 ($n_j$ = that snapshot's SEBA set sizes; the rest unassigned), and score the split against $(V, A)$ the same way. pixel_best is the largest of these 24 scores. It is chosen using the labels, so it is an upper reference for single-statistic splits of these sizes. Best class bipartition (default rule): over all 511 splits of the 10 classes into two groups, the one whose labeling has the largest matched Jaccard with the $r = 2$ sets, and the rank of $(V, A)$ among them (1 = best).

Image statistics

From the probe pixels ($32 \times 32 \times 3$, scaled to $[0, 1]$, no normalisation), twelve statistics per image, with $Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B$ (Rec. 601 luma of the gamma-encoded values):

  • mean_r, mean_g, mean_b: channel means over the 1,024 pixels; luminance: mean of $Y$; contrast: standard deviation of $Y$ (RMS contrast); saturation: mean over pixels of $(\max - \min) / \max$ of $(R, G, B)$ (0 where $\max = 0$).
  • bc_r, bc_g, bc_b, bc_lum: mean over the border ring (the 448 pixels within 4 px of the edge) minus mean over the central $16 \times 16$ pixels, for $R$, $G$, $B$ and $Y$ (a background proxy); bc_dist: the Euclidean norm of (bc_r, bc_g, bc_b).
  • hf_frac: with $F$ the 2-D DFT of $Y - \bar Y$, the share of $\sum |F|^2$ at frequency radius above 8 cycles per image (half the Nyquist radius) among all non-zero frequencies.

For each block (every single-snapshot block; layer blocks stem..logits, b1..logits, b5..pool and b7..pool at every checkpoint; time blocks over all checkpoints and over the later half at every site), 17 functions: $\psi_1..\psi_8$ and the SEBA vectors for $r = 2, 3, 4$.

  • Correlation: Pearson correlation over the $N$ probe images between a function and a statistic. The sign for $\psi_k$ follows the stored sign convention; only $|\mathrm{corr}|$ is sign-free.
  • Set contrast: for hard set $j$ ($r = 2, 3, 4$), the mean of the standardised statistic over set $j$ minus its mean over all other images (unassigned included), in units of the statistic's SD over the $N$ images.
  • Within-class correlation: the Pearson correlation after subtracting, from both the function and the statistic, their mean over the image's class. Class make-up alone cannot produce it: it measures whether $f$ tracks the statistic among images of the same class.
  • $R^2$ of the fit (a closed-form least-squares fit): $R^2$ of $\psi_k$ regressed on the 12 standardised statistics plus an intercept. Its null permutes the images (same bank as below); stored per block as the 95th percentile pooled over $k = 1..8$ (expected null mean about $\text{rank}/N$, the 12 statistics having rank {{fact:q.img.rank}}; largest over blocks {{fact:q.img.r2_null_hi_max}}).
  • Class contrasts (labels describe the statistics only): mean standardised statistic over class $c$ minus over the other classes, and over $V$ minus over $A$.

Thresholds. One bank of 1,000 image permutations is applied to the statistics, the same draws for every block. Per block: pointwise = 95th percentile of $|\text{null corr}|$ pooled over the $17 \times 12$ entries (a two-sided 5% level for one entry); familywise = 95th percentile over draws of the largest $|\text{null corr}|$ among the block's $17 \times 12$ entries; familywise for $\psi$, per statistic = the same over $\psi_1..\psi_8$ only; the same pointwise and familywise pair for the set contrasts. Global = 95th percentile over draws of the largest $|\text{null corr}|$ over every block and entry ({{fact:q.img.thr_global}} here). The within-class correlations have their own null, permuting images within each class (a second bank of 1,000), with familywise thresholds per block and per statistic over $\psi_1..\psi_8$.

Gradient coherence

$G_t$ is the cosine Gram of per-sample loss gradients at checkpoint $t$. For the sets of a time block at one site (all checkpoints, or the later half), at each $t$: mean of $G_t$ over ordered pairs $i \ne j$ in the same set, minus the mean over assigned pairs in different sets (pair-weighted, unassigned excluded). The same for the 10 classes. Null: permute set labels among the assigned images, 100 permutations; the band is the measured 2.5 / 97.5 percentile range (pairs sharing an image are not independent, so it is not a standard error). Set alignment: mean $\cos(g_i, \bar g)$ over each set's members.

Class-union reference (b9; $r = 2$ and 4; both time blocks). Give each class wholly to the set that holds most of its assigned images. This labels images by unions of classes, merging classes as the sets do but cutting none. The primary reference computes the same within-minus-between statistic for these union labels on the images the sets assign, so its population is the same as the sets' own statistic (unassigned images excluded). The reported quantity is the ratio of the sets' statistic to this reference at the final checkpoint. Comparing the two separates "the sets merge classes" from "the sets cut across classes"; the reference is needed because the gradients carry the one-hot label. A secondary version applies the union labels to all $N$ images, a different population from the sets'.

Decodability

A validation fit, not part of the construction: 10,000 CIFAR training images (1,000 per class) fit, all 10,000 test images evaluated (they include the {{fact:n_images}} probe images); features centred by the fit mean and L2-normalised; flattened maps and spatial channel means ("GAP") for convolutional sites. Closed-form ridge on one-hot targets, no intercept, $\lambda$ by generalised cross-validation on the fit set; weighted cosine $k$-NN, $k = 20$, votes $\exp(\cos/0.07)$.
