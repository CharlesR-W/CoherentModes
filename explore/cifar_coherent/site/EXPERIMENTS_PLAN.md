## 2. The experiments

Order is for the reader: what the groups are, where and when they form, what blocks reflect, how they relate to
training, the coarse-grained descriptions, other networks, and finally pixel statistics.

**Editorial rule (review point 7).** Each experiment states **one measured answer** (X2 states three, one per
operationalization, at the coordinator's request) and has **one lead figure**. Any other panel stays inline only if
it changes the answer or rules out the strongest alternative; this plan names the reason for each one it keeps.
Everything else goes to App 4 (Reference charts) or the appendix named. Answers are bounded to what was tested:
ResNet-20, seed 0 unless stated, the 2,000-image label-stratified probe, eight checkpoints, the kNN rule (rule 0)
unless stated.

Every experiment section follows one template (section 4): **Question** (h2) · **Why it is interesting** · **How I
measure it** (operationalization, assumptions, justification) · **Controls and nulls** · **Answer** (one sentence of
facts, then the lead figure) · **Further evidence** (only the kept panels) · **Caveats** · **Open checks** · **In the
explorer** (one screenshot, one link line).

Cost labels: **json** = re-plot existing JSON (seconds); **local** = new computation from local files, under ~5 min
on this box. No Modal runs in this pass (coordinator decision).

Scale conventions every writer must respect: archive spectra (`layer_*.npz` `vals`, `coarse_summary` `sv` for
cells) are **lazy** eigenvalues λ_M of M = (I+P)/2; `bn-spectra` plots **λ_P = 2λ_M − 1**. Any chart mixing them
converts explicitly and says which in the caption. Stored SEBA labels: `seba_label[mode, pair, ri, n]` (int8, −1 =
unassigned) with ri indexing `meta.seba_r` = [2, 3, 4, 6, 8] (`schema_v2.py`); use these, never membership
reconstructed from the int16 `vecs`.

### X1 `x-groups` — What groups of images does the last stage of the network treat alike at the end of training?
- **Why.** The point of the method is to find, without labels, groups of inputs a network processes the same way.
  The last stage is the closest the network gets to its decision, so it is the natural first look: how many images
  the groups hold, how persistent they are across the stage's layers, and how they sit against the ten classes the
  network was trained on.
- **Operationalization.** SEBA sets of the layer block b7..pool at the final checkpoint, rule 0, r = 4. r is a
  resolution, not a discovered number (no gap dominates: facts `q.f1.b7_pool.final.r{2,3,4}.gap_after_*`).
  Persistence = retention of each SEBA vector in each snapshot of the block. Classes only validate: matched Jaccard
  against the ten classes with the size ceiling. Which block carries the null test: **b7..b9**, a representative
  block fixed before class outcomes (B), whose trajectory-shuffle and Gaussian draws are stored; **b7..pool has no
  stored null draws**, so no null claim is made about it.
- **Answer (one sentence, facts).** Four sets of sizes `q.f1.b7_pool.final.r4.set{1..4}.size` with
  `…n_unassigned` unassigned, each retaining ≥ `…retention_min` in every snapshot of the block; against the classes
  they reach matched Jaccard `…vs_classes` of a size ceiling `…vs_classes_ceiling`.
- **Lead figure.** Class make-up heatmap `Q:q_classmix.named.layer_t7_b7-pool_r4` (rows: sets in reliability order,
  then unassigned). The four live image grids go in a collapsed "the sets' images" disclosure under it.
- **Further evidence (kept inline).**
  1. *Spectra side by side* (rules out "the leading modes are what any shuffled data give"): b7..pool and b7..b9 at
     the final step, both on the λ_P = 2λ_M − 1 scale, same y axis, null bands (trajectory shuffle, Gaussian) **only
     on b7..b9**, from `r20s0b/layer_64000.npz` `vals` and `r20s0c/rep_b7-b9_nulls_draws.npz`; local.
  2. *Overlap across ranks* (addresses "four is my choice"): contingency of `seba_label` at r = 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 for
     pair (7, 10), unassigned as its own row/column, drawn as an alluvial or stacked bars, with the conditional
     entropy H(r | r') beside it; the plot must show whether the sets refine or reshuffle, not assume it; local.
- **To appendix.** Rule robustness bars (`vs_rule1..4`) and readout comparison (`vs_b9_alone`, `vs_b9_time_late`)
  → App 1 ablations section, with their permutation nulls (local); `bn-matrix` b7–b9 → App 2.

### X2 `x-when` — When in training does each layer's grouping take its final form?
- **Why.** If the groups are learned they appear at some point in training; when, and whether deep layers lead or
  follow, says whether the network builds its coarse organisation before it is accurate or after.
- **Three operationalizations, three separate answers** (never merged into one sentence):
  - **(a) Agreement with the final sets (label-free, the headline).** Matched Jaccard of each site's single-snapshot
    r = 4 sets with the same site's final sets, permutation null; milestone `q.when.r4.<site>.first_step` (first
    checkpoint above 0.5 and above the null's 97.5th percentile) and `…settled_step`.
  - **(b) Alignment to a class reference (validation).** r = 2 sets against the bipartition {airplane, automobile,
    ship, truck} / other six. This reference was chosen **after** seeing the final split, so it is post hoc. The
    pointwise permutation test is exceeded at step 0 at every site (`q.va.onset_step.all_sites` = 0), so it dates
    nothing; the reported milestone is `half_step` = first checkpoint from which the **normalised excess** (0 = null
    mean, 1 = size ceiling) stays ≥ 0.5, i.e. half of the size-adjusted possible excess, *not* half of the final
    alignment. The rank among 511 bipartitions describes where this post-hoc reference ranks; it is neither a
    prespecification nor a selection-adjusted test, and the text says so. Strongest alternative: image statistics;
    control: best single pixel-statistic split of the same sizes (an upper reference chosen with labels).
  - **(c) Operator distance to the final operator (label-free).** Projector distance against the two-seed floor (AL
    `w2`), reported per site from `al.w2.r20s0.rule0.<distance>.settled`, **including "n/a (cut unresolved)"**. At
    k = 9 most sites have an unresolved floor cut and b7, b9, pool, logits settle only at the final checkpoint; at
    k = 4 settling ranges from step 2,560 (stem) to 64,000. The old AL reading "every site reaches the seed floor
    before the first learning-rate drop" is not supported by the facts and is dropped.
- **Answers (facts only).** (a) per-site `first_step` (e.g. b1 and b2 at 1,024, b3 at 2,560; write all from facts);
  (b) normalised excess ≥ 0.5 sustained from step 64 at b7–logits, 1,024 at b6, 2,560 at b3–b5, never at stem–b2
  (`q.va.<site>.half_step`); (c) per-site `settled` at the resolved cut named in the text, with unresolved sites
  listed as such.
- **Lead figure.** `Q:q_when.0_4.lines_vs_final` (answer a), with validation accuracy from `curves.json` as a thin
  strip on the same x axis (json).
- **Further evidence (kept inline, one per remaining answer).**
  1. Answer (b): normalised excess lines `Q:q_va.by_mode.0.lines_norm_excess`, with b9's pixel-baseline score drawn
     on the matching score panel (`Q:q_va.by_mode.0.score`, `Q:q_va.pixel_baseline.score`) because it rules out the
     strongest alternative (colour) after step 0; json.
  2. Answer (c): live `al-w2` set to k = 4 (resolved in most snapshots) with hollow markers for unresolved cuts.
- **To appendix (App 4).** 511-rank heatmap/lines, per-class placement (`Q:q_va.class_placement`), the q_when
  heatmap and vs-classes lines, `Q:q_classmix.cells.2.b9` slider.
- **Open checks.** Denser checkpoints (73 saved) to place milestones within 0–64 and 256–1,024 (not run here).

### X3 `x-depth` — Where along depth does the network redraw its grouping?
- **Why.** If most layers carry the grouping forward and a few redraw it, those few are where the network changes
  what counts as similar: a map of the network by what it does rather than by its architecture.
- **Operationalization.** Matched Jaccard between adjacent sites' single-snapshot r = 4 sets at the final step and
  at step 0, with the permutation null per pair (`Q:q_carry.0_4.adjacent`, `.adjacent_null`). Step 0 is the
  control: shortcuts and image statistics alone make neighbours agree there. Stage boundaries (stride 2) are named
  on the axis. Second measure, operator level: adjacent projector distance at a resolved cut (k = 3) against the
  seed floor; sets at r = 4 and spans at k can disagree, and the plot shows both rather than choosing.
- **Answer (facts).** Adjacent agreement at the end ranges `q.carry.r4.final.adjacent_min`–`…max` (null ≤
  `…adjacent_null_hi_max`), lowest at `…adjacent_min_pair`; the mean over adjacent pairs is
  `q.carry.r4.step0.mean_adjacent` at step 0 and `q.carry.r4.step64000.mean_adjacent` at the end.
- **Lead figure.** Adjacent-pair profile (x = stem→b1 … pool→logits): final and step-0 set agreement with null
  bands; json from `Q:q_carry.0_4.adjacent(_null)`.
- **Further evidence (kept).** The operator-level profile on the same x axis: adjacent d_3 with the seed-floor band
  and unresolved cuts hollow, from `SA/pairs_0.npz` (`proj`, k index per the study's cut list) and `SA/cells.json`;
  local. Kept because it rules out "the low points are an artefact of hard sets at r = 4" (or shows they are).
- **To appendix (App 4).** The site × site matrix `Q:q_carry.0_4.matrix`, `al-w1`, width-2/3 blocks (`q_carry.*.w2/w3`).
- **Open checks.** The harmonicity intervention (`harmonic.py`, REFRAME_PLAN.md design v3): whether set boundaries
  mark sharp changes in the downstream computation. Not run in this pass.

### X4 `x-blocks` — Which layer's structure does a whole-path block reflect?
- **Why.** Averaging walks from layer a to the output asks what the network treats alike along the whole path. Which
  single layer that answer most resembles tells whether one stretch of the network dominates the path.
- **Operationalization.** For each start site a, the leading span of the averaged block a..logits compared with each
  single site c's leading span: mean cos² of principal angles of the leading k = 1, 2, 3 nontrivial eigenvectors
  (`layer_64000.npz` `vecs` × `scale`, rule 0; local). Single-mode (k = 1) matches are reported only where the
  1|2 cut is resolved; otherwise only the span (k = 3) result is stated. Resolution: eigenvalue gap λ1 − λ2 of both
  operators and, for single sites, `span_1` from the alignment cut diagnostics (≥ 0.5). Stated up front, by
  construction: the block's leading function retains less at a than a's own (it maximises mean retention), so the
  information is in *which* c it resembles.
- **Answer (facts, new).** For each a, the site c of largest span overlap and its value, with resolved/unresolved
  flags (new facts `x.blocks.*`).
- **Lead figure.** Heatmap start site a × site c of span overlap at k = 3, cells hatched where k = 1 would be
  unresolved; local.
- **Further evidence (kept).** `static_dynamic.png` (b1..logits vs b1), as the worked example; retention profiles
  (`Q:findings_support.f5`) go in its disclosure.
- **To appendix.** Average vs product (C `avgprod`, `product-blocks`, `product_summary.json`) → App 1: no
  order-specific test exists. If the owner later wants one (e.g. ordered vs reversed chain on non-symmetric data),
  it becomes an open check here.

### X5 `x-training-signal` — Do the images grouped alike in activations also have similar loss gradients?
- **Why.** Activations say what the network treats alike; per-image loss gradients say how training would move the
  whole network for each image. Agreement between the two would mean the activation groups are also groups for the
  learning signal; disagreement would mean they are organised differently.
- **What the gradients are.** Whole-model gradients: per-image cross-entropy gradient over **all trainable
  parameters** (`measure_v2.py`, `vmap(grad(loss))`), one cosine Gram per checkpoint (`gradgram_<step>.npy`). They
  do not depend on the site. Comparing sets from different sites changes only the **grouping**; no chart is
  layer-local, and the text says so.
- **Operationalization (label-dependent).** Within-minus-between gradient cosine on the **assigned images only**,
  for (i) the time-block sets and (ii) their class-union grouping (each class wholly to the set holding most of it)
  **on the same assigned images**, shown as two series, not only as a ratio. Nulls: set-label permutation among
  assigned images; and a **within-class null** that permutes set labels within each class, preserving each class's
  per-set counts and the unassigned mask (local, from `gradgram_<step>.npy` with `analysis.grad_within_between`
  logic). Ratios are shown only where the class-union difference exceeds its own null's 97.5th percentile; many
  site-level differences are ~0.003 at the final step (`q.grad.r2.<site>.all.final.sets_diff`), where a ratio is
  not interpretable.
- **Operationalization (label-free reference, kept separate).** Overlap (mean cos² of principal angles) of each
  site's leading modes with the leading modes of the GGN-cos and NTK-cos sample kernels at the same checkpoint
  (`data/kernels/compare_ggn.json`, r = 4 and 10, rule 0). The loss-gradient-cos reference in the same file uses
  labels and goes in a separate, labelled panel. The line r/N is a **scale reference** (expected overlap of random
  spans), not a control.
- **Answer (facts).** At b9 (time block, all checkpoints), sets vs class union on the same assigned images:
  `q.grad.r{2,4}.b9.all.final.sets_diff` against `…class_union_assigned_diff` (ratio only if interpretable).
  GGN/NTK answer: new facts from `compare_ggn.json`.
- **Lead figure.** b9 over training: sets and class-union differences on the same images, set-permutation band and
  within-class band; local.
- **Further evidence (kept).** Site-mode overlap with GGN-cos/NTK-cos by site, final step, with the r/N scale line
  (label-free; it is the only check that does not build the label into the gradient); json.
- **To appendix (App 4).** `Q:q_grad.0_4_9_all.setalign`; every-site bars (titled "groupings from each site's time
  block, whole-model gradients"); loss-gradient-cos and dot-site panels.
- **Removed.** "units the learning signal acts on" and similar phrasing.

### X6 `x-coarse` — How much of each layer's walk can k groups keep, and does the spectrum prefer a k?
- **Why.** A partition into k groups is the simplest description of a layer. This asks how much of the layer's
  neighbourhood structure such a description keeps, compared with what is there in total and with what random
  groups keep, and whether the spectrum points to a number of groups.
- **Operationalization** (`SPEC_COARSE.md`). χ² mutual information of the coupling defined by the doubly stochastic
  operator. **Headline quantity: absolute retained information**, shown with the total I(X;Y) (or as a fraction of
  it) and the random-partition reference at the same k. Single snapshots: one-sided I(c(X);Y) (`I_start`; cells
  are symmetric so start = end). Chains: two-sided I(c(X);c'(Y)) (`I_two`), the coherence quantity; one-sided values
  secondary. Ceilings use **F_{k_eff}** (`keff_start`, `keff_end`; empty groups reduce k). The efficiency
  η = I/F_{k_eff} is a **tightness diagnostic** only (it is 1 for every partition on the lazy uniform walk) and
  goes in a secondary panel. In the coarse archive SEBA is scored at r ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16} (sparse markers, not a line); plot all of them. Classes and
  predictions are **validation markers** at their k_eff. Gap criterion (`coarse_summary.block_gap`): 1 − σ for
  single snapshots, σ_k/σ_{k+1} for chains, "clear" = largest ratio ≥ 1.15 and > 1.1 × runner-up.
- **Answer (facts).** At k = 10, final step: retained information of spectral k-means and of random partitions as
  fractions of the total, for single snapshots and for the chain b9→logits (new `x.coarse.*` facts from
  `coarse_summary.json`); clear gaps after 10 at the final checkpoint (rule 0) for b7→logits, b8→logits, b9→pool and b9→logits; b9→logits is the worked example, not the only case (compute the list from `coarse_summary.json`, never hard-code it).
- **Lead figure.** Retained information vs k (2..32): total line, F_{k_eff} ceiling, spectral k-means line, random
  line, SEBA and validation markers; panels: b9 single snapshot, chain b9→logits (two-sided), final step; json.
- **Further evidence (kept).** η panel (secondary, labelled tightness); gap position heatmap site × step with the
  criterion above (answers the second half of the question); json.
- **To appendix.** Leakage vs Ky Fan (`ncut`, `kyfan`) and the coupling statements → App 3; per-site tables → App 4.

### X7 `x-markov` — Is the constructed walk through the layers approximately Markov under the tested partitions?
- **Why.** If moves between a few groups from layer to layer depended only on the current group, depth would have a
  small, readable state machine. Testing that on the constructed walk says how far the tested per-layer groupings
  are from such a description.
- **Operationalization** (`SPEC_COARSE.md` §6; exact, from the operators). Exported values are **normalised
  fractions**: `defect_frac` = I(Z_s; C_{s+1} | C_s) / I(Z_s; C_{s+1}); `mem_frac` = I(C_{s+2}; C_s | C_{s+1}) /
  H(C_{s+2} | C_{s+1}); `ck` vs `ck_ind` = Chapman–Kolmogorov total variation vs distance from independence. Entries
  stored as −1 are **undefined** (zero denominator) and are plotted as missing, never as values. Partition
  sequences: each site's spectral k-means, one shared partition (b9's), PCA, random, classes (validation).
- **Control, stated correctly.** The lazy uniform walk with the same partitions **keeps** the "image stays put with
  probability ½" mechanism and removes the geometry of the non-self transitions. A similar defect on the control
  shows that the stay-put mechanism alone can produce a defect of that size; it does **not** decompose the real
  defect into parts. Only adjacent triples and pairwise transitions are tested.
- **Answer (facts).** Per-site partitions: defect fraction `c.mk.k10.spectral.real.defect` against
  `…uniform.defect` on the control; the shared partition reduces the measured errors (defect
  `c.mk.k10.shared.real.defect`, eight-site CK `c.mk.h8.shared.real.ck` vs `c.mk.h8.shared.ckind`). Wording: "reduces
  the measured errors", never "closed" or "lumpable" as a certificate.
- **Lead figure.** Defect fraction per adjacent pair, k = 10, final step: real vs uniform for own-site and shared
  partitions (classes as validation markers); missing entries gapped; json from `markov_summary.json`.
- **Further evidence (kept).** CK error vs horizon with distance from independence (shows how much long-range
  dependence the composed coarse model loses); json.
- **To appendix (App 4).** Memory vs k, memory over training, PCA/random sequences.
- **Also.** The X7 writer replaces the misstatement in current Findings §7 ("it is alignment, not geometry"; "on the
  uniform walk a shared partition is exactly closed, so these residuals are geometry" needs the same care) within
  the new fragment; the old findings file is retired, not patched.

### X8 `x-across-nets` — In the tested ResNets, do different seeds and depths go through matching sequences of operators?
- **Why.** If independently trained networks, or a 20-layer and a 56-layer one, pass the same images through
  matching sequences of operators, that sequence is a property of the task and recipe rather than of one run; where
  they diverge is where individual runs have freedom.
- **Operationalization.** Projector distance (and diffusion-kernel distance, no cut) between single-site operators
  on the shared probe; DTW monotone matching read against the block-permutation null, the constant-matrix reference,
  the two-seed floor, and the step-0 reference; inversion fraction (is monotonicity found or imposed). **The
  unresolved-cut share of the path sits next to every DTW cost** (it is large at k = 9, so k = 3 is reported too).
  Scope: ResNet-20/32/56, two seeds each, this probe, rules 0/1, eight checkpoints; different-seed pairings only in
  summaries. CKA stays a baseline.
- **Answer (facts only; current AL numbers are hand-typed and must become `al.*` facts or be dropped).** DTW cost
  across depth vs seed floor vs null at k = 3 and k = 9, final step, with unresolved share; first checkpoint below
  the null in both different-seed pairings.
- **Lead figure.** Live `al-c4` (DTW cost over training with null band, seed floors, and the unresolved-share panel).
- **Further evidence (kept).** Live `al-c1` at the final step (path, unconstrained matches, inversions): rules out
  "the match is imposed by DTW".
- **To appendix (App 4).** `al-c2` table, `al-c3`, `al-c5` modes and images, seed-0 vs seed-1 set agreement
  (`r20s0c` `agree_I3`, local).

### X9 `x-image-stats` — How does the association of the leading eigenfunctions with twelve pixel statistics change over training?
- **Why.** An untrained network already maps similar-looking images together, so some of what any layer groups by
  can come from simple image properties (brightness, colour, background). Tracking how strongly the leading
  eigenfunctions follow such properties, from step 0 to the end, shows where and when that association weakens.
- **Operationalization.** Per site and checkpoint: R² of an in-probe least-squares fit of ψ1..ψ8 on twelve
  standardised pixel statistics (a closed-form fit, not part of the construction), against its image-permutation
  null; |corr| per statistic with pointwise/familywise thresholds; within-class correlation (class means removed,
  within-class null), which class make-up alone cannot produce. **Caveat in the text:** these are linear, in-sample
  fits on twelve chosen statistics; a low value does not rule out nonlinear or other image information.
- **Answer (facts).** R² of ψ1 at step 0 (`q.img.step0.stem.r2_psi1`, `q.img.step0.b9.r2_psi1`) and at the final
  step, per site (new facts from `Q:q_imgstats.heat_r2_psi`).
- **Lead figure.** R² (ψ1, and max over k) vs training step, one line per site, null line; json.
- **Further evidence (kept).** Within-class max |corr| for luminance and mean blue over training (rules out "the
  association is only class make-up"); json from `Q:q_imgstats.heat_maxabs_psi_wc`.
- **To appendix.** The statistic × site heatmaps and other lines → App 4; agreement with the pixel and untrained
  sources (`agree_I1`, `agree_I2`) → App 2.
