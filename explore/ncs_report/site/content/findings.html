1. The last four blocks group images into kinds of thing

What we are looking at

DINOv2 ViT-S/14: the coherent sets ($r = 8$) of the layer block L9 to L12 (four snapshots), kNN graph, $k = 15$.

Claim (reading). The block L9 to L12 splits the {{fact:v.n}} images into eight sets of {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.sizes}} images ({{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.n_unassigned}} unassigned), and seven of the eight are each made mostly of one or two kinds of thing. I name them from their classes and the heatmap below: dogs and other mammals, snakes, spiders, crabs and insects, road vehicles, birds, food, buildings and monuments (with some clothing and devices), and devices, containers and clothing. The eighth mixes clothing, fungi and odd objects. The network was trained without labels and the sets were found without them.

How whole the classes are (observed). {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.n_ge16}} of the {{fact:v.n_fine}} classes have at least 16 of their 20 images in one set, and {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.n_whole}} have all 20. The heatmap gives the same picture by class group. Read down a column: {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.mix.one_set_groups}} each have at least {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.mix.one_set_min}} of their images in one set, while {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.mix.spread_groups}} spread over several (at most {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.mix.spread_max}} in any one).

At two sets (observed). Asked for $r = 2$, the same block splits mammals and birds from everything else: one set holds {{fact:v.L9_L12.r2.set2.dogs_share}} of the dog images and {{fact:v.L9_L12.r2.set2.birds_share}} of the birds, and {{fact:v.L9_L12.r2.set2.share_mammal_bird}} of its images are dogs, other mammals or birds. The other set holds the artefacts, the food and {{fact:v.L9_L12.r2.set1.invert_share}} of the invertebrates. {{fact:v.L9_L12.r2.reptiles_unassigned}} of the {{fact:v.L9_L12.r2.reptiles_n}} reptile images go to neither.

How I read it, and what else it could be
  • Why "kinds" and not "classes". Most sets hold several classes, and the classes inside one set share what they are (several dog breeds; snakes with spiders and crabs; obelisks with boathouses), not one look. The images in each set vary a lot in colour, pose and background.
  • Eight is my choice. The construction gives sets at any stored $r$; at $r = 2$ the split is mammals and birds against everything else, at $r = 8$ the kinds above. No $r$ is certified by a spectral gap (see Method), and that is not the standard I use here.
  • Where the probe could mislead. The display groups mix levels (dogs are {{fact:v.dog_classes}} of the {{fact:v.n_fine}} classes), so a set that is "dogs and other mammals" is also partly a statement about which classes ImageNet-100 sampled. The fine-class counts in the captions are the stronger evidence.

Try it. Open this block in the explorer, hover a set's row to isolate it, then move the block's start back to L1.

2. The leading eigenfunctions run between kinds

What we are looking at

DINOv2 ViT-S/14: the first eigenfunction $\psi_1$ of the layer block L9 to L12, coloured on every panel; the side panel shows the images at its two ends.

Claim (observed, then reading). The sets are a reading aid; the eigenfunctions are the object. In the block L9 to L12 the 12 images at each end of each leading eigenfunction are one or two kinds, a different kind at each end: $\psi_1$ has food and devices at one end and primates at the other, $\psi_2$ ambulances and insects, $\psi_3$ pasta and ambulances. {{fact:v.L9_L12.eta2_group.psi1_pc}} of the variance of $\psi_1$ lies between the ten class groups ($\eta^2$, the between-group share of variance).

The same, layer by layer (observed). Measured over the leading eight eigenfunctions of each single block, the share of variance between fine classes rises from {{fact:v.eta_fine.L1}} at L1 through {{fact:v.eta_fine.L6}} at L6 and {{fact:v.eta_fine.L9}} at L9 to {{fact:v.eta_fine.L12}} at L12 (chance {{fact:v.eta_fine.chance}}).

How I read it, and what else it could be
  • An eigenfunction's two ends are the images the block treats as most unlike along that direction. Each end here is one or two kinds, so at their ends the leading directions of the late block's geometry separate kinds rather than looks (colour, background). The middle of each eigenfunction is mixed; only the ends are this clean.
  • The eigenvalues are close together ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$ = {{fact:v.L9_L12.lam1}}, {{fact:v.L9_L12.lam2}}, {{fact:v.L9_L12.lam3}}), so an individual $\psi_k$ could rotate into its neighbours under a small change; the span is the more stable object.

Try it. Open $\psi_1$ of the block and step through $\psi_2..\psi_8$; then do the same for L1 alone.

3. With depth, classes stay together more and more

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 8$) of the single snapshots L1, L6 and L9 (the first three columns of the Overview figure), and the same count at every site.

Claim (observed). The number of classes with at least 16 of their 20 images in one set ($r = 8$, one snapshot at a time) rises from {{fact:v.L1.r8.n_ge16}} at L1 to {{fact:v.L6.r8.n_ge16}} at L6, {{fact:v.L9.r8.n_ge16}} at L9 and {{fact:v.L12.r8.n_ge16}} at L12; when set labels are shuffled it is at most {{fact:v.ge16.null_max}}. This counts fine classes staying together, whatever else shares their set: the largest set holds {{fact:v.L9.r8.largest_share}} of the images at L9 and {{fact:v.L12.r8.largest_share}} at L12. That the sets are kinds rests on the image grids above, not on this count.

What changes (reading). At L1 the sets follow the look of the picture: one set has {{fact:v.L1.r8.set1.top}}, mostly red things. At L6 they are coarse and part-semantic: one set holds {{fact:v.L6.r8.set1.top}} (natural textures in close-up), another the small dogs. At L9 they are close to the late-block kinds.

Try it. In the explorer, set the block to L1 alone, then L6, then L9, and watch the side panel's images.

4. Block 12 alone splits off single classes

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 8$) of the single snapshot L12, the CLS token after the last block.

Claim (observed, then reading). At L12 alone the top eigenvalues are {{fact:v.L12.lam1}}, {{fact:v.L12.lam2}} and {{fact:v.L12.lam3}}, against {{fact:v.L9.lam1}}, {{fact:v.L9.lam2}} and {{fact:v.L9.lam3}} at L9, and {{fact:v.L12.r8.n_single_class_sets}} of its eight sets are exactly one class each ({{fact:v.L12.r8.single_class_sets}}: all 20 images, nothing else). The set sizes are {{fact:v.L12.r8.sizes}}. I read this as a resolution effect of the probe, not a finer ontology: with 20 images per class and 15 neighbours each, a class whose images are each other's nearest neighbours becomes a nearly separate piece of the graph, and an eigenvalue near 1 picks it out. The last block's geometry is sharp enough for that; the block L9 to L12 averages it away.

What else it could be
  • The two singled-out classes are both birds; a class whose images look alike is the easiest to isolate. With more images per class, or a larger $k$, I would expect fewer single-class sets at L12 and the kinds of the late block to come back; that is a check on the list below.
  • The same snapshot keeps classes whole at every stored $r$ (classes with 16 or more of their images together: {{fact:v.L12.r2.n_ge16}} at $r = 2$, {{fact:v.L12.r4.n_ge16}} at $r = 4$, {{fact:v.L12.r8.n_ge16}} at $r = 8$), so the L12 geometry still keeps classes together; at $r = 8$ it picks out single classes where the block L9 to L12 shows kinds.

Try it. Open L12 alone and compare its spectrum in the side panel with the block L9 to L12.

5. Early layers group contexts by the token just read

What we are looking at

Mamba-130m: the coherent sets ($r = 8$) of the residual stream after layer 4, painted on the stored layouts of the residual blocks and the state.

Claim (observed). At layer 4 the walk links contexts that share the token at $t$. A step of the walk lands on a context with the same token far more often than chance: the excess share is {{fact:m.T_ex.L4}}, on a scale where 0 is chance and 1 is every step (the permutation band reaches {{fact:m.T_band_hi.max}} at most). The sets show the same thing, unevenly. {{fact:m.L4.r8.n_token_sets}} sets are one frequent token each ({{fact:m.L4.r8.token_sets_tokens}}; the commonest token covers {{fact:m.L4.r8.token_share_min}} to {{fact:m.L4.r8.token_share_max}} of each), but together they hold only {{fact:m.L4.r8.token_sets_size}} contexts ({{fact:m.L4.r8.token_sets_frac}}). Two broad sets of {{fact:m.L4.r8.broad_sizes}} contexts hold {{fact:m.L4.r8.broad_frac}}.

The extremes of its eigenfunctions isolate frequent tokens (observed). Counting the token at $t$ among the 12 contexts at each end (the ends only; the bulk of each eigenfunction is not examined here): one end of $\psi_1$ is {{fact:m.L4.psi1.pos.tok}}, the other {{fact:m.L4.psi1.neg.tok}}; the ends of $\psi_2$ are {{fact:m.L4.psi2.pos.tok}} and {{fact:m.L4.psi2.neg.tok}}.

How I read it. Early in the stack, linked contexts share the token just read, and even more its class (excess share {{fact:m.Cc_ex.L4}}), though they also share the kind of source ({{fact:m.W_ex.L4}}; the chart in finding 7). Only the most frequent tokens are common enough at this sample size to form a set of their own; everything else sits in the broad sets.

6. Across all layers, the sets mix token families, punctuation and layout

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 16$) of the residual stream averaged over all 24 layers (the block operator averages the 24 layers' walks).

Claim (observed, then reading). With the walks of all 24 layers averaged, the sixteen sets are mixtures of token families, punctuation and document layout, not one kind of distinction. Read by their most prominent members (the commonest tokens at $t$, then at $t+1$): three sets are mostly function words, led by determiners in one ({{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set12.tok_t}}), prepositions in another ({{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set15.tok_t}}, followed by {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set15.tok_next}}) and pronouns and to in a third ({{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set5.tok_t}}, followed by {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set5.tok_next}}); one holds digits; one subscripts and superscripts in formulas ({{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set8.tok_t}}, followed by digits); one LaTeX; one the line ends of poems ({{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set2.size}} contexts, {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set2.top_class_share}} of them newlines and {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set2.top_source_share}} from poetry, followed by {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set2.tok_next}}); others clause punctuation, sentence ends, code indentation and code symbols. Several are mixed: the commonest token of set 13 ({{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set13.tok_t}}) covers only {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set13.share_pc}} of it. One large set holds word pieces.

The eigenfunctions (observed). Each end of the leading eigenfunctions is mostly one token in one kind of source (token at $t$ and source, counted over the 12 contexts at each end): $\psi_1$ runs from {{fact:m.Bpoint.psi1.neg.tok}} ({{fact:m.Bpoint.psi1.neg.source}}) to {{fact:m.Bpoint.psi1.pos.tok}} ({{fact:m.Bpoint.psi1.pos.source}}); $\psi_2$ from {{fact:m.Bpoint.psi2.neg.tok}} ({{fact:m.Bpoint.psi2.neg.source}}) to {{fact:m.Bpoint.psi2.pos.tok}} ({{fact:m.Bpoint.psi2.pos.source}}). The large set of word pieces

(observed). Set {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.catch.set}} holds {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.catch.size}} of the {{fact:m.n}} contexts; {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.catch.word_share}} of them end in a word start or a word continuation, and it takes {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.catch.t90}} distinct tokens to cover 90% of it. 7. In late layers, linked contexts share the token less and have more similar next-token distributions

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 8$) of the residual stream after layer 24, and the walk's neighbour statistics at every layer.

Claim (observed, then reading). From layer 4 to layer 24 the walk's excess share of links between contexts with the same token at $t$ falls from {{fact:m.T_ex.L4}} to {{fact:m.T_ex.L24}}, and the excess share between contexts from the same kind of source rises from {{fact:m.W_ex.L4}} to {{fact:m.W_ex.L24}}. Linked contexts' next-token distributions become more similar beyond what sharing the token explains: $\tilde\kappa_{|T}$ goes from {{fact:m.kappaT.L4}} to {{fact:m.kappaT.L24}}. The late sets mix token families with the kind of source and with what follows: LaTeX commands followed by {{fact:m.L24.r8.set2.tok_next}}; function words in wiki and poetry; line breaks and symbols in code and forum posts.

How I read it, and what else it could be
  • A language model's last layer feeds the next-token prediction, so it is natural for its geometry to link contexts with similar predictions, whatever token they end in. The rising $\tilde\kappa_{|T}$ measures that on the walk's links; it does not say the network computes anything in particular.
  • The source share also rises, and next-token distributions differ by kind of source (LaTeX predicts LaTeX), so part of the predictive association could come through the kind of source. The whole-state finding below is the cleaner test of grouping by source.

8. Along reading, the residual block's set vectors lose most of their smoothness within two tokens; the state's keep most of it for sixteen

What we are looking at

The coherent sets ($r = 8$) of the whole recurrent state, painted on the six main layouts and on the two along-reading blocks; and the retention of sets as the same contexts are read further.

Claim (observed). Take the SEBA vectors ($r = 8$) of an object at the token $t$, and ask how smooth they still are on the walk of the same contexts after $\tau$ more tokens have been read. This measures particular vectors on later walks, not what information the network keeps. The all-layer residual block's vectors, on residual layer 16's walk, have mean retention {{fact:m.ret.Bpoint_on_L16.t0}} at $\tau = 0$ and {{fact:m.ret.Bpoint_on_L16.t2}} at $\tau = 2$ (chance {{fact:m.ret.chance}}): most of it is lost within two tokens, though not all, and {{fact:m.ret.Bpoint_on_L16.t16}} remains at $\tau = 16$. For the whole recurrent state's vectors on its own walk it is {{fact:m.lag.whole.t0}} at $\tau = 0$ and still {{fact:m.lag.whole.t16}} at $\tau = 16$, and the whole state's sets after 16 more tokens agree with its sets at $t$ at AMI {{fact:m.lagami8.whole.t16}}.

How I read it. The residual block's sets are organised around the token at $t$ (finding 6), and the residual geometry after a few more tokens is organised around different tokens, so those sets are no longer smooth there. The whole state's sets follow the kind of source (finding 9), which does not change as reading continues.

9. The recurrent state's sets follow the kind of source and differ from the untrained network's

Claim (observed). The whole state's eight sets follow the kind of source (AMI with source {{fact:m.whole.ami_W.r8}}): maths, code, wiki, forum and poetry, with a few splits (maths formulas apart from maths prose, code comments apart from code, poem line ends apart from the rest of poetry). The bag reference, which averages the trained model's input embeddings of the prefix's tokens, ignoring order and bypassing the recurrent layers, links contexts from the same source as strongly. But the state's walk also links contexts with the same token class and with similar next-token distributions, which the bag barely does; and the untrained network's whole state links contexts from the same source much less.

Two limits on this. The five kinds of source ({{fact:m.snippets_per_source}} snippets each) differ sharply in vocabulary and layout, so the result says the state separates these five, not how it would divide closer or more numerous kinds. And until the split-half check is run, I do not know which set boundaries would survive another sample of snippets; the coarse grouping by source, which the bag shows too, is the part I would expect to.

The whole state's leading subspace agrees with the untrained network's at {{fact:m.init.a8.whole}} and with the bag's at {{fact:m.cmp.whole.bag.a8}} ($a_8$, mean squared cosine of the principal angles between the spans of $\psi_1..\psi_8$).

The two earlier whole-snippet studies

Before token contexts, Mamba-130m and Pythia-160m were probed with {{fact:o.n_snippets}} whole snippets ({{fact:o.snippet_len}} tokens, {{fact:o.per_source}} from each of {{fact:o.n_sources}} kinds of source) as the items. Two results carry over.

Along reading, the sets follow the source; along depth, the class of the token (observed, Mamba). Blocks that average one layer over positions in the snippet gave sets that follow the source (AMI {{fact:o.v1.reading.source.range}} over the {{fact:o.v1.n_reading}} such blocks and $r = 2$ to 8). Blocks that average layers at one position gave sets that follow the class of the token at that position (AMI {{fact:o.v1.depth.cur_class.range}} for $r = 2$ to 8, against {{fact:o.v1.depth.source.range}} for source). That contrast is what motivated the token-context study above.

The sets come to follow the source early in training (observed, Pythia). The reading-block sets ($r = 4$) match the five sources at matched Jaccard {{fact:o.py.step0.min}} to {{fact:o.py.step0.max}} at initialisation, {{fact:o.py.step512.min}} to {{fact:o.py.step512.max}} after 512 steps, and {{fact:o.py.final.min}} to {{fact:o.py.final.max}} at the end of training.

The two models' top-level two-way cuts of the snippets (the root of each study's first zoom tree, each on one layer's reading block) put {{fact:o.rootcut.agree}} of the {{fact:o.rootcut.n}} snippets on matching sides. At whole-snippet grain, both models' sets follow the kind of source.

Checks I'd like to run

  • Split halves. Rebuild each object on two disjoint halves of the items and compare the leading eigenfunctions where the halves meet a common set of anchors. This is the main missing check for both networks (the ViT activations and the Mamba Gram matrices are on Modal volumes).
  • More token contexts. Several occurrences of each content word, to see whether the large set of word pieces gets structure (finding 6).
  • More images per class, or a larger $k$, at ViT L12. To confirm that the single-class sets are a resolution effect (finding 4).
  • A true zoom. Rerun the construction on one set's members, rather than reading them under another $r$ of the same object.
  • Token contexts through training. The Pythia result suggests the token-context structure would also be worth watching over checkpoints.
