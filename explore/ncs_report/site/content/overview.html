Which inputs does a network treat the same, and at which layer? The ResNet-20 report asked this of an image classifier with a label-free spectral construction I call Neural Coherent Sets. Here it runs on DINOv2 ViT-S/14, a vision transformer trained without labels ({{fact:v.n}} ImageNet images), and on Mamba-130m, a state-space language model ({{fact:m.n}} token contexts). In the ViT's last blocks the images group into kinds of thing. In Mamba's residual stream the contexts group by the token just read, and over all layers by families of tokens, punctuation and layout; its recurrent state groups them by kind of source (maths, code, wiki, forum or poetry).

The procedure is the ResNet report's, unchanged (step by step there): in each snapshot, link every item to its 15 nearest neighbours by cosine and turn the links into a random walk; average the walks over a block of snapshots; take the eigenfunctions of the average, the primary object; and read the leading ones as coherent sets with SEBA, as an aid to reading them.

One layer or many. A single layer's sets show what that layer treats alike. A block's sets show what its layers treat alike together: a function on the items counts as smooth only if it changes little, on average, along the links of all the layers' walks. So if layer 4 links two contexts because both end in “ the” but the later layers do not, the all-layer walk links them only weakly, and its sets keep the groupings that persist through depth.

The maths, in one place

With $\hat h_i$ the centred, unit-normalised activation of item $i$ in snapshot $c$, $C_{ij} = \langle \hat h_i, \hat h_j\rangle$. The kernel $K_c$ is the symmetrised kNN graph ($k = 15$) on $d_{ij} = 1 - C_{ij}$; Sinkhorn finds $v > 0$ with $P_c = \mathrm{diag}(v) K_c \mathrm{diag}(v)$ doubly stochastic, and $M_c = \tfrac12(I + P_c)$. A block $B$ has $\bar M = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{c\in B} M_c$, whose eigenvectors in decreasing order of eigenvalue minimise the block-average penalty $\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_c \tfrac12\sum_{ij}(P_c)_{ij}(f_i - f_j)^2$ among unit functions orthogonal to the constant and to each other. SEBA (Froyland, Rock and Sakellariou 2019) rotates $[\psi_0, \dots, \psi_{r-1}]$ and soft-thresholds at $0.99/\sqrt N$ until the columns are sparse; item $i$ goes to the column that is largest there if it is positive. What differs here from the ResNet run is in Method.

The short answer for each network

DINOv2 ViT-S/14

Early blocks group images by look; the last four group them by what they show. At L1 every set mixes unrelated classes; by L6 rough groupings appear. In the block L9 to L12, seven of the eight sets are each mostly one or two kinds, which I name from their classes: dogs and other mammals; snakes, spiders, crabs and insects; road vehicles; birds; food; buildings and monuments; devices, containers and clothing. {{fact:v.L9_L12.r8.n_ge16}} of the {{fact:v.n_fine}} classes have at least 16 of their 20 images in one set. The block's first eigenfunction has primates at one end and food and devices at the other, and {{fact:v.L9_L12.eta2_group.psi1_pc}} of its variance lies between the ten class groups. At L12 alone, single classes split off as sets of their own. Findings 1 to 4 · the eigenfunction

Mamba-130m

The residual stream groups contexts by the token just read and, over all layers, by families of tokens, punctuation and layout; the recurrent state groups them by kind of source. At layer 4 a step of the walk lands on a context with the same token at $t$ far more often than chance (excess share {{fact:m.T_ex.L4}}; 0 is chance, 1 is every step), though two broad sets hold {{fact:m.L4.r8.broad_frac}} of the contexts. With all 24 layers' walks averaged, one set is led by {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set12.tok_t}}, another by {{fact:m.Bpoint.r16.set15.tok_t}}; others hold digits, LaTeX, the line ends of poems, sentence ends or code indentation, and one large set holds word pieces. By layer 24 linked contexts share their token less ({{fact:m.T_ex.L24}}) and have more similar next-token distributions. Along reading, the residual block's set vectors lose most of their smoothness on the walk within two tokens (retention {{fact:m.ret.Bpoint_on_L16.t0}} → {{fact:m.ret.Bpoint_on_L16.t2}} on layer 16's walk: a property of these vectors on later walks, not of what the network remembers); the whole state's keep most of theirs for sixteen ({{fact:m.lag.whole.t0}} → {{fact:m.lag.whole.t16}}). Findings 5 to 9 · the eigenfunctions

Is the eigen-structure sound? As far as I have checked, yes: the leading eigenfunctions spread over many items (at least {{fact:v.L9_L12.pn_min}} images and {{fact:m.pn.Bpoint.min16}} contexts by participation number), Mamba's change little when the neighbour count goes from 15 to 8 or 30 and share almost nothing with the untrained network's.
