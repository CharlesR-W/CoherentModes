Layout

The kernel rule decides which probe images count as neighbours in each snapshot before the walk $P_c$ is built. With cosine similarity $C$ and distance $d = 1 - C$, a temperature rule uses $K_{ij} = e^{-\kappa d_{ij}}$; $\kappa$ is the inverse temperature. The answer depends on the rule, so it is exposed here rather than hidden.

  • kNN graph, k = 15 (default): link $i$ and $j$ if either is among the other's 15 nearest neighbours. Rank-based, no $\kappa$; the same neighbourhood size as the UMAP layouts.
  • per-point perplexity 100 / 300: each image gets its own $\kappa_i$ so that its neighbour distribution has perplexity 100 (or 300, coarser); the t-SNE affinity. Expect results close to the kNN graph.
  • kNN scale (flat reference): one $\kappa = 1/d_{15}$ per snapshot. A nearly flat kernel.
  • half-mass, calibrated: one $\kappa$ per snapshot chosen so the 15 nearest neighbours carry half the kernel mass.

The last two rules use one temperature per snapshot; in the ResNet they failed in opposite ways (too flat, too sharp) and are kept here for the same comparison. The side panel gives $\kappa$ and the median 15-NN kernel mass over the block's snapshots.

How to use the explorer

The grid. One row (the released DINOv2 ViT-S/14 weights; there are no training checkpoints), twelve columns: the CLS token after blocks 1 to 12, read out through the model's final LayerNorm ($L1$ to $L12$). Each panel shows the same 2,000 probe images, placed by a 2-d UMAP of that snapshot's activations (AlignedUMAP across sites). Positions are for display only; no number on this page is computed from them.

The block (outlined) is a run of consecutive sites (a layer block); one snapshot alone is a single-snapshot block. Its block operator $\bar M$ averages the lazy walks $M_c$ of its snapshots. The colouring comes from that block only and is painted on every panel, so you can follow the same images to the other layers.

The side panel describes the selection: the coherent sets (or the eigenfunction's two ends) with their images, the spectrum of $\bar M$, retention $\rho_c$ in every snapshot, the panel under the pointer enlarged, and per-snapshot numbers.

Extras. The class groups (10 groups of the 100 ImageNet classes; fine class in the tooltip) and the pixel statistics only colour and validate; none enters the kernel or the sets. Definitions: glossary.

How to use the explorer

The items are 8,000 token contexts: 8 positions $t$ in each of 1,000 text snippets (maths, code, wiki, forum, poetry). A context is the snippet's prefix up to and including the token at $t$; its activations are Mamba-130m's at that token.

Colour from picks one stored spectral object: a single layer of the residual stream or of the recurrent state, a block that averages the walks of several layers (all 24, or a third), the whole recurrent state, the same contexts read $\tau$ tokens further, or a reference (bag and recent: averages of the model's input embeddings of the tokens, over the whole prefix or its last 16 tokens, bypassing the recurrent layers; pred: the model's next-token distribution). Its sets or eigenfunction colour every panel; each panel keeps its own layout.

The panels are the stored 2-d layouts (UMAP of $\psi_1..\psi_{16}$ of the object named above each panel). Every panel shows the same 8,000 contexts; hover one to ring it everywhere and read it.

The side panel lists the sets: size, token-class make-up, the commonest tokens at $t$ and $t+1$, and the member with the largest SEBA value. Click a set to see 12 members, then how its members fall under another $r$ of the same object (click again to narrow). With an eigenfunction, it lists the contexts at its two ends. Token classes, sources and positions only colour and describe. Definitions: glossary.
